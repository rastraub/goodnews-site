## What it is

A shelter foster program places an animal in a temporary home. The stay might be a few nights or a few weeks. The animal may be a kitten too young to adopt, a dog healing from surgery, or a pet who is frightened by a noisy kennel. The shelter or rescue usually remains the owner. The foster caregiver provides the quiet, the routine, and the daily care.

[Best Friends Animal Society](https://bestfriends.org/foster) describes fostering as opening your home for a while so an animal can be somewhere calmer than a full shelter, which also frees a kennel for another animal. That is the job. It is not a trial adoption unless the group says it is, and it is not the same thing as training a service dog. A service dog learns tasks for one handler. A foster pet is waiting for an adopter.

This is also different from foster care for children. Those are separate systems, with courts and caseworkers. An animal foster application will not place a child in your home, and a child-welfare program will not send you a litter of kittens.

## How it works

You apply with a shelter, a rescue, or a national group that can point you to a local program. The [ASPCA's get-involved page](https://www.aspca.org/get-involved) is one front door to volunteer and foster roles. A local humane society is often the closer door. The group asks about your home, other pets, children, and how long you can help. Some caregivers take bottle-fed kittens. Some take an adult dog who already knows how to walk on a leash. Say which one you can actually do.

The group should send food, supplies, and a vet plan, or tell you exactly what they will reimburse. Your tasks are the ordinary ones: feed on their schedule, keep the animal safe, give only the medicine they prescribed, note changes in eating or energy, and bring the animal back for vaccines or an adoption event. You send photos when they ask. You do not promise an adopter a personality the animal has not shown.

When the stay ends, the animal returns to the group for adoption, or moves to another foster. Some caregivers later adopt the animal. That is allowed only if the group agrees. Falling in love is common. It is not a failure of the program, and it is not required.

## How to help

Ask a shelter near you whether they need homes, or start with a national explanation such as Best Friends and then apply locally. Donating the supplies a foster program lists, such as kitten formula or towels, helps the people who already said yes.

If you find a loose animal, call the shelter or animal control and follow their instructions. A photo on a neighborhood page can also help an owner. Taking the animal straight into your bedroom, with your own pets, skips the step that checks for a microchip and a vaccine record.

School programs sometimes foster as part of a class. Those still need a rescue's rules, an adult lead, and a plan for weekends and holidays, when the building is empty and the animal still needs care. A foster home is a home, not a mascot.
