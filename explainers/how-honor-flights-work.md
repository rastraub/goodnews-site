## What it is

An Honor Flight is a trip that takes veterans to visit memorials, most often in Washington, D.C. The veterans are the guests. They are not asked to pay for the trip. [Honor Flight Network](https://www.honorflight.org/about-us/) is a national nonprofit made of independent local groups called hubs. Its stated mission is to celebrate veterans by inviting them to share a day of honor at the nation's memorials.

The same about page says honored veterans always travel free of charge, paid for by donations. It also says the network began with World War II veterans and later included veterans of the Korean War and the Vietnam War. A hub can explain who it is flying now. Do not assume a neighbor qualifies, or does not, from a general article. The hub's application is the list that matters.

## How it works

Each hub plans its own trips. [Honor Flight Network's application page](https://www.honorflight.org/how-to-apply/) says each hub has its own sign-up process, and it points veterans to the hub near them. A typical trip is a full, scheduled day or a short tour: a flight or a bus, time at the memorials, and a return. Guardians sometimes travel with veterans who want or need a companion. Whether a guardian is part of a particular trip is the hub's rule, not a rule this site can set.

Volunteers meet the group, push wheelchairs when asked, hand out water, and stand in a welcome line when the flight comes home. Donors pay for the seats the veterans do not pay for. A company or a family sometimes sponsors one flight. The gift still runs through the hub or the national organization, so the trip stays on the books.

The emotional part is real, and it is not a show. People cry at memorials. A good volunteer makes the day easier and does not turn a veteran into content.

## How to help

If you are a veteran, or you are helping one apply, start at the [how to apply](https://www.honorflight.org/how-to-apply/) page and then the hub it points you to. Ask the hub what paperwork and what medical details it needs. Do not buy a plane ticket on your own and call it an Honor Flight. The point of the network is the shared day, the guardianship, and the welcome, not a solo weekend in the capital.

If you want to support the trips, give through [Honor Flight Network](https://www.honorflight.org/donate/) or through the hub that serves your area, and confirm you are on their official site before you pay. Hubs also need guardians and ground volunteers. They will say what training, if any, that job takes.

A classroom can write letters if a teacher confirms that a hub wants them. Unsolicited packages and surprise visits at a memorial are not help. The calm version is the useful one: an application, a donation to the real organization, or a volunteer shift they asked for.
