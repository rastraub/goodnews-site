## What it is

A Little Free Library is a small book-sharing box, usually on a post, where anyone can take a book or leave one. There is no card and no due date. The person who looks after it is called a steward. The box works because someone nearby treats it as a tiny branch library: dry, visible, and not empty for weeks.

The name Little Free Library belongs to a nonprofit. Its [start guide](https://littlefreelibrary.org/start/) walks through finding a spot, choosing a steward, getting a box, registering it, and telling the neighbors. You can also build an ordinary book box and never use the trademark. If you want the official name, the charter sign, and a listing on their map, you register with them. Their [build page](https://littlefreelibrary.org/start/build-a-little-free-library/) explains that the name is trademarked and that a registered box is the way to use it. The site, not this page, lists the current fee.

## How it works

People take a book, leave a book, or do one and not the other. The exchange does not have to be even. A steward checks that the door still shuts, that rain is not getting in, and that the shelf has something a child or a neighbor might want. Children's books, paperbacks, and anything that can survive a little weather tend to move fastest. A soggy encyclopedia helps no one.

Registration is separate from carpentry. A box you buy from the nonprofit's shop comes registered, their start guide says. A box you build yourself is registered by getting a charter sign. After that, the steward sets up an account and can add the box to the world map. Their guide says the box does not appear on the map until the steward marks it as mapped. Until then it can still sit on a corner and lend books. The map is how strangers find it.

A town, a landlord, or a homeowners' association may have its own rules about posts, sidewalks, and signs. Those rules are local. The nonprofit cannot waive them. Ask before you dig.

## How to start or help

The steps on this page follow the nonprofit's own order: a legal spot, a steward, a weatherproof box, registration if you use the name, and then the slower job of keeping books in it. If a box already exists on your block, you do not need a second one across the street. Ask the steward what they are short of. Clean children's books are the usual answer.

Schools and 4-H clubs often build the box as the project and then hand the steward role to a neighbor or a library, because a class year ends and a sidewalk box does not. Write down who checks it in July. A library that nobody looks after becomes a wet pile of paper, and the block remembers that.

If the project is really about getting books into a classroom or a pantry reading corner, a book drive may fit better than a new post in the ground. The box is one tool. The habit is sharing books without a due date.
