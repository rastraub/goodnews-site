## What it is

The [Audubon Christmas Bird Count](https://www.audubon.org/community-science/christmas-bird-count) is a winter bird census run by volunteers. It began in 1900, and Audubon calls it the world's longest-running bird count. Audubon reports more than 85,000 participants and more than 44 million birds counted in 2025, across more than 2,700 active counts in 21 countries.

It is community science. Ordinary people, from expert birders to first-timers, collect data that scientists use. Audubon says more than 300 peer-reviewed studies have used count data, including work on grassland bird declines and species shifting their ranges.

## How it works

The count is a collection of thousands of local counts. Each one happens inside a circle 15 miles (24 km) wide. The circle's leader, the compiler, picks one day between December 14 and January 5 and tries to keep the same timing each year.

Consistency is the point. Volunteers return to the same circles and use the same method year after year. That lets scientists tell a real change in a bird population from a random good or bad day. Each count records dates and times, how much effort observers put in, the weather, and every bird seen.

You do not need to be an expert. [Audubon](https://www.audubon.org/community-science/christmas-bird-count) says first-timers are often paired with experienced counters, and you only need to show up ready to notice. Some people walk a route with a field group. Others watch a backyard feeder inside the circle, if their count allows it. Others help compile the results.

## How to help

Start at Audubon's [join page](https://www.audubon.org/community-science/christmas-bird-count/join-christmas-bird-count) and look for a circle near you. Contact the leader early, since some circles fill their groups ahead of time. Ask what to bring and whether feeder counts are allowed.

For a family or a class, a good first count is a short one: an hour with an experienced group, binoculars if you have them, and a notebook. Afterward, look up the birds you could not name.

If you cannot count this year, you can still support the work by giving to the [National Audubon Society](https://www.audubon.org/donate) or a local bird club that runs a circle.
