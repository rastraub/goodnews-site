## What it is

A volunteer bed build is a scheduled day when people cut, sand, and assemble beds, then deliver them to children who do not have one. [Sleep in Heavenly Peace](https://shpbeds.org/about/) is the national nonprofit best known for this work. Its about page says volunteers build, assemble, and deliver bunk beds to children and families who need them, through chapters in local communities. Families apply to a chapter. Volunteers do not pick a house on their own.

Other towns run one-day builds with a church, a scout troop, or a furniture maker, using a similar shape: a plan, a shop, a list of families who asked, and a delivery. The national group is the clearest front door when you do not already know a local lead. A mattress on a curb with a handmade sign is not the same project.

## How it works

A chapter keeps a list of requests. On a build day, volunteers work from a pattern so the beds match and the hardware is complete. Some people cut lumber. Some sand. Some sew or bag bedding. A site lead keeps tools, fingers, and the schedule in order. At the end of the day, or on a separate delivery day, a small team takes the bed to the home, sets it up, and leaves.

The family asked for this. That is the ethical center of the visit. Volunteers are guests. They do not tour the home, comment on it, or film the child. The chapter, not a volunteer with a phone, decides what story, if any, is told afterward. Many chapters would rather show the shop than the bedroom.

Bedding is part of the gift and a common place people accidentally cause harm. A chapter may want new sheets, pillows, and quilts in certain sizes. A used mattress can bring bedbugs into a home that did not have them. Ask the chapter for the list. Then stick to the list.

[Their volunteer page](https://shpbeds.org/volunteer/) is where individuals sign up, and the [get involved page](https://shpbeds.org/get-involved/) is the wider set of ways to help, including starting a chapter if a community does not have one. Starting a chapter is a commitment with tools, storage, and a year of Saturdays. It is not a single service afternoon.

## How to help

If a chapter is near you, sign up for the job they are short of. If you have never used a saw, say so. Sanding and labeling parts are real work. Businesses sometimes sponsor the wood for a build. That gift should go to the chapter, with a receipt, not as a pile of lumber dropped at a school.

Students can help when the chapter invites them and an adult from the organization is in charge. A class bedding drive works the same way as a food drive: the recipient names the items, the school sets the date, and someone delivers the bags to the chapter, not to a random porch.

If you know a child who needs a bed, do not publish their name to raise money. Point the family to the chapter's application, or ask a school counselor to do it. The build is supposed to end with a place to sleep, not with the child as a fundraising story.
