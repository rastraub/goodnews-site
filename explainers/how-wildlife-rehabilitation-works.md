## What it is

Wildlife rehabilitation is, in the words of the field's [Minimum Standards](https://www1.maine.gov/ifw/docs/Standards-4th-Ed-2012-final.pdf), "the treatment and temporary care of injured, diseased, and displaced indigenous animals, and the subsequent release of healthy animals to appropriate habitats in the wild." The goal is not a pet or a zoo animal. It is a wild animal that can survive on its own again.

It is licensed work. The [National Wildlife Rehabilitators Association](https://www.nwrawildlife.org/page/Do_I_Need_a_License) explains that U.S. rehabilitators must hold permits from state and federal governments. The U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service covers migratory birds and federally threatened and endangered species. Each state agency covers its other mammals, birds, reptiles, and amphibians. Volunteers can often help under a licensed rehabilitator or facility.

## How it works

Most rescues start with a member of the public who finds an animal and makes a call. [Animal Help Now](https://ahnow.org/about.php), a free website and app, has connected people to help since 2011 and went nationwide in 2012. Its database lists more than 3,700 helpers, including wildlife rehabilitators, rescues, hotlines, and veterinarians who treat wildlife.

The first question is whether the animal needs help at all. [Wyoming Game and Fish](https://wgfd.wyo.gov/Ask-Game-and-Fish/Heather%2C-what-do-I-do-when-I-find-injured-or-ldquo) notes that deer, cottontails, birds, and other species often leave their young alone for much of the day to hide them from predators. Young owls that outgrow the nest often end up on the ground while their parents keep feeding them.

When an animal does need care, the rehabilitator treats injuries, feeds it the right diet, and gives it space to heal and, for young animals, to grow up wild. [Texas Parks and Wildlife](https://tpwd.texas.gov/huntwild/wild/rehab/) reminds callers that rehabilitators are permitted only for the species named on their permits, often have other jobs, and may need time to call back. Patience helps.

## How to help

Save [Animal Help Now](https://ahnow.org/injured-wildlife.php) on your phone before you need it. Ask your local wildlife center whether it needs volunteers, transport drivers, or supplies, and help prevent injuries in the first place: Animal Help Now points to everyday threats like window strikes, vehicle strikes, and attacks by cats and dogs.
