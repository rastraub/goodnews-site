## What it is

A volunteer fire department is a fire department staffed all or mostly by people who are not full-time, paid firefighters. They are neighbors who hold other jobs and answer emergency calls when they are paged. According to the [National Volunteer Fire Council's fact sheet](https://www.nvfc.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/volunteer-fire-service-fact-sheet.pdf), volunteers make up 62% of firefighters in the United States, about 635,100 of roughly 1,018,100.

Most departments are volunteer, too. The [NFPA's U.S. Fire Department Profile](https://www.nfpa.org/education-and-research/research/nfpa-research/fire-statistical-reports/us-fire-department-profile) counted 29,452 fire departments in its 2020 survey. Of those, 18,873 were all volunteer and 5,335 were mostly volunteer. Only 18% were all-career or mostly-career, though those larger departments protect most of the population.

## How it works

Volunteer departments are the backbone of small-town and rural fire protection. NFPA found that 95% of volunteer firefighters serve in departments protecting fewer than 25,000 people, and about half are with small, rural departments protecting fewer than 2,500 people. Many volunteers stay for a long time: 43% had more than 10 years of active service.

The work is not only fires. The [NVFC](https://www.nvfc.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/volunteer-fire-service-fact-sheet.pdf) says volunteers respond to medical emergencies, vehicle crashes, natural disasters, hazardous materials, water rescues, and general public service calls, and that they spend a great deal of time training to be ready. When a call comes in, members are alerted, meet at the station or the scene, and respond as a crew.

Volunteers also save their communities money. The NVFC fact sheet cites an estimate that time donated by volunteer firefighters saves localities about $46.9 billion a year, and notes that for many communities a fully paid department is not feasible.

The [National Volunteer Fire Council](https://www.nvfc.org/about/), founded in 1976, represents volunteer fire, EMS, and rescue services nationally and helps departments recruit, train, and support their members.

## How to help

If you want to serve, the NVFC's [Make Me A Firefighter](https://www.makemeafirefighter.org/) site lists local volunteer fire and EMS opportunities, and you can sign up there to hear about new ones near you or contact your state fire association. Each department sets its own requirements and training.

You do not have to fight fires to help. Ask your local department what it needs, follow its fire-safety advice at home, and say thank you, especially around the holidays.
