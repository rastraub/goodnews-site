BBC News reported on November 15, 2026, that the gardens at Burghley House won this year's Historic Houses Garden of the Year award on a public vote. The Elizabethan house sits on the Cambridgeshire-Lincolnshire border, between Stamford and Peterborough, inside parkland laid out by Capability Brown. Head gardener Joe Whitehead, who started there as an under-gardener 20 years ago and returned to lead the garden in 2017, has spent eight years on an organic programme with seven staff and 20 volunteers. They have planted more than 100,000 bulbs and plants and put in a network of hard paths. Whitehead told the BBC the paths turned a lawn you crossed at random into a garden that leads you somewhere.

The award citation described a sculpture garden set in borders and wildflowers, and a Garden of Surprises rebuilt from 16th-century notes, with water, sound, and movement. Ursula Cholmeley, chair of the Historic Houses gardens committee, said the team had kept a centuries-old garden feeling current. The gardens are open to visitors from February through November. The prize is the vote and the work already in the ground. It does not rank every other garden in Britain.
