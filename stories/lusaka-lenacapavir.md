The first boxes of a generic, twice-yearly injection to prevent HIV have reached Zambia. BBC News reported on November 26, 2026, citing the Global Fund, that the shipment arrived on Wednesday. Uganda is expected to receive a delivery in the coming days, and a Global Fund shipment for Zimbabwe is planned for early 2027. The reports named the country. They did not name the town where the boxes were unloaded.

Lenacapavir is given as pre-exposure prophylaxis twice a year. The Global Fund said it is nearly 100 percent effective at preventing HIV infection, and that the generic version is expected to cost about $40 a person each year. The original drug, from Gilead Sciences, cost about $28,000 a year in the United States. In a 2024 trial of more than 5,000 adolescent girls and young women in Uganda and South Africa, none of those who received the injection contracted HIV. That result is from the trial. It is not a promise that every later user will be protected, or that follow-up is finished. The World Health Organization recommended the drug for prevention in July 2025.

The Global Fund said more than 189,000 people in 10 countries had already received a first dose of lenacapavir by November 2026, before this generic supply. It expects 24 countries to have received some supply by the end of the year, and it has a goal, with the United States government, of reaching at least 3 million people by the end of 2028. That number is a target. The news this week is that a lower-priced generic version has started to arrive.
