More than 300 Indigenous technology leaders spent two days in Wellington at a summit built around the people who will do the work next. RNZ reported on November 27, 2026, that Te Hapori Matihiko, the community for Māori in digital work, hosted New Zealand's first Indigenous Tech Summit at Tākina on Thursday and Friday. Co-founder Lee Timutimu said the programme sat on five pillars, and that the most important was the future and mentoring the next generation. A rangatahi advisory group helped design parts of the meeting.

Chief executive Katie Brown said the group has run the Matihiko Awards for three years and wanted a place where the people being honored could teach. She pointed to work already underway, including Te Hiku Media's tool for transcribing te reo Māori and the data storage network used by Te Kāhui Raraunga. The summit is to be held every second year, alternating with the awards. One visitor, Tracey Savelio of Tokelau, co-founder of Sovata AI, told RNZ it felt validating to compare notes with Indigenous peers. The news is a meeting that happened, with a repeat planned. It is not a new school, and it does not say how many young people were in the room.
