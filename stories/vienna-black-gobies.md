Black gobies in the northern Adriatic Sea grow faster and reach larger sizes than the same species did over the past 8,000 years, Phys.org reported on November 19, 2026. Paleontologists at the University of Vienna compared fossil ear stones, called otoliths, from sediment cores with otoliths from gobies living there now. The work is published in Proceedings of the Royal Society B. Otoliths add rings as a fish grows, a little like a tree.

The paper's measurements put the median length of modern fish at 76.8 millimetres, against 46.7 millimetres in the Middle Holocene and 54.2 in the Late Holocene. Modern fish were also about 65 percent larger than gobies from the Holocene Climatic Optimum, a warmer stretch roughly 8,000 to 5,500 years ago. First author Isabella Leonhard said warmer water is only a small part of the story. Larger fish have declined under environmental change and fishing, and a small generalist may be facing fewer predators, less competition, or more food.

Department head Martin Zuschin said inconspicuous species show how a food web is changing, not only the fish that end up on a plate. The university's release says the cause of the recent growth cannot yet be pinned down, and it is unclear whether the trend will last. The finding is a long baseline for one non-commercial fish. It is not a claim that the Adriatic is getting healthier overall.
