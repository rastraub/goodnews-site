The Ghana News Agency reported on November 16, 2026, that the Center for Climate Change and Gender Studies at the University of Energy and Natural Resources in Fiapre, Bono, launched a book titled "Smallholder Tree-Crop Farming in Ghana and South Africa." Professor Mercy Afua Adutwumwaa Derkyi, head of the center, co-edited it. The book draws on an inclusive value-chain project from 2014 to 2019 and argues for a farmer-centered reading of how cocoa and oil palm are grown. Co-authors include Professor Daniel Obeng-Ofori and Dr. Martha Ataa Asantewaa.

Lead editor Professor Miriam Ros-Tonen, of a collaboration between the University of Amsterdam and partners in Ghana and South Africa, said farmers in both countries depend on perennial crops for income, food, and longer-term earnings, even though the crops differ. She asked Ghana Cocoa Board, the Ministry of Food and Agriculture, and the Forestry Commission to share knowledge and technology with those farmers. Co-author Dr. Kwabena Asubonteng also noted the government's tree-planting work. The news is the book and the record of farmer experience inside it. It is not a new yield figure.
