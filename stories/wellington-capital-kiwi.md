Wild kiwi are roaming the hills around Wellington for the first time in more than 150 years, Mongabay reported on November 24, 2026. The Capital Kiwi project, led by Māori iwi with neighbors and private landowners, has translocated more than 250 birds since late 2022. Counting chicks hatched in the wild, founder Paul Ward estimates the local population now exceeds 300.

Stoats, brought to New Zealand in the late 1800s, kill nearly every kiwi chick in places without predator control. Before the releases, teams put out more than 5,300 traps across about 24,000 hectares of farmland, forestry, and suburban reserves. Ward said chick survival under that trapping has passed 90 percent, and that the release sites have enough food for adult birds that can fend for themselves.

Rawiri Walsh, the project's kiwi guardian and iwi liaison from Taranaki Whānui, said monitoring shows the released birds are in good condition and laying eggs. The moves follow a tono, a formal request to gift the birds, and receiving communities welcome them with a pōwhiri. Walsh said placing a kiwi in a burrow is a way of putting something back into the land. Ward said the same partnerships are now being used to ask which other treasured species might return. The news is this population and the chick survival where traps are in place. It is not a count of every hill around the city.
