Twelve riders, none older than 15, galloped decorated horses up the approach to Kamo Shrine in Kikuma, Imabari, on October 18. Ehime Shimbun reported that day that the otoma no hashirikomi, a prayer for the harvest and for households, sent the children the length of a path of about 300 metres, including the last uphill stretch, while spectators applauded.

The paper said the ride is counted as more than 600 years old and is designated an intangible folk cultural property of Ehime Prefecture. Horse owners in the Aiba-kai and the nonprofit Kikuba-kai, which works to keep the practice going, organize it. The city's own listing puts a neighborhood parade on October 17, leaving the shrine at 10:30 a.m., and the running itself on the morning of October 18, the third Sunday of October.

The riders wore festival costume and took the horses at a run. The report is about that morning's twelve riders. It is not a roster of every child in Kikuma who can ride.
