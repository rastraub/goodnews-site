Two adult African rock pythons rescued from an electricity transformer in Kumasi are doing well at the city zoo, and six hatchlings from their eggs have been released, the Ghana News Agency reported on November 18, 2026. The rescue was in April, at Ahodwo-Daban. Technicians from the Electricity Company of Ghana were there for a fault. A distress call brought keepers from Kumasi Zoological Gardens, who caught both snakes and 42 viable eggs in about three hours. The eggs had been in the transformer. Dr Meyer Ziekah Yiryele, the zoo's resident veterinarian, said one snake was male and one female, and that the timing matched the species' breeding season.

The eggs went into an incubator. In May, six hatched in a makeshift incubator at the zoo, ten eggs spoiled, and the rest were still being watched. Yiryele said then that the young would go back outside once they were ready, because the snakes help keep a balance with the animals they eat. On a visit in late October he told the agency the adults had settled in their enclosure. He also said the hatchlings had since been released in more than one place. The November report is that follow-up: two adults at the zoo, six young released, ten eggs lost. It does not say how many of the remaining eggs hatched after May, or where each youngster was set down.
