Fresh beaver feeding signs have been recorded along Semington Brook at Hurst Farm, near Worton. BBC News published the Local Democracy Reporting Service account about 19 hours before this story. Beavers vanished from Britain centuries ago. The signs are another mark that they are settling in Wiltshire.

The first official confirmation of a free-living population on this brook, a tributary of the Bristol Avon, came last year. Since then, evidence such as gnawed trees has grown. Wiltshire Wildlife Trust says free-living beavers are in the Bristol Avon and in parts of the Hampshire Avon. In April, a beaver was filmed swimming near Bradford on Avon.

Gabriel Connor-Streich, of consultants Greenshank Environmental, said the opening is to improve conditions so the animals can establish and spread. Housing developers can pay into a biodiversity scheme agreed with the landowner. Those payments are meant to fund 30 years of habitat work, including about a mile of Semington Brook and its side streams so water can move more naturally, plus grassland, hedges, and an existing lake. Connor-Streich said the payments would support restoration where beavers are already feeding and where water voles have been recorded nearby. The feeding signs are what has been seen. The 30-year work is a funding plan, not a finished project.
