A Japanese night heron was confirmed in Singapore for the first time, and the sighting is also the first for mainland Southeast Asia. The Straits Times reported on November 11, 2026, that birder Richard Clayton photographed the bird on the morning of November 10 along Jalan Mat Jambol in Pasir Panjang. Yong Ding Li, regional head of flyways and species conservation at BirdLife International, saw the photo, went to the street, and found the bird around 9:35 a.m.

Watchers first thought it might be a Malayan night heron, a regular seasonal visitor. Yong said the plumage lacked the white streaks, barring, and dark crest he would expect on a Malayan night heron of a similar age. He checked with experts in Malaysia, Hong Kong, and Japan. The Japanese night heron is globally threatened, with a wild population estimated at only several thousand. It breeds mainly on Japan's main islands and on South Korea's Jeju Island, then flies thousands of kilometres to winter in the Philippines and Borneo. Yong said a recent typhoon in southern China could have blown this migrant off that path. The bird needs dense, damp forest. He said its stay may be short if it cannot find that cover. The news is one confirmed visit. It is not a new breeding colony.
