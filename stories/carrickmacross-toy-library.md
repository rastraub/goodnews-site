More than 500 children in Louth, Cavan, and Monaghan have sat through a workshop on where a toy comes from and where it goes when the playing stops. RTÉ reported on November 27, 2026, that the sessions are run by the Carrickmacross Toy Library. Outreach officer Laura McCormack told the station the point is that toys do not appear on a shop shelf by magic, and that a book library already feels ordinary, so a toy library should too. A third-class group at Bunscoil Lughaidh Naofa in Carrickmacross looked at what different toys are made of and which of them can be recycled. Each class makes a play pledge, a promise to leave more room for play in the school week.

The library is a charity that opened six years ago. It now has more than 1,200 toys and 500 member families, who borrow for a small annual or quarterly fee. Chief executive and co-founder Jill Kennon said the line they use is "buy less, play more," and that as far as the library knows it is the largest stand-alone toy library in Ireland. The first 20 workshops were a pilot. The outreach job was funded by the Toy Show Appeal. The news is a finished round of school visits and a lending collection that is already open. It is not a promise that every school in the three counties has had the workshop.
