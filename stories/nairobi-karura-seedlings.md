More than 4,000 indigenous tree and shrub seedlings went into the ground along Wangari's Track in Karura Forest. The Kenya Forest Service published the account on November 26, 2026. The planters were the service itself, Friends of Karura Forest, KFEET, the Athi Water Works Development Authority, and Zhongmei Engineering Group.

The service said the point of using indigenous species is to rebuild habitat and to help the forest support the plants and animals that belong there. It also tied the planting to soil, water, and the other work a city forest does. Karura sits inside Nairobi. The note did not say how many people turned out, and it did not claim the seedlings have already survived a dry season. The figure is the number put in the ground that day.

Friends of Karura Forest has spent years replacing plantation trees that do not belong in the forest. This day's work is one more stretch of that job, along the path the service called Wangari's Track. It is not a new total for the whole forest.
