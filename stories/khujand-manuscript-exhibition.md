Copies of 18 manuscripts of classical Persian and Tajik literature went on display at the Khujand Fortress Museum, The Times of Central Asia reported on November 19, 2026. The originals remain at the British Library. The exhibition runs through January 2027. It includes work connected with Rudaki, a court poet for the Samanid rulers in Bukhara who died in the middle of the 10th century; Firdawsi, author of the Shahnameh, the Book of Kings; Omar Khayyam, known for poems and also for algebra and calendar reform; and Kamoli Khujandi, a 14th-century poet born in Khujand. The British embassy said the books, kept in London, mark a shared heritage. Anvar Yaqubi, deputy governor of Sughd province, was at the opening with British ambassador Katherine Smitton.

The British Library says its Persian manuscripts number more than 11,000, from Central Asia, India, and elsewhere, and that many are the only known copy. The Khujand museum is officially the Historical Museum of Sughd. The fortress on the Silk Road is more than 2,500 years old in the chronicles, and a larger domed museum building opened in 2024. The news is these 18 copies and the months they will be up. The original manuscripts did not travel.
