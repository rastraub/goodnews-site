The Gayasan National Park Nature Center in Seongju, North Gyeongsang, is running a fall listening program called Forest Sound Demon Hunters. The Korea Herald reported on October 3, 2026, that the session starts with recordings of forest birds, then a mission at four spots along an 800-meter trail. It ends back in the hall, where visitors lie still with the windows open and try to pick out cricket, bird, and cicada sounds. Nature interpreter Shin Mi-yeon described fireflies that come out in the evening. The Herald also described Mongolian oaks and the acorn weevils that use them.

The park's flagship species include the leopard cat and the snow primrose. The Seongju center is one of 10 nature centers run by the Korea National Park Service. The article is a guide to a program visitors can join this fall. It does not report how many people have taken part, and it does not measure whether the listening changes what they notice later.
