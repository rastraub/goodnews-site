Australians had already logged more than 3.7 million birds in the 2026 Aussie Bird Count when the Guardian reported on it on October 24. Sean Dooley of BirdLife Australia said the tally was on pace to beat 2025, when about 64,000 people counted more than 5 million birds. Last year's most common records were the Australian magpie, the rainbow lorikeet, and the noisy miner. Anyone can join by watching for 20 minutes and entering the birds in the project app. The count ran through midnight on Sunday, October 25.

One of those watches happened at Cedars Christian College in Wollongong, where teacher Karen Jooste used the count with a year-two class. The school looks onto a creek. Students recorded tawny frogmouths with two chicks, magpies, magpie-larks, wood ducks, Pacific black ducks, king parrots, rainbow lorikeets, and a bowerbird. The exercise doubled as practice in careful looking, writing, and the idea that a local patch of habitat is worth a number. The national total was still climbing when the article was published.
