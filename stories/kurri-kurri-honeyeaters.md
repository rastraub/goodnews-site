Forty-six zoo-bred regent honeyeaters were released into woodland near Kurri Kurri. The Newcastle Herald reported the release on October 18, 2026. The species is critically endangered. About 300 birds are thought to remain in the wild. They once ranged from around Adelaide to north of Brisbane. Breeding is now probably limited to New South Wales, centered on the greater Blue Mountains.

This is the fifth year of releases on Wonnarua Country. Tara Dever, chief executive of the Mindaribba Local Aboriginal Land Council, welcomed the birds home and said they are living in the wild with no roof over them. Before they left, the honeyeaters were tutored with recordings of wild song and kept in aviaries with other species so the woodland would not be a first encounter. Mick Roderick, a recovery adviser at BirdLife Australia, said the site was chosen for its trees and its food. BirdLife will keep watching. Anyone who sees a banded bird is asked to report it. The release is a partnership of the Mindaribba council, the New South Wales environment department, BirdLife Australia, and Taronga.
