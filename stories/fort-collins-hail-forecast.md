Colorado State University researchers, working with NVIDIA on a project backed by the National Science Foundation, have built artificial-intelligence models that look for hail sooner than a typical warning, the university reported. Hail-StormScope already offers about two to three hours of lead time. Many hail warnings today arrive with 30 minutes or less. A second model, CSU-MLP, looks further out, from one to eight days ahead.

The extra hours are the useful part. Hail can dent roofs and cars, and it can ruin a crop, before a short-notice warning gives anyone time to act. The university describes tools that extend the watch. It does not claim every storm will be called perfectly. Forecasters still decide what reaches the public.
