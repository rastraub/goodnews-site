Pražský deník reported on November 12, 2026, that Prague Zoo is keeping honey badgers again. A young female born last year at Olomouc Zoo is settling into the Africa Up Close pavilion and the outdoor enclosure in Troja. Mammal curator Pavel Brandl said several European zoos raised young last year, which is unusual, and that this female is unrelated to lines descended from Prague's animals of a quarter-century ago. After a month in quarantine she learned to enter a transport box and shut herself inside it. In the exhibit she unscrewed screws the keepers had not known were there.

Prague has kept the species since 2001 and recorded the first natural rearing of honey badgers in Europe. The previous pair were very old, and the female died last year. If things go well, a male from Safari Park Dvůr Králové is due next year. He would be a grandson of Prague's first honey badgers. The zoo says the animals are known for loose, tough skin and for standing up to much larger threats. The news this week is one young female on view, not a cub.
