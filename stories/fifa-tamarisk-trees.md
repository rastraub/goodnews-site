The Jordan News Agency, Petra, reported on November 4, 2026, that the Arab Potash Company and the Royal Society for the Conservation of Nature launched a tamarisk forest project at Fifa Nature Reserve, in the southern Dead Sea region. The inauguration at the reserve headquarters included a ceremonial tree planting and a briefing on the work. APC is funding it. RSCN is carrying it out.

Petra said Fifa is the world's lowest-elevation wetland on the Ramsar list and a stop for birds migrating between Africa and Europe. About 40 bird species have been recorded there, most of them nesting. The project is aimed at habitat for wildlife that includes the Dead Sea sparrow and the Nubian nightjar. Planned work includes surveys of where tamarisks stand, how healthy they are, and whether they are regenerating; a study of livestock grazing so herders have guidelines; more patrols with the Ministry of Agriculture and the Royal Administration for Environmental Protection; and awareness sessions for students and neighbours.

APC chair Shehadah Abu Hdaib and chief executive Maen Nsour, and RSCN board chair Batool Ajlouni and director general Fadi Naser, spoke at the launch about the partnership. The news is the opening and the list of tasks. Petra did not report how many trees were planted beyond the ceremony, or any new bird count.
