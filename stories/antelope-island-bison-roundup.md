Hundreds of riders spent Saturday on horseback at Antelope Island State Park, moving the bison herd into corrals, KSL reported. Danny West called it a good day in the saddle. For volunteer John Parke, the roundup is also a tradition he passes to his kids.

Utah State Parks spokesperson Ally O'Rullian said the gather is how the park looks after the herd and the island. Once the animals are in, staff can spot medical problems, give vaccines, and check cows for pregnancies. The island can support roughly 500 to 550 bison. Calves arrive each year, and an auction after the roundup keeps the herd inside what the habitat can hold. A little more than 250 were sold last year. The public can watch the next stage, the Working of the Bison, on free tours from October 27 through October 29. The auction is set for 10 a.m. on Tuesday, November 3.
