Thirty-three people from 15 Brazilian states and the Federal District are traveling the Paraguay River from Corumbá to Asunción as a floating classroom. Campo Grande News reported that the trip runs from November 9 to 15 and overlaps Pantanal Day on November 12. It is the final stage of a postgraduate course in strategies for nature conservation, run by the Instituto Homem Pantaneiro and the Federal Institute of Mato Grosso do Sul, with support from the state military police.

The wetland does not stop at a border. It spreads across Brazil, Bolivia, and Paraguay. Biologist Fábio Olmos, one of the lecturers, told the paper the river is a place to see real changes: a drier landscape, vegetation shifting toward the dry Chaco of the neighbours, and more fires. He said those changes are happening faster than older shifts in the Pantanal's history. Participants are also looking at how people use the banks. Freddy Ramírez of Paraguay's Fundación Moisés Bertoni, which manages three protected areas, is part of the exchange and argued for longer-term planning among the three countries. Ângelo Rabelo, president of the institute, said the river is the thread that ties the biome across borders, and that a fine is a late tool if protection was not planned. The group includes environmental military police and a teacher, Lidiane Valesca Severino, who said she will take the lessons back to her students. The course, created in 1992, has already trained more than 700 officers. The news is this voyage. It does not claim the fires or the drying have stopped.
