São Paulo Zoo presented Hope on November 24, a female Spix's macaw and the first of her species born at a Brazilian zoo. iG reported the presentation on November 25, 2026. She weighed 12.4 grams at hatching and is still under close watch in the zoo's conservation center. She is not in the public area.

Biologist Fernanda Guida, who leads the bird section, said Hope was badly positioned in the egg and had reached the limit of incubation, so the team helped her hatch. Guida said the name marks that help, and the hope of a better future for the species. Keeper Rafael Oliver described the first sound she made as the moment the long preparation came back to him. In 2025, two pairs laid 38 eggs and none hatched. Guida said the breeding season is not over, and that another egg from Hope's mother is still a hope rather than a plan.

The International Union for Conservation of Nature has listed the Spix's macaw as extinct in the wild since 2000. Brazil's official list calls it critically endangered. With Hope, the zoo's group is 28 birds, which iG said is about 8.5 percent of roughly 330 birds in human care worldwide. The work sits inside a national plan with the environment ministry and the Chico Mendes Institute. The zoo received 27 birds in August 2023 and, instead of holding them only for a short time, opened a conservation center in May 2024. iG said the center cost about 5 million reais, covers about 900 square meters, can hold up to 44 birds, and sits more than 200 meters from the rest of the park, with its own keeper. Since November 2024, two young males have been on view for visitors. The stated aim is a possible return to the wild later. This account is one hatching. It is not a release date.
