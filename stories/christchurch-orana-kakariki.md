An aged pair of critically endangered orange-fronted parakeets has produced two surprise clutches at Orana Wildlife Park in Christchurch, RNZ reported on November 24, 2026. Sophia and Prince Polo had been officially retired from breeding because of their age and moved to a display area. Native fauna manager Catherine Roughton said Sophia had first been paired with a bird called Poldark. After he died, she was paired with Prince Polo, and keepers found eggs.

About four weeks later, while the new chicks were about a month old, Sophia went back into the nest box and laid again. Prince Polo has been feeding the first four chicks. Roughton called that a full-on job for him.

The kākāriki karaka has twice been declared extinct. About 450 are now thought to remain, mostly on offshore islands or at the Brook Waimārama Sanctuary near Nelson, with a small remnant population in Canterbury. Department of Conservation recovery lead Wayne Beggs said Prince Polo and Sophia were collected as eggs from wild nests in the Poulter Valley about eight years ago and hold important genetics for the captive program and for wild places where their offspring are released. The news is this pair breeding again. It does not change the estimate of how many birds are left in the wild.
