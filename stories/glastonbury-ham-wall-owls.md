Ham Wall, the RSPB wetland near Glastonbury, had its strongest barn owl breeding season in about 20 years. BBC News reported on November 26, 2026, that warden Steve Couch counted 25 owlets from five pairs. He called the birds an iconic species that visitors love to see. The year before had been the poorest.

The Barn Owl Trust puts the breeding season between March and August. Couch tied the swing to voles, which make up about 45 percent of a barn owl's diet. In 2025, he said, winter floods and summer droughts had displaced those voles. This past season, rain at the right time flushed new grass, and the maintained wetland kept a food supply even in the heat. The grass is looked after on purpose, which feeds the small mammals and gives the owls cover.

Chris Sperring, conservation officer at the Hawk and Owl Trust, told the BBC that Ham Wall's success was not the whole of Somerset. Heat slowed grass growth elsewhere, and vole breeding with it, so other places saw fewer births. He said the lesson of the past two summers is how much managed wetland is worth. The news is 25 owlets at one reserve. It is not a national census.
