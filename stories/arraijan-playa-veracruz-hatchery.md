A hatchery for sea turtle nests is now open at Playa Veracruz, in Panamá Oeste. Infobae reported on October 9, 2026, that Fundación Mi Voz para Tus Ojos and Tierra Náutica built it with the Ministry of Environment and the National Aeronaval Service. Nests that are exposed to predators or other trouble on the beach can be moved there and watched through incubation.

The project grew out of several years of patrols, nest rescues, training, and beach education under the Tortugas de la City program. Marino Ábrego, deputy director of coasts and seas at the ministry, said conserving sea turtles is not a job one person or one office can do alone.

The same reports described a separate release: 127 olive ridley hatchlings, Lepidochelys olivacea, went into the sea at the Playa La Barqueta Agrícola wildlife refuge. That refuge is not Playa Veracruz. Rangers there watch nests and release hatchlings in the early morning. The new hatchery is a place to hold risky nests. Infobae did not report how many nests it has taken in.
