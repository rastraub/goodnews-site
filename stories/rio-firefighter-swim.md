Three Dublin firefighters pulled a 15-year-old girl from the sea off Rio de Janeiro on a rest day from the World Rescue Challenge. RTÉ reported the account on November 24, 2026, from an interview with sub-officer Stephen Bonfil. He was swimming about 30 meters from shore with firefighter-paramedics Paul Stanley and David Lorenzo when Stanley noticed the girl struggling. A person with her turned and signaled for help.

Bonfil said they put her in a rescue position for the swim back, and that she lost consciousness in a strong current. The three traded the hold as they came in, checking for sand under their feet and finding they were still in deep water. She was still unconscious on the beach. Stanley, who is also a paramedic, checked her. She was breathing, and she regained consciousness. Friends who had come over were upset. Bonfil called it a team effort using skills the three already train.

The same Dublin team later placed in sections of the competition, including a joint extrication challenge with Paraguay. Those results are separate from the swim. RTÉ did not name the girl, and it did not say whether she needed hospital care after she woke.
