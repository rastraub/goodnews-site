Thousands of Queen of Spain fritillaries reached Britain this year, and some of them have already produced a home-grown generation. The Guardian reported on October 29, 2026, that the golden butterfly, usually only a handful of sightings in a British summer, arrived from France and Spain along the south coast and from the Netherlands into East Anglia.

Those migrants bred. British-born offspring emerged this autumn. Last week, hundreds were on the wing at East Tilbury and Linford, beside the Thames in Essex. Neil Hulme, a butterfly expert, called the influx a bold attempt at colonisation and said the numbers were phenomenal.

He expects some of the butterflies to be seen again next spring. He was careful about what that means. The species has probably overwintered successfully in Britain only twice in 30 years, at Minsmere in Suffolk in the 1990s and near Chichester around 2010. A sighting next spring would be a foothold, with fresh arrivals from Europe, not proof of a permanent population.
