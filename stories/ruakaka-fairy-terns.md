The Department of Conservation said on November 4, 2026, that a young pair of tara iti, New Zealand fairy terns, has been fishing in the Ruakākā estuary and landing on a shell patch near the river mouth. The last confirmed breeding record at Ruakākā was in 1989. Programme lead Ayla Wiles said it is too early to know whether the pair will nest this season. One bird is a two-year-old female reared at Auckland Zoo. DOC said that, given the pair's age and inexperience, breeding this year is far from certain.

Over winter, DOC looked at possible new sites at Ruakākā, northern Waipū, and Ōmaha, and chose the north side of the Ruakākā river mouth. Hapū Patuharakeke helped build the shell nesting area. Fairy terns lay camouflaged eggs directly on the ground, and shell is the surface they use. Bream Bay Coastal Care added four automated rat and possum traps to its network. Volunteers from Waipū About Tern and Bream Bay Coastal Care have been watching the site, with seasonal rangers due as the season goes on.

Wiles said the wider population may reach an estimated 14 to 15 breeding pairs this season, the highest number in many years. Juliane Chetham of Patuharakeke Te Iwi Trust Board called the birds' return a sign for the health of the coast, while DOC's own wording is that the birds are exploring, not that a nest has been found. The news is the sightings, the shell patch, and the four new traps. It is not a confirmed nest at Ruakākā.
