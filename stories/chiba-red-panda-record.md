Futa, a red panda at Chiba Zoological Park, is set to be recognized by Guinness World Records as the oldest living red panda in captivity. The Japan Times reported the plan on November 13, 2026. The ceremony is on Tuesday, when Futa turns 23 years and 135 days old. That is roughly a third longer than the usual lifespan, which the paper put at 12 to 17 years. Red pandas are listed as endangered by the International Union for Conservation of Nature. The 30-minute ceremony is followed by a photo session. Seats go to the first 150 visitors after the zoo opens. People without a numbered ticket can watch from standing areas. The zoo will also hand out 20,000 limited cards from November 21, and merchandise goes on sale at the zoo from November 24.

The same title was last given in February 2020 to Taylor, a red panda at a zoo in Montana, at 21 years and 265 days. He died the following month. The oldest captive red panda on record is still Kusu, of Itozu no Mori Zoological Park in Kitakyushu, who died at 24 in 2015. The Japanese Association of Zoos and Aquariums counts 224 captive red pandas in Japan, six of them at Chiba. Red pandas are smaller than giant pandas and live in mountain forests, including parts of China and Myanmar. The news is a scheduled recognition of one animal's age. It is not a change in the number of red pandas in the wild, and the ceremony had not yet been held when the paper published.
