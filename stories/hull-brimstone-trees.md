BBC News reported on November 14, 2026, that Butterfly City, a wildlife group in Hull, is giving away alder buckthorn saplings so brimstone butterflies have somewhere to lay eggs. Director Sean Clough said 1,500 trees are free for residents, community groups, schools, and organisations. Alder buckthorn leaves are the only food of brimstone caterpillars, and the tree should not be confused with sea buckthorn. Clough said a female brimstone can detect a buckthorn from up to three miles away.

Daily collection points include East Park Animal Education Centre and Pickering Park Aviary, from 10:30 to 15:00, and EMS on Marfleet Lane on weekdays from 10:00 to 15:00. Rainbow Community Garden is open weekdays from 09:00 to 15:30. The Orchard Centre is taking collections on Tuesday, November 17, and Tuesday, November 24. Other spots listed by the station include Grain, Scrapstore, Plant and Paint, and Bransholme Chat, each with its own hours. The trees are available. A count of next year's butterflies is not in the report.
