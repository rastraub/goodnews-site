Families of smooth-coated otters are being seen along the Klang River in Kuala Lumpur, including on a sandy bank under the highways. Phys.org reported the sightings on November 23, 2026. The species is listed as vulnerable. The Klang was once classed as severely polluted, a Class 5 river. Years of work brought it to Class 3, moderately polluted. Ecologist Woo Chee Yoong said that change seems to be what drew the otters, and that otters mean fish, and fish mean a river system healthy enough for the neighborhoods around it.

Retired soldier Hasan Ujang watches them from a riverside apartment and compared their play to children swimming. Videos of otters sunbathing on concrete banks and swimming by elevated stations have spread online. Woo, who co-founded the Malaysian Otter Network after local reports in 2019, said nobody yet knows how many groups there are or how they move, or whether they returned or were simply overlooked. The network wants a way for residents to report sightings.

The same animals are less welcome at the Tropicana Golf and Country Resort, where residents say otters have raided fishponds at at least 12 houses in the past year. Yee Sze Mun, 89, said they killed arowana he had kept for 15 years. He called them a nuisance and also said he could not blame them for trying to eat. The residents' association said the visits began after forest next door was cleared. Security staff think about 25 otters use that forest. Woo, brought in to advise, tells people to block yard entries and net their ponds, because the otters are protected. Former mayor Maimunah Mohd Sharif called the river otters an asset and a benchmark for a cleaner city. She wants the Klang to reach Class 2B, clean enough for body contact. It is not there. Garbage traps and solar interceptors have already pulled tens of thousands of tonnes of waste from the water. The news is otters on a river that is less dirty than it was, and a real conflict with pond fish. It is not a census, and it is not a river safe to swim.
