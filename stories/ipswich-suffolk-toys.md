Libraries across Suffolk have taken in a record pile of Christmas toys after a two-year pause in the appeal. BBC News reported on November 25, 2026, that more than 3,800 toys, from dolls and stuffed animals to jigsaws and board games, have been handed in since the start of November. BBC Radio Suffolk runs the drive with Suffolk Community Libraries and Vertas. Charities will pass the toys to families. Volunteers have been sorting the boxes.

Breakfast presenter Wayne Bavin said he had not expected to beat the previous year. The first weeks brought a trickle of boxes, he said, and then the pile doubled and doubled again. On Tuesday, Mekala Hodgson, managing director of Advantage Angels, announced a £15,000 gift on air. She said £11,000 of it had already bought 700 toys, with £4,000 still to spend. Leah Carson, of the YMCA in Ipswich, who volunteered to organise donations, said it mattered that people were still giving what they could.

Gifts can be left until December 4 at 12 of the county's 45 libraries, including Ipswich, Bury St Edmunds, Lowestoft, Beccles, Felixstowe, and Stowmarket. The news is the 3,800 toys already in, plus the money Hodgson has put toward more. It is not a count of every child who will receive one.
