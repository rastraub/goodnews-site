A sunbittern chick at Wrocław Zoo is walking the South American aviary and already using the wing display adults use as a warning. The zoo said on November 27, 2026, that the chick hatched on October 15 after about thirty days of incubation, and that it is the first this pair has produced. It explores on its own and still depends on its parents for food. In the wild, sunbitterns hunt along wetlands and coasts in South America, taking fish and insects. When threatened they open their wings and lean forward so the eye-spots show. The zoo said the youngster has started doing that to other birds in the aviary.

The same note said two black-winged mynas have been on view since November 4. They arrived on September 15 from Plzeň Zoo in the Czech Republic. The zoo said the species is critically endangered, with an estimated decline of as much as 80 percent in a decade and about 200 birds left in the wild, pressured by trapping and by the loss of forest on Bali and Java. The news in the bird house is one chick that has hatched and two mynas that have settled in. It is not a release of either species.
