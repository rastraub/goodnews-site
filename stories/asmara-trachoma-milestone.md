The World Health Organization validated Eritrea's elimination of trachoma as a public health problem on November 16, 2026. The news release, datelined Asmara, Brazzaville, and Geneva, said Eritrea is the 11th country in WHO's African region and the 34th worldwide to meet that bar. Director-General Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus called it the result of two decades of work. Validation means an independent review group accepted the country's evidence that the agreed thresholds have been met and that a system exists to find and manage future cases. It does not mean the infection can never return.

Baseline surveys ran from 2006 to 2020. Eritrea then used WHO's SAFE strategy where it was needed: surgery for the inward-turning lashes called trichiasis, antibiotics to clear infection, facial cleanliness, and environmental improvements, especially water and sanitation. Health workers mapped communities with digital tools, including hard-to-reach places. All 58 sub-zobas, the second-level administrative areas, were surveyed, and interventions were scaled up wherever trachoma was endemic. Most of the antibiotics were oral azithromycin donated by Pfizer through the International Trachoma Initiative. A community-led sanitation approach delivered gains with limited outside money, WHO said.

WHO Representative Mary Stephen credited communities, the Ministry of Health, the Fred Hollows Foundation, and health workers. The country will keep looking for new trichiasis cases. Globally, the number of people needing interventions fell from an estimated 1.5 billion in 2002 to below 100 million by November 2025, about a 94 percent drop. As of March 2026, 92 million people still lived in places where trachoma blindness was a risk. Eritrea's certificate is one country's milestone inside that larger, unfinished map.
