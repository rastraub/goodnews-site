Volunteers marked ten years of keeping Newport Library open beyond the hours the county can staff, the Pembrokeshire Herald reported on November 18, 2026. The Newport Library Working Group, the first of its kind in Pembrokeshire, held a coffee morning at the library on Saturday, November 14. Chair Kim Everett said the group now has 24 volunteers. In the 2025/26 financial year they gave 722 hours at the desk, adding six opening hours a week to the five and a half hours of paid staff time. The county library service still supplies the books and the computers. Five people who started in 2016 are still behind the counter.

The library began in rented rooms on the main road. Volunteers raised money toward the rent with help from Newport Town Council. In January 2019 it reopened at the Canolfan Croeso Welcome Centre, which the town council lets the group use for a peppercorn rent. Classes from Ysgol Bro Ingli visit on Friday afternoons, often greeted by a volunteer. There is a weekly Welsh conversation class, and a former teacher holds reading sessions for children. Author readings, open gardens, and quiz nights have raised funds. Town council chair Ros McGarry said the school visits help children get used to using a library. The news is this anniversary and these hours. It is not a count of every library volunteer in Wales.
