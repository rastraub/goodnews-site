HapWare's ALEYE, a wearable that started as a Colorado School of Mines capstone, is on TIME's Best Inventions of 2026 list in the accessibility category. Smart glasses pick up visual cues, and a wristband turns them into distinct vibrations. The university says a wearer can feel a smile, a wave, or an offered handshake without having to see it.

Mines alum Jack Walters and Bryan Duarte, a haptic researcher who is blind, founded the company. Walters and other Mines students first built a wearable around Duarte's haptic technology in the senior design program. The device is meant for people who are blind or deaf, and for people with autism who have trouble reading nonverbal cues. It does not restore sight or hearing. It delivers a signal that sighted conversation usually leaves unspoken. Mines later invested $250,000 in the company through its venture fund.
