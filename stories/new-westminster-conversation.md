At You N Me' in New Westminster, a customer can order a drink, something to eat, and a conversation. CBC News reported on November 22, 2026, that owner Amrin Satani built the café around that third item. She arrived in Canada from India in 2011 knowing no one, and she still remembers having more to say than anyone to tell. She had planned to study medicine. Health care was not her path. In 2025 she opened the shop, also called Humanity Café.

Satani said the conversation is for a happy day, a lonely one, a complaint, or a win, and that it carries no charge and no tip. She called it the best thing a person can get there. Counsellor Shane Trudell, clinical director of Nightingale Counselling in Vancouver, told CBC he is glad the offer is in person. Asking out loud for company, he said, is a high bar, and the people who clear it are the ones likely to benefit. The Canadian Mental Health Association says 13 percent of Canadians aged 15 and older often feel lonely. Trudell estimates loneliness is more common than the people who name it, and that the remedy is not the same for everyone. The news is this menu. It does not say a café talk stands in for clinical care.
