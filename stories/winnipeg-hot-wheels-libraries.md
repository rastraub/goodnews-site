Two front yards in Winnipeg are lending toy cars the way a corner box lends paperbacks. CBC News reported on November 27, 2026, that Justin Nicol built a Hot Wheels library outside his Transcona house after his own collection filled a room. He started with a medicine cabinet. When children kept coming, he built a larger blue box, added car decals, and nailed shingles on the roof. Kids may take a car or trade one. He said two boys down the street check the box on the way to school, and that they sometimes arrive with a bag of cars to restock it. The younger boy likes to line them up.

Across the city, in St. James, Farron Wyer built a box she calls the Little Pit Stop. She has two young sons, one of them autistic, and she wanted a place where sharing was the point of the stop. She hung lights and set painted kindness rocks around the base. She and Nicol had not heard of each other. CBC described the match as a coincidence. The news is two working boxes and the children already using them. It is not a city program, and it does not say how many cars are in either yard.
