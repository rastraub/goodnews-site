Guildford has been wired as a living laboratory, and two new studies say its green spaces are doing quiet work on heat, noise, and dirty air. Mongabay reported on October 28, 2026, that a sensor network across the Surrey town produced finer measurements than many earlier park studies.

One study of a large park found the surroundings cooler, quieter, and less polluted, with that effect shrinking about every 100 metres from the boundary. Different kinds of greenery cooled the people inside them differently. A second study looked at extreme heat. Large green spaces such as woodland showed the smallest rise in temperature. Stoke Park, about 52 hectares in the middle of town, is one of those places: fields, a pond, gardens, and oak, spruce, and beech.

The researchers said planners could use that detail when they design green space. The findings describe Guildford's existing parks. They do not forecast a future heat wave.
