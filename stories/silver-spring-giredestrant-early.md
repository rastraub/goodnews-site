The Food and Drug Administration approved once-daily giredestrant as the first new hormone therapy in more than 20 years for a specific group of people with early-stage breast cancer: ER-positive, HER2-negative, stage I to III disease at intermediate or high risk, taken after surgery. In the 4,170-patient phase 3 lidERA trial, giredestrant cut the risk of invasive recurrence or death by 30 percent compared with standard hormone therapy. At three years, 92.4 percent of people on giredestrant were free of invasive cancer, compared with 89.6 percent on the standard therapy. That is a few percentage points, not a guarantee. The medicine has side effects, and patients and their doctors have to weigh them. Longer follow-up on overall survival is still being collected. This approval is for that early-stage use after surgery. It is not the separate approval, reported earlier, of giredestrant with everolimus for a form of advanced breast cancer.
