A male Amur tiger named Amur walked out of an acclimatisation enclosure at Ile-Balkhash State Nature Reserve on October 31. The Astana Times reported the release on November 2, 2026. Before the gate opened, specialists examined his health, watched whether he could get food on his own, and noted how he reacted to people. He is wearing a satellite collar so the reserve can follow his route.

He is the second tiger returned to this landscape. A female named Umit was released on July 31 and is hunting independently, also with a collar. Two cubs that arrived with the adults remain in care until they are old enough for the same checks. Tigers disappeared from the Ili River and southern Balkhash region in the middle of the last century. The reserve, set up in 2018, has been rebuilding prey, including a September move of 30 kulans from Altyn-Emel. The report covers these two adults in the wild. It does not describe a population that is breeding on its own yet.
