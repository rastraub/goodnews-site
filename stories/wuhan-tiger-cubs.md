China Daily reported on November 13, 2026, that four Siberian tiger cubs are on view at Wuhan Wildlife Kingdom, the first litter bred at the park. They have moved into an upgraded nursery where specialist keepers provide round-the-clock care. Keepers watched the mother and the cubs after the birth and transferred the young gradually once they were eating meat on their own. In the nursery they get tailored diets, regular feeding, weight checks, and individual health records, in a clean, temperature-controlled room.

The cubs were born at the Mingzhishan enclosure, which opened in February on the woodland of Wumei Mountain. The renovated nursery opened to visitors this month. Its other young residents include twin brown bears, spotted hyenas, coatis, and macaques. Visitors can watch health checks, training, and enrichment, and the park plans weekend science sessions for children. Separately, monitoring released in July by China's National Forestry and Grassland Administration put wild tigers in the Northeast China Tiger and Leopard National Park at 72, up from 27 in 2017. The Wuhan litter is a zoo first. It is not that wild count.
