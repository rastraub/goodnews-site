Volunteers have put the first trees of a new upland woodland into the ground on Skiddaw. BBC News reported on November 27, 2026, that Cumbria Wildlife Trust wants to plant 600,000 trees over the next ten years on Skiddaw Forest, a 3,000-acre reserve it calls the highest in England. The name comes from the land's old use for hunting, not from trees that were already there. The trust's aim is about 620 acres of temperate rainforest over the next century, open and scrubby rather than a closed canopy, plus work it has already started on more than 1,000 acres of peat bog.

More than 100 volunteers took part. They are due to plant about 20,000 trees this winter, with partner groups adding another 20,000. Volunteer Sharon Woodruff, from Kendal, said the group planted about 50 trees each, above 500 meters, after walking more than three miles each way from the nearest road. Project officer Isaac Johnston said the trust has been buying and planning the site for more than two years. The news is a planting week that has already happened, on a reserve the trust owns. It is not a claim that the mountain is a rainforest now.
