Twelve young griffon vultures from Spain have arrived around Isnello, in Sicily's Madonie, bringing the local count to 92. Corriere della Sera reported the transfer on November 10, 2026. The birds are part of a reintroduction of a species that used to live on the island. In past decades the population fell after poison set for foxes also killed vultures that later ate those carcasses. The young birds are in the aviary at contrada Terra dei Poveri, in Isnello. They will spend six to eight months settling in before they can fly free.

The project is led by Madonie Park with the International Foundation for Mediterranean Biodiversity and the municipality of Isnello. The transfer was done with GREFA, a Spanish group that recovers wildlife. Park president Giuseppe Ferrarello said the arrival is another step in a project the park has backed, and that bringing a species back returns part of the area's natural wealth. Ernesto Xusto Álvarez, president of the GREFA foundation, and park zoologist Antonio Spinnato are among the people on the work. The colony already includes several adult pairs. Griffon vultures feed mostly on animal carcasses. This count is the birds in and around this project, including the twelve that are not flying free yet.
