ABC News reported on November 15, 2026, that two pieces of sandstone from Petrie's Quarry, handed to the University of Queensland's Dinosaur Lab, appear to carry footprints of a sauropodomorph. Palaeontologist Anthony Romilio said the animal walked on its hind legs and kept a long neck and tail, and that it was older than the better-known long-necked dinosaurs people picture. The stone is about 230 million years old. One print was found by a teenager at the quarry more than 60 years ago. Both pieces were discovered in the 1950s.

Educator Sue-Belinda Meehan said the Petrie family opened the quarry in the 1860s, and that its pale stone went into the General Post Office, All Hallows School, the Old Customs House, and several churches. She pictured stonemasons treating a footprint as a flaw and cutting it out. Romilio said the Albion hills were once a wide, shallow river flat, also home to giant salamander-like animals. The news is the two stones in the lab. It is not a claim that a track is still visible in a city wall.
