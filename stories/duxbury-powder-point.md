Around 9:30 p.m. on Monday, Nov. 23, Duxbury officers at Powder Point Bridge could see part of a car about 800 feet offshore in the bay, Boston 25 News reported. Chief Michael Carbone said the first officer entered the water immediately. The water was over his head, and the current made the swim difficult. The car was a convertible and was still partly above the surface. He cut through the roof with a knife and pulled the driver, an adult woman, up onto the roof. Two more officers swam out and helped bring her and the first officer back to shore.

The three officers were taken to a hospital to be checked because of the cold water. The driver was taken to Beth Israel Deaconess Hospital-Plymouth. Police said they believed she had been the only person in the car. Divers, including Jack Ahern, then searched the submerged convertible. Ahern told Boston 25 the bay was cold and dark, with only a few feet of visibility on a good day. He said the headlights were still on while the car was at the surface, which gave the divers a direction, and that the lights were out by the time they went in.

Boston 25 said it was not clear what sent the car into the water. In the station's [report filed the night of the rescue](https://www.boston25news.com/news/local/duxbury-police-cut-through-roof-car-save-life-woman-who-crashed-into-water/PNVQURKUHZAHBIVFSG3B7BC6S4/), firefighters and paramedics took her to the hospital, and she was expected to be OK.
