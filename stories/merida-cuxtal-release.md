Twenty-one animals that neighbors in greater Mérida had rescued or turned in are back inside the Cuxtal municipal ecological reserve. Quadratín Yucatán reported on November 26, 2026, that the federal environmental prosecutor, Profepa, released them that day with protection staff from the state water agency Japay and specialists from Animaya zoo. Before the release, the animals went to wildlife management units, other sites that handle wild animals, and local zoos for veterinary care until they were fit to survive on their own.

The count was eight boa constrictors, five Virginia opossums, four white-nosed coatis, two raccoons, one gray hawk, and one great black hawk. Under Mexico's endangered-species standard, the paper said, the boa is listed as threatened and the great black hawk is under special protection. The local name in the story for the coati is tejón. The news is one release of animals that had already been rehabilitated. It does not say how long each animal had been in care, or whether any of them will be tracked.
