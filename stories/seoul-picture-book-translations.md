Twenty middle and high school students from immigrant families are translating children's picture books for elementary schools in Seoul. The Korea Herald reported the project on November 12, 2026, after the Seoul Metropolitan Office of Education announced it on Thursday. Three picture books, from Korea, Honduras, and Morocco, will be translated into Chinese, Russian, Vietnamese, and Mongolian. The education office is working with the local publisher Asian Hub. Finished books are to be distributed in December to elementary schools that offer Korean classes for students from multicultural families. Each set will include the books in five languages, including Korean.

The students and outside language experts will review the translations so younger children can follow the stories and the cultural context. The teens will also record read-alouds that readers can play by scanning QR codes printed in the books. Seoul Education Superintendent Jung Keun-sik said it matters that picture books made by immigrant students in their mother tongues will help younger students who are learning Korean, and that he hopes the translators take pride in their language and culture. An orientation photo with the article is dated October 16, so the announcement is not the first day of the work. The books are still on their way to schools. December is the planned handoff, not a delivery that has already happened.
