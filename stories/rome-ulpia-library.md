The western library of Trajan's Forum is open to the public again, Corriere della Sera reported on November 17, 2026. The room, identified as the Bibliotheca Ulpia, sits beside Trajan's Column, below today's street level, and was long used as a storeroom. It was uncovered in 1932 during work for the Via dell'Impero. Superintendent Claudio Parisi Presicce said it was built around the year 112, after the Dacian wars, and that it held about 10,000 volumes plus procurators' edicts and other legal writings. It stayed in use until the fourth century, when part of the collection was moved to the Baths of Diocletian.

The restoration took 18 months and cost €1,755,000 from Italy's National Recovery and Resilience Plan, under the Caput Mundi project. Until the end of the year, visits are on Friday, Saturday, and Sunday, from 9:20 to 15:20, in groups of no more than 20, every 40 minutes, at €7. The hall has two floors. Each wall has seven niches that were once lined with marble. Parisi Presicce said the Greek books belonged on this side and the Latin books in the other part of the library. A walkway leads in. Work included stopping water leaking from the gardens above, consolidating the old walls, and adding archaeological pieces, audio, and video. A projection narrated in the voice of Minerva reconstructs how the rooms were used. Mayor Roberto Gualtieri said the opening shows spaces the public had not seen. The site joins the Musei in Comune system. The news is this access. The ancient books themselves are not back in the niches.
