Taiwan News reported on November 16, 2026, that Kinmen County showed off a community otter project run with the Kinmen Wildlife Rehabilitation and Conservation Association and Shang Shan Ecology. Kinmen is the only place in Taiwan with wild Eurasian otters. The work includes pulling out fishing gear that can hurt the animals and watching the places they use. Shang Shan manager Chen Kuang-yao said the change in the communities mattered as much as the numbers, and that the company pays bonuses from its own funds to communities and people who do the work well.

Cameras set up by residents have filmed adults and cubs through the year, Central News Agency reported. Chen said winter cubs may mean the otters breed in summer and winter, not only on the spring-and-summer schedule people often expect. Taipei Zoo research assistant Jang-Liaw Nien-hung said European studies mostly describe winter as a poor time to raise young, because food is scarce, while zoo records and field work show Kinmen's otters reproducing in both seasons, possibly because the climate is warmer. He asked that mating sites be left quiet all year. Earlier zoo research found invasive tilapia have become a steady food, alongside shrimp, crabs, and other prey. The news is the cameras and that year-round pattern. It is not a census of every otter on the island.
