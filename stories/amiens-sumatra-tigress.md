Amiens Zoo scheduled a young Sumatran tigress for a flight to Japan so she would not be bred with her twin. France 3 reported the plan on November 23, 2026, and updated it on November 24. Rimba was born at the zoo in March 2024. The updated article said she was due to fly on Wednesday, November 25, to a zoo in Japan, awake in a crate she had been allowed to enter for a week so the box would not be a surprise. She would be able to stand during the flight.

Sumatran tigers are the smallest tiger subspecies, and the rarest. France 3 said the IUCN lists them as critically endangered in the wild. They once lived across the Indonesian archipelago and now survive wild only on Sumatra, with a wild population put at about 400, roughly matching the number in human care. Captive tigers descend from 37 founders. Specialists choose each pairing to keep those lines from folding in on themselves, with a long-term hope of animals that could one day be returned to the wild. Rimba's move is meant to keep her from breeding with her brother Toba.

The departure is the first change in the zoo's Sumatran tiger space. Toba also has to leave, because he can no longer share the enclosure with their mother, Ménya. The zoo did not give a date or a destination for him, and France 3 said that move is expected in early 2027. A new breeding male is then due for Ménya, who has already had three cubs. The article was written before the November 25 flight. This account does not add whether Rimba has landed.
