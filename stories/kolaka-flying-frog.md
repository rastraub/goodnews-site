A frog from the Mekongga Mountains of Sulawesi now has a scientific name, Phys.org reported on November 17, 2026. Rhacophorus kurniatiae was described in Zootaxa by researchers at Indonesia's Museum Zoologicum Bogoriense, the University of California, Davis, and other partners. The animals were found on biodiversity surveys from 2009 to 2013, not on a trip this month. At about 1,860 metres, near a seasonal lake the team called Danau Coca-Cola, they found thousands of bright green frogs small enough to sit on a cheek.

Flying frogs in this group use wide, webbed feet to glide between trees. This one differs in snout shape, call, DNA, and the way it breeds. Alamsyah Elang Nusa Herlambang said it is the first Rhacophorus they have seen lay eggs in a gelatinous mass on plants above or near water, with the eggs and tadpoles developing in that gel. Relatives build foam nests. Lead author Wahyu Trilaksono collected the frogs. The name honors Hellen Kurniati, a longtime herpetologist at the Bogor museum who died after years of work on Indonesia's amphibians and reptiles.

Andrew Engilis said the Mekongga work, including this frog and other new species from the same expeditions, is part of why the region is under consideration to become a national park. That is a discussion he described, not a park that has been gazetted. The news is the published description.
