A 10-hectare farm on the edge of Lusaka is selling vegetables to airline meals and bringing the kitchen scraps home as compost, Mongabay reported on November 23, 2026. Loctaguna Organics supplies radishes, asparagus, cherry tomatoes, and several lettuces to Newrest Zambia, which caters flights out of the capital. On the return trip the farm collects organic waste, so the scraps do not need a separate truck.

Founder Kanangwa Newlove said soil health is the base of the system. The farm grows vegetables, fruit, herbs, and animal products without synthetic fertilizers or pesticides and employs 12 people. Worms turn tea bags, cardboard, and scraps into compost. Goat and pig manure goes back on the fields, and some meal residues feed the pigs. Leaves of comfrey, fermented in water, are used as a pest spray, with tithonia flowers added. Newlove said an internal comparison of one hectare of onions put the farm's costs about 35 percent below a conventional setup, and that garbage-collection costs fell by about 35 percent and pig-feeding costs by about 45 percent after the scrap pickups began.

She also works with Kasisi Agricultural Training Centre to share the methods with small farms. The operation has an organic certification through Ubumi PGS Zambia Organics, a local participatory check rather than a distant auditor. Newlove said income from Newrest and from selling produce on site covers the farm, and that wages are above the recommended farm rate. She also said staffing is hard, including occasional theft. She did not say how much of the income comes from the caterer. The news is this loop on one farm. It is not a figure for how much airline waste Zambia diverts.
