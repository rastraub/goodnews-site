BBC News reported on November 18, 2026, that the National Trust is inviting volunteers to plant 169,000 trees at Elf Hills, on the Wallington estate near Morpeth. Sessions are set for November 21, 24, and 25. No experience or special tools are required, and shuttle buses will run from the visitor entrance to the site. The new wood is planned at 109 hectares, about 269 acres, with areas for nature, timber, and open pasture. Five of the trees were grown from seeds of the Sycamore Gap tree at Hadrian's Wall, as part of the Trees of Hope project.

Project manager Elaine More said the planting is meant to be a wood people can use for years. The trust said it should support red squirrels and pine martens. That is a hope for the habitat, not a count of animals already there. Since 2021 the charity has planted more than 500,000 trees at Wallington, toward a million on the estate by 2030. The Elf Hills work is funded by a £2.5 million English Woodland Creation Offer. The news is the invitation and the trees already planted elsewhere on the estate. The 169,000 at Elf Hills are not in the ground yet.
