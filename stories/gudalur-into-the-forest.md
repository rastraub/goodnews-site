A workshop in the Nilgiris is carving the plants that choke the forest into the art that explains it. The Hindu reported on November 25, 2026, that The Elephant People, a social enterprise in Gudalur, opened its first exhibition, Into the Forest. The group makes hand-carved sculptures, masks, and everyday objects from invasive plants, including Lantana camara and Senna spectabilis. More than 50 people work across carving, painting, design, production, and operations.

Rhea Antony, who leads storytelling and design, told The Hindu that sharing space with wildlife is ordinary life in Gudalur, inside the Nilgiris Biosphere Reserve. The invasive plants spread through native habitat, suppress grasses, and can push animals toward farms and houses when food thins out. The workshop clears that growth with the forest departments of Tamil Nadu, Kerala, and Karnataka, then treats the wood as material instead of waste. The show includes large sculptures, smaller carvings, and installations. For children there are painting and story-building workshops, plus play that introduces species, habitats, and the sounds animals make.

Antony said the group has spent three years gathering stories of wildlife, people, food, and older ways of living with the forest. The exhibition is one room of that work. It does not, by itself, clear the invasive plants from the reserve.
