A village on Malaysia's east coast marked its largest river-terrapin release in the history of a project that began in 2011. The New Straits Times reported on October 24, 2026, that 690 southern river terrapin hatchlings went into the Kemaman River at Kampung Pasir Gajah on October 3.

The Turtle Conservation Society of Malaysia, led by Chen Pelf Nyok, collected 1,673 eggs during a 34-day nesting window in February and March. Of those, 1,254 hatchlings emerged. Keepers raised them for about four months, then chose 690 for the river. The society says that since 2011 it has incubated 13,224 eggs and released more than 8,000 young turtles.

The southern river terrapin, Batagur affinis, is critically endangered. In Malaysia it is protected under the Wildlife Conservation Act 2010 and is known from rivers in Kedah, Perak, Terengganu, and Pahang, not from Sabah or Sarawak. Release day also included a coloring session, a bracelet workshop, and a pre-loved clothes exchange. One large release does not by itself rebuild the species. It does show a village staying with the work for fifteen years.
