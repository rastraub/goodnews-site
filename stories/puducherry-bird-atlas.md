A September survey around Puducherry logged 158 bird species and 43,346 individual birds. The Hindu reported on October 14, 2026, that the fourth round of the Puducherry Bird Atlas brought together 54 people, among them experienced birdwatchers, school and college students, and community volunteers. They covered 68 grids and filed 300 checklists on eBird.

Svarnim, a unit of the Sri Aurobindo Society, organized the count across the Puducherry-Villupuram-Auroville-Cuddalore region. Earlier rounds ran in December 2025, March 2026, and June 2026. The June round had recorded 124 species. Coordinator Ankush Nirmalkar said the grid is repeated each season because wetlands such as Oussudu and Bahour do not stop at a district boundary.

Of the 43,346 birds, 43,130 were assigned to a confirmed species and 216 were left in a broader group. Notable records included two European rollers, a lesser frigatebird, red-necked phalaropes, great knots, and Temminck's stints, along with a long list of ducks, rails, shorebirds, and raptors. T.P. Raghunath of Svarnim said the four seasons will be combined into a first-year report, with recommendations for the wetlands and the people who depend on them, and that the same protocol is meant to be repeated so later years have a baseline.
