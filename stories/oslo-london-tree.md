This year's Christmas tree for Trafalgar Square was felled on Friday in a forest outside Oslo, and it already has a name. BBC London reported on November 21, 2026, that the spruce is 21 metres tall, about 69 feet, and about 75 years old. It is nicknamed Jolly Cheery. The Lord Mayor of Westminster, Karen Scarborough, and the Mayor of Oslo, Anne Lindboe, cut it with a two-handled saw.

Norway has sent a tree to London every year since 1947, a standing thank-you for Britain's support during the Second World War, when King Haakon VII and the Norwegian government lived in exile in the city. Lindboe said the message each year is that Oslo has not forgotten. The lights go on in the square on Thursday, December 3, with both mayors there. For the second year, a social media poll helped choose the tree. Organisers said the road legs in Norway and England will use electric and biodiesel vehicles, cutting the journey's carbon footprint by 80 percent, and that the tree will cross the North Sea on a regular cargo ship from Brevik. In London it will be dressed in the usual Norwegian way, with 25 metres of festoon lighting and energy-efficient bulbs.
