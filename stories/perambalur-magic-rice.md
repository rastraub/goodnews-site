Students at Dhanalakshmi Srinivasan College of Agriculture in Perambalur have grown traditional Samba paddy on 50 acres by natural farming methods, The Hindu reported on November 16, 2026. Two of those acres are Komal Saul, a variety also called Magic Rice, grown by Indigenous communities in the Sivasagar region of Assam's Brahmaputra valley. Seedlings go in a foot apart, and the crop takes about 150 days. The grain is not boiled. A soak in warm water for 30 to 40 minutes is enough to soften it.

The students worked the whole sequence: ploughing, nursery beds, transplanting, weeding, organic fertilizer, and harvest. The Hindu said that skipping a high-heat boil leaves more vitamins and antioxidants in the grain. That is the account given for this variety. It is not a laboratory nutrition trial, and the 50 acres are this college farm, not a new figure for rice in Tamil Nadu.
