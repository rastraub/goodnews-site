Bogong moths are moving through Sydney in numbers people have not been used to seeing. Nine News reported on October 14, 2026, that a swarm blown off course by wind and rain settled on the sand at Bondi Beach, part of a migration of millions along the east coast. The moths make this flight every spring toward alpine caves. This year the rise is large enough that holiday-makers in the Blue Mountains and residents along the coast have been posting swarms at their doors. The insects are drawn to artificial light, which is one reason they pile up on buildings.

Zoos Victoria's Moth Tracker, which logs photos people send in, shows thousands of confirmed sightings this season, clustered on Sydney and the New South Wales south coast, with more reports inland and in Victoria. The same tracker shows how thin the migration was in 2019-20, when hot weather and the bushfire season knocked the numbers down. Nine News described the moth as endangered. A thick spring does not retire that status.

The swarms on the coast are a stop, not the destination. A thick spring does not mean the long decline has been retired.
