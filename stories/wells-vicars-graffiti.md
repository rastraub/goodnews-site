Restorers at Vicars' Close in Wells found graffiti more than 250 years old while working on a tower roof, the BBC reported on November 20, 2026. The close is thought to be Europe's oldest residential street. Its houses date to the 14th century and were built as homes for people serving Wells Cathedral. There are 27 of them, with a chapel, a library, a treasury, and a dining hall linked to the cathedral by a covered walkway.

The tower roof was almost covered in scratched marks: initials, decorative patterns, outlines of hands and feet, and dates. One foot outline goes back to the 1780s. The Very Revd Toby Wright, Dean of Wells, called the find one of the gifts the close keeps offering, hidden for centuries. The Grade I listed houses are being restored, and some are set to open to the public next year. Georgina Landau, the cathedral's director of development, said the graffiti will be part of future tours, and that fundraising for the project is not finished. The news is this discovery on one tower. It is not a claim that every roof on the street has been read.
