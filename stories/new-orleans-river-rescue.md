Nearly 70 New Orleans Fire Department members helped pull people off a river cruise boat and out of the Mississippi after a tanker hit it, WAFB reported. On November 13 the Atlantic Asphalt tanker collided with American Cruise Lines' American Heritage at the Port of New Orleans, and the boat later capsized. Chief Matthew Spector, the department's chief of safety and special operations, said in a Tuesday interview that firefighters were already on the dock, under the wharf, and in boats when he arrived. They boarded the paddle wheeler with other responders and helped 70 to 80 people off. When the ship began to capsize, one firefighter jumped back to shore and injured his foot. Spector said that firefighter is recovering and should return to work.

The work then shifted to people in the water. Crews put out boats, ladders, and flotation devices, helped a tugboat, and hoisted people onto the dock. First responders and Good Samaritans together brought nearly 200 people to safety. Spector credited the training and the habit of working with partner agencies, not a last-minute scramble. A visitor who later looked at the capsized boat said everyone got out. The article does not publish a separate official injury count for the passengers.
