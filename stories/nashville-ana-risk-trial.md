A randomized trial at Vanderbilt found that a transparent AI risk score, paired with a fast-track referral to rheumatology, shortened the wait for an autoimmune diagnosis, Vanderbilt Health News reported. Among nearly 2,000 analyzed patients who already had a positive ANA test, flagging the high-risk patients cut the median time to diagnosis from 138 days to 43. The study is published in NEJM AI and was supported by the National Institutes of Health. The 43 and 138 figures are medians in this group of patients. The result is a faster diagnosis, not a treatment and not a cure, and it applies to the patients in the trial.
