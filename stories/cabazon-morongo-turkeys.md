Volunteers packed 15,000 free turkeys on Monday at the Morongo Community Center near Cabazon, The Press-Enterprise reported. It was the 40th annual Thanksgiving outreach of the Morongo Band of Mission Indians. The birds went to 136 nonprofit groups, churches, and charities across the Inland Empire, the Coachella Valley, and Los Angeles. Those groups plan to hand them out at community events, food pantries, and giveaways before Thanksgiving.

Volunteer Aaron de la Cruz, who works at the center and helps with the tribe's after-school program, said he felt fortunate to pack the boxes. Tribal Chairman Charles Martin said the tribe did not have much to give when the program began, and that the idea was that no one should go hungry during the holidays. Tribal officials said that since it started, the program has provided nearly 4.8 million holiday meals and almost 245,000 turkeys to local families, older residents, and veterans. Amanda Galindo of the Coachella Valley Rescue Mission in Indio said her shelter received 300 of Monday's turkeys. The 15,000 birds are this year's giveaway. The 4.8 million meals are the running total since the program began.
