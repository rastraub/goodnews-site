A biologist at Ranganathittu Bird Sanctuary found a short-lived bamboo mushroom on the woodland floor and called it a delightful encounter. The Hindu reported the sighting on November 20, 2026, from Mysuru. S. Gagana, the sanctuary's biologist and nature education officer, said she came across Phallus indusiatus on November 17: a dark cap, a white stalk, and a lace-like skirt. The net around the stalk is the indusium, which is why the fungus is also called the veiled lady or the bridal veil. The common name bamboo mushroom comes from its habit of growing with bamboo, though Gagana said it also appears in other moist, shaded spots rich in rotting leaves.

Deputy conservator Prabhu Gowda, of the Mysuru wildlife division, said it grows on dead bamboo when moisture and temperature suit it, and that it has a distinctive smell. C. Hemavathi, a retired botany professor in Mysuru, confirmed the identification. She said the species is not uncommon in wetter country such as Kodagu, so its appearance in Ranganathittu's riverine woods is the notable part. The fungus is eaten in some Asian kitchens after it is processed, and Gagana warned that a wild mushroom should not be picked for the table without an expert identification. The news is this one sighting, not a claim that the sanctuary has a new population count.
