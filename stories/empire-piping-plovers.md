The Great Lakes piping plover reached 99 breeding pairs in 2026, Michigan Public reported, up from roughly a dozen pairs in the late 1980s. A record 184 chicks fledged. It was the fifth record breeding season in a row for the endangered shorebird.

That count is almost two-thirds of the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service recovery goal of 150 pairs, 50 of them outside Michigan. The largest nesting site is Sleeping Bear Dunes National Lakeshore. The birds are still pressed by shrinking beach, predators, people on the sand, and gaps in seasonal conservation staffing. The record is a count of pairs and flying chicks, not a declaration that the species is out of danger.
