Ústecký deník reported on November 13, 2026, that Zoo Ústí nad Labem has bred a bear cuscus. Spokesperson Veronika Schranková called it one of the most important breeding successes of recent years. The youngster is the second born in a European zoo in the past 12 months. The other was born at Tierpark Berlin. The zoo said that before this, only Zoo Wrocław and Tierpark Berlin had raised the species.

Keepers watched the pair mate at the end of June, which the zoo said no other zoo had managed to record. Chief zoologist Petra Padalíková said the first movements in the mother's pouch showed up in mid-October, and keepers then saw the baby while the female cleaned the pouch. Like other marsupials, bear cuscuses are born very undeveloped and finish growing there. The sex is not yet known. The zoo has kept the species since 2017 and received this breeding pair from Berlin at the end of 2024. Director Ilona Pšenková said the European population in human care is six animals, so each birth matters, and that raising this one is still ahead of them. The zoo now holds the only female in Europe.

Leaves are the main food. A pair needs about 200 kilograms of frozen leaves to get through winter, and volunteers and students help prepare those stores. Wild bear cuscuses live in the forests of Sulawesi and nearby islands, and that population is declining. This is one birth in a very small zoo population. It is not a count of animals in the forest.
