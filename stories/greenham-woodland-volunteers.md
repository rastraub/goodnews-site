Residents spent two days cutting invasive shrubs out of an eight-acre woodland in Greenham, near Newbury in Berkshire. The BBC reported that the work was organized by Protect Earth. Landowner Simon Rance, who bought the site about a year ago, said rhododendrons had been suffocating it.

Neighbor Greg Loxton was among the people who showed up. Phil Sturgeon of Protect Earth told the BBC that plants like these shade out the woodland floor, and that some neglected woods end up with very little living in them. Volunteer Tom Walker used leave from a desk job to help. The tools on site included loppers, bow saws, and pole-mounted machetes, used on rhododendron and cherry laurel.

Rance said he hopes the wood can eventually have public paths and outdoor lessons, because a school is nearby. That is a plan, not a trail that is open now. The two days of cutting are a start on letting light back onto the ground.
