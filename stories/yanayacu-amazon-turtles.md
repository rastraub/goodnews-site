Peru's park service spent the middle of October putting Amazon river turtles back into the water. Peru21 reported on October 17, 2026, that the Ministry of Environment, through Sernanp, and the conservation group CIMA-Cordillera Azul released 14,645 turtles in and around Cordillera Azul National Park.

The count was 14,146 taricayas (Podocnemis unifilis), 454 charapas (Podocnemis expansa), and 45 teparos. On Friday, October 16, 6,640 taricayas and charapas went into Quebrada Carnaval and the Cushabatay and Ucayali rivers. Releases had already begun on October 9 in San José de Yanayacu, Santa Catalina, and Boca Noaya.

Peru21 said the turtles face illegal egg collection, capture of the animals themselves, and changes to the waters they use. Charapas are among the largest freshwater turtles in South America. The October releases are a return of animals that had been kept for that purpose. They are not a new census of how many turtles now live in those rivers.
