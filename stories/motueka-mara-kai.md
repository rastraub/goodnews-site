A traditional food garden in Motueka is growing kai for the people who live nearby, and it is testing whether Māori potatoes and kūmara can be grown at a larger scale. RNZ's Country Life reported on November 21, 2026, from Te Māra o Puanga Kairau, on land owned by Wakatū Incorporation. Una Stephens, who was helping host visitors, said her aim is food that puts colour back in children's faces. The visitors were growers, exporters, grassroots groups, and researchers, gathered for a food strategy for Te Tauihu, the region of Tasman, Nelson, and Marlborough. Wakatū and the Riddet Institute are leading that work.

Miriana Stephens, Wakatū's deputy chair, said the region can grow a great deal and still has trouble getting that food fairly to local people. Speakers at the Motueka meeting noted longer lines at food banks and empty community pantries, including workers such as teachers and police among the people seeking food. The "Motueka Call" from the conference asks communities to grow and share more local food, back local producers, and cut waste. A regional strategy is to be finished by September 2028. Angela Morris showed delegates her market garden at Toad Hall, which supplies her café. None of this is a claim that the lines are already shorter. The garden is producing food now. The plan is still being written.
