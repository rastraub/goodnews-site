A recycling crew in Cannock stopped the truck and kept a neighbor from bleeding to death. BBC News reported on November 26, 2026, that Jack Moxon cut his wrist on November 17 while pushing a bag down into his bin. The lid of a tin severed an artery. Hospital staff later told him that without first aid he could have bled out in five to eight minutes.

Three Biffa loaders, Ash Cheneler, Anthony Brown, and Adam McLean, were coming down the street for the collection. Cheneler said he first thought Moxon was waiting for the bin to be emptied. A colleague saw that something was wrong. Three more workers, Kyle Bate, Mick Aston, and Ross Hollis, brought bandages from nearby. Michael, a driver and first aider, ran to the McArthurGlen shopping centre for a bleed kit. Moxon said the six of them worked without hesitation and kept him going for about 25 minutes. He later met them again and told them he would not be alive without them.

An ambulance that was first described as hours away reached him about an hour after the cut, after a second call. The West Midlands Ambulance Service apologised and said it was reviewing the calls. The news is the crew's first aid on the street. It is not a finding about how the ambulance service is staffed.
