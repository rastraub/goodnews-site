The University of Exeter reported on November 13, 2026, that juvenile green turtles are treating a protected wetland in Sharjah as a home. A study tracked nine animals with shells shorter than 60 centimetres and found they spent more than 80 percent of their time in the Al Qurm Wa Lehhfaiiah Protected Area, on the Gulf of Oman coast. The turtles were five times more likely to be in a man-made basin than in surrounding habitats. The basin was dug in 2003 as an extension of the Khor Kalba mangroves. The wider waterfront plan changed, and the basin stayed.

Dr Owen Exeter said the basin floor is covered in algae and that few predators such as sharks reach it through the mangroves. The natural channels still matter, because they let the turtles move with the tides. The site has been a Ramsar wetland and a Key Biodiversity Area since 2013, and Sharjah currently protects most of it from building, fishing, and boating. It is described as the last mangrove ecosystem on the UAE's east coast. One tracked turtle swam about 700 kilometres south to central Oman. The paper, in Aquatic Conservation, is about nine juveniles in this wetland. It does not claim the nesting beaches are secure.
