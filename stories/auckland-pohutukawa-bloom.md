The pōhutukawa are opening, and not all of them are the red people expect. RNZ reported on November 25, 2026, that Bruce Burns, an associate professor at the University of Auckland, walked through the variety as the Christmas trees started to bloom.

He said at least 42 cultivars are produced around the country for gardens. Besides the familiar wild tree, plantings include a Tahitian species, Metrosideros collina, common in Auckland gardens, and Metrosideros kermadecensis from the Kermadec Islands. The wild pōhutukawa's natural range runs only about as far south as a line from Gisborne to Taranaki. South of that, including Wellington and the South Island coast, the trees were carried by people and have naturalised. A golden form was found on Motiti Island around 1940 and has been planted since. White and pink trees turn up rarely. Wild trees around the Rotorua lakes are known for a pinker flower.

Flowering runs from late November into early February, but a single tree blooms for about two weeks. Burns said trees that flower early one year tend to flower early again, so the display is staggered, with a peak toward Christmas. He also said the tree has been taken to places such as California, Spain, and South Africa, where it can crowd out local plants. New Zealanders complain about weeds brought in from elsewhere. This is a case of the traffic going the other way. The bloom at home is the news. It is not a claim that the tree is harmless everywhere it was planted.
