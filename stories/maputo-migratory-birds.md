Shorebirds that nest in the Arctic are spending the other half of the year on mudflats south of Maputo. Mongabay reported on October 20, 2026, that Maputo National Park links Eurasian breeding grounds with wintering sites in southern Africa. Alex Ngari of BirdLife International's Africa programme said the park's lagoons, mangroves, and tidal flats matter on that route. The curlew sandpiper, Calidris ferruginea, listed as vulnerable, breeds in the Arctic and feeds on invertebrates in the mud. Ospreys, Madagascar bee-eaters, flamingos, and herons use the same coast.

The park was created in December 2021 by combining older terrestrial and marine reserves, about 1,700 square kilometers. More than 5,000 animals have been moved in since 2010. Mozambique's conservation agency, ANAC, co-manages the park with the Peace Parks Foundation. Bartolomeu Soto said communities are involved in mangrove restoration, fisheries management, and monitoring. The landscape is tied to the iSimangaliso wetlands across the border as a UNESCO site. The East Atlantic Flyway crosses about 75 countries and carries on the order of 90 million birds. A protected mudflat in Mozambique cannot hold a migration together if the other stops fail. A recent global bird assessment, cited by Mongabay, found nearly half of assessed migratory species in decline. The park is one working stop, not the whole route.
