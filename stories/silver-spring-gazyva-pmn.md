The Food and Drug Administration approved Gazyva, also called obinutuzumab, for adults with primary membranous nephropathy who are at risk of the disease getting worse. The goal of the approval is to induce remission of proteinuria, protein leaking into the urine. It is the first drug approved specifically for that use in adults. Gazyva was already approved for certain cancers, for active lupus nephritis, and for childhood-onset idiopathic nephrotic syndrome. In the phase 3 MAJESTY trial, 142 adults were randomly assigned to Gazyva or to tacrolimus for 104 weeks. At week 104, 36 percent of the Gazyva group met the study's definition of complete remission, compared with 6 percent of the tacrolimus group. Those figures are for this trial, not for every person with kidney disease. The label carries a boxed warning for hepatitis B reactivation and for a rare brain infection called PML. Common side effects in the study included infusion reactions and upper respiratory infection. The approval is not a cure.
