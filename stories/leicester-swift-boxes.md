BBC News reported on November 15, 2026, that swifts have made breeding attempts in nest boxes installed across Leicestershire. More than 360 boxes went up through Leicestershire County Council's Swift Action for Swifts project and the Leicestershire and Rutland Ornithological Society. Six breeding attempts are confirmed, and monitoring is continuing. House sparrows, great tits, and blue tits have used some of the boxes as well.

Natural England's species recovery programme paid for the work, including 150 swift callers that play colony sounds to draw birds in. Adam Tilbury, the council's cabinet member for the environment, said these are the first breeding attempts in the project boxes, and that swifts usually do not start breeding until several years after they fledge. The project team tied the wider decline to lost nest sites in buildings and fewer insects, and said swifts are often about three or four years old before they breed. Six attempts are an early sign. They are not a recovered county population.
