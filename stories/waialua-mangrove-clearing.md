More than 40 volunteers spent the weekend in Waialua clearing mangroves and debris from Ki'iki'i Stream, Hawaii News Now reported. Residents, farmers, heavy-equipment operators, and Team Rubicon worked the banks together. The aim was to open more than a mile of the stream, out to where it meets the ocean, so water can move through.

Waialua resident Kalei Gamiao said people had been asking for permission for more than 30 years. He and his father, Derek Gamiao, helped lead the project. They told the station it took years because they needed the Gilman Estate, which owns the land, to agree. Once that yes arrived, the crew went to work as a neighborhood, not as a single contractor.
