Biologists carried 204 olive ridley eggs off beaches in Seal Beach and Huntington Beach and drove them to the Aquarium of the Pacific in Long Beach. The Los Angeles Times reported on September 25, 2026, that the nests, found September 16 and September 19, are the first known naturally occurring sea turtle nests on the U.S. West Coast. Forecast surf from storms off Mexico, plus high tides, was expected to wash them away.

Teams from NOAA and the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service packed the eggs in sand-filled coolers and set them in a quiet recovery room for a 45- to 60-day incubation. It is not yet known whether any eggs are viable, and they will be left undisturbed. If hatchlings do emerge, the aquarium plans to release them quickly so they still have the energy for the swim offshore.
