Fifty-three cattle are grazing at RSPB Wild Haweswater so that thicker plants do not take the whole hillside, BBC Newsround reported on November 19, 2026. The herd is Scottish-bred Belted Galloways and Luings. Site manager Glen Swainson said their short legs, thick double coats, and wide backs suit uneven ground and harsh weather. Their long tongues wrap around vegetation and tug it, which leaves plants at different heights. That mix, he said, helps insects, small mammals, amphibians, reptiles, and birds.

Young trees matter on the reserve because they can slow the flow of water and give animals shelter. Six horses work the same ground, trampling dominant plants and spreading wildflower seed. The report is about this herd and those horses at Wild Haweswater. It does not say the reserve has finished changing, or how many new trees have taken hold.
