The Guardian reported on November 19, 2026, that a bar-tailed godwit called Phil has finished a round trip of about 6,800 miles between Norfolk and Siberia. It is the first time this migrant's route from Britain has been mapped in that detail. The Wash Wader Research Group fitted a tracker weighing 6 grams in January and named the bird for Phil Ireland, the group's longtime leader.

The tracker followed him from the Wash to the Wadden Sea coasts of the Netherlands, Denmark, and Germany in March, then to the Taymyr Peninsula in central Siberia in late May. On the return he crossed the Arctic Ocean off northern Norway, paused on three small Norwegian islands, and came back through the Wadden Sea. He has been recorded again at RSPB Snettisham, where thousands of birds pack the shore at the highest tides. RSPB coastal adviser Jeff Kew said a bird tagged on the Wash in January had gone thousands of miles and made it back. Dr Nigel Clark of the research group said the Wash is Britain's most important coastal wetland for migratory waders, and that Phil's track shows it is one stop on a longer flyway, the east Atlantic route used by millions of birds between the Arctic and South Africa. The news is this one mapped journey.
