The Les Wilson Barramundi Discovery Centre in Karumba released 3,000 fingerlings into local wetlands last week, the first baby barramundi the Gulf hatchery has reared in more than five years. ABC News reported the release on November 22, 2026. The centre, a draw for the small town, shows visitors how fish are raised and put back to replace fish that anglers take. It had not produced a single barramundi since 2020.

Carpentaria Shire Mayor Jack Bawden said the council took over the hatchery in 2012, after commercial fishers had run it, and that staffing trouble followed. Late last year the council put David Hammann, a former commercial fisher on staff, in charge even though he had no formal aquaculture training. He told ABC he spent a year trying tank sizes, new broodstock, and what he called local secrets. The parents are wild Southern Gulf barramundi, a stock distinct from east-coast fish. Hammann said females are checked for egg size before hormone injections and spawning.

He has a permit to release up to 100,000 into the Norman River and another 100,000 near Burketown, and he hopes to fill those numbers next season. Before 2020 the centre also sold low-cost fingerlings to fishing groups stocking lakes at Mount Isa and Cloncurry. Bawden called 3,000 a real improvement on zero. The news is this release. It is not those larger quotas, and it does not say how many of the 3,000 will survive.
