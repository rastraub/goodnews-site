Leo Youngdahl, 6, found a conulariid while turning rocks at Schramm Park State Recreation Area, The Nebraska Courier reported. He had been searching for crinoids, the sea-lily fossils common in eastern Nebraska. The rock he flipped held a 2.75-inch herringbone imprint. Kelly Ekue, outdoor education specialist at the Schramm Education Center, did not recognize it among the six fossils the park usually sees. Experts she contacted identified a conulariid, described as a jellyfish-like animal shaped like an upside-down pyramid, which lived between 200 million and 500 million years ago.

The last known Nebraska specimens were found 55 years ago, across the Platte at Louisville and Weeping Water. Youngdahl left this one at the park for education programs. The outcrop is bedrock about 350 million years old, from when a saltwater sea covered Nebraska. Ekue asks visitors to bring a camera, leave picks and hammers at home, and stay out from under overhanging rock. Most finds there are smaller than a penny.
