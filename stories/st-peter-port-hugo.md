Year 5 pupils in Guernsey's States schools are spending Friday lessons on Victor Hugo's years on the island, out of the classroom. BBC News reported the programme on November 16, 2026. The States and Guernsey Museums are working with the Victor Hugo Centre and the Priaulx Library in St. Peter Port. The sessions run every Friday through the end of November. They are part of the Cultural Enrichment Programme, which gives primary-age children free experiences during the school day.

Pupils have toured Victoria Tower, handled Victorian objects, and met an actor playing Hugo. At the Priaulx Library they have seen a giant octopus puppet drawn from his novel Toilers of the Sea. Miffy Lane, the museums' access and learning manager, said the sessions are living history: a short play built by researchers who know the local record. Dinah Bott of the library said Hugo's story is woven into the island, and that she would like local history in the regular curriculum. That wish is hers. The news is the Friday visits already under way for these year 5 classes.
