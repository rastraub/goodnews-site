Four cheetah cubs at Zoo de La Flèche now have names. Ouest-France reported on November 12, 2026, that the zoo's statement that Thursday called the males Kembu, Zuwe, and Yaru, and the female Saja. The names refer to places in the cheetah's range in sub-Saharan Africa. The cubs were born in May, the park's first litter, a few months after it opened a breeding centre devoted to the species. Since September they have been in a new enclosure and visible to the public. They had simply not been named yet.

The zoo said the four are growing well. They spend a lot of time playing together and watching their mother, and their separate personalities are still hard to tell apart. Nursing has ended, and each cub now gets its own ration. They respond well to keeper activities and have started getting used to handling for future veterinary exams. The same report noted two other births at the park. Squirrel monkeys Glue and Intie, a mother and daughter, each had a baby a few weeks apart, at the end of August and in mid-September. On September 14 the park also had its first sunbittern chick, a bird known for the coloured circles it shows when it spreads its wings. The cheetah names are a label for a litter already on view. They are not a change in the number of cheetahs living wild.
