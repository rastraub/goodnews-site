Georgina Long and Anthony Thomas have jointly won the 2026 Prime Minister's Prize for Science, ABC Science reported on November 24, 2026. Each receives $250,000. It is the first time the top prize has been shared by two fields. Long is a medical oncologist at the University of Sydney and the Melanoma Institute Australia. Thomas is a theoretical physicist at Adelaide University.

ABC said Long led the international work that produced the first effective drugs for advanced melanoma. Survival for people with stage 4 disease improved 10-fold, and survival for people with secondary cancers in the brain improved 24-fold. Those figures describe trials already completed, not a new study announced with the prize. Her work also fed melanoma treatment guidelines in Australia, Europe, and the United States. She told ABC her goal is still to cure cancer. The prize does not say that goal has been reached.

Thomas's prize is for physics, not for a medicine. Early in his career he helped build a model of how protons are structured inside an atomic nucleus, work ABC said has shaped how scientists explain the smallest pieces of ordinary matter. The news is the shared award and the results already on the record. It is not a new patient trial.
