Richmond Library in North Yorkshire has opened at Mercury House on Station Road. BBC News published the Local Democracy Reporting Service account as the doors opened. The library left Queens Road, a site the council partly leased, for a building it already owns, where it shares space with other council services. Councillor Simon Myers, the executive member for culture, said the move had been months in the planning and that he was delighted to welcome users.

The council said the new site has an accessible entrance, disabled parking, and bus stops outside, after earlier worries about access. The old building had also had maintenance problems that closed some rooms to the public. Staff and volunteers spent recent weeks moving books. The new rooms include a larger children's section meant for more school groups, a dedicated IT area, and ground-floor space for the local studies collection.

Since 2017 the library has been run with Catterick, Richmond and Colburn Community Libraries, a community group that relies on volunteers, with council staff alongside them. Myers said he hopes the shared building brings in more readers and more volunteers. The news is this opening. It is not a count of how many people walked in on the first day.
