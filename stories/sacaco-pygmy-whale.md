A new extinct pygmy sperm whale, Scaphokogia huachafo, has been named from the Late Miocene Pisco Formation of Peru. Communications Biology published the description on November 9, 2026. The specimen, catalogued as MUSM 972, comes from Aguada de Lomas in the Sacaco basin, Arequipa, from layers laid down about 6.3 to 5.6 million years ago. The authors say the face is more asymmetrical than in other cetaceans they compared, and possibly more than in other mammals. The snout is a dense, downward-pointing tube of thick bone and ossified cartilage.

They also reconstructed the neck joint in close relatives and read the shape as fitting a bottom-feeding life, with no direct match among living toothed whales. That feeding picture is an inference from bones. It is not an observation of a living animal. The paper adds one species to the record of whale skulls. It does not change the count of pygmy sperm whales alive today.
