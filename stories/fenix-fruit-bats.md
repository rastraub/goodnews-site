Mongabay reported on November 13, 2026, that fruit-eating bats in the Atlantic Forest disperse seeds from more than 140 plant species and can deposit between 600 and 25,930 seeds per hectare a day even on degraded ground, according to Embrapa Forestry. Sandra Bos Mikich of Embrapa and Gledson Vigiano Bianconi of the Federal Institute of Paraná tested that habit at Vila Rica do Espírito Santo State Park in Fênix. Artificial fruits scented with essential oil of pariparoba drew bats about 50 meters out of the forest into a farm field. Later work isolated synthetic compounds from Ficus, Piper, and Solanum fruits so the lure would not require harvesting the wild fruit the bats eat. Lays Cherobim Parolin, a professor at the Pontifical Catholic University of Paraná, said those compounds are already made for other industries.

Bats in the study digest a meal in about 30 minutes and circle a scent for up to four minutes, long enough, the researchers say, to drop seeds. Of the seeds found at the attraction points, 73.3 percent belonged to pioneer species that can establish in the open. The team marked more than 1,800 great fruit-eating bats and Seba's short-tailed bats and radio-tracked 32. Bianconi said farmland did not stop them moving between forest patches. He also said the method is not yet a standard restoration protocol. Pedro Brancalion of the University of São Paulo said bats will also spread plants people do not want, including the introduced tropical almond. The English article is a translation of reporting first published in Portuguese on October 15. The field results are real. A recipe a restoration crew could copy everywhere is not.
