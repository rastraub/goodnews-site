The Live Turtle Museum Singapore reopened on November 20 at 11 Chencharu Close in Khatib, The Straits Times reported that day. It is the collection's fourth home since it began on Neil Road in 2001, and it had been closed for 18 months. Founder Connie Tan said the move cost $2 million. The plot is about 1,200 square metres, one of the larger sites the museum has had. Visitors can see indoor and outdoor habitats, join supervised feeding, and look at more than 3,000 turtle and tortoise objects. Species on show include the Aldabra giant tortoise, the alligator snapping turtle, the pig-nosed turtle, the Malaysian giant river turtle, and the radiated tortoise.

The animals did not all make the years of moving. Tan said the museum now has more than 600 turtles and tortoises, compared with more than 1,000 when it was at Chinese Garden from 2002 to 2018. She said temporary holding spaces make the animals uncomfortable and that their numbers drop. The previous site, at ORTO in Yishun, closed in February 2025 because the land is being redeveloped. The Chencharu Close lease is three years at a time, and the first term ends in September 2027. Construction problems delayed the opening after the lease was signed. A team from Nanyang Technological University is studying the museum's history and how people relate to turtles, Tan said. The news is this reopening and this head count. It is not a promise that the animals can stay past the current lease.
