Family Promise Rochester received $500,000 from Google to expand work that keeps families housed in southeastern Minnesota, KIMT reported. The money is aimed at three things: growing the eviction-prevention program, renovating Promise House, the shelter that can hold 13 families, and reaching the youngest children sooner. So far in 2026, the organization says the prevention program has helped keep 566 children housed, with financial help, a housing specialist, and links to local resources before a short crisis becomes homelessness. It puts the cost of keeping a family housed at about $1,300.

More than half of the children in the shelter are under 6, and because they are not in school yet they often miss the screenings older children get. The Google money will help the nonprofit work with child-care providers, Head Start, and parenting coaches. Executive director Carrie Moscho said the gift lets them keep more families in their homes and reach young children earlier. Luke Hellier, a Google public-affairs manager, said the company was supporting that mission. The organization was clear about the limit: the contribution covers new and expanded work. Day-to-day costs at Promise House, including advocates on site around the clock and basic supplies, still depend on gifts from people, congregations, and local businesses.
