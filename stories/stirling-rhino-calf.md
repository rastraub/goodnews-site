A southern white rhino calf was born at Blair Drummond Safari Park, near Stirling, on November 19. STV News reported the birth on November 24, 2026. The calf weighs 45 kilograms. Her mother is Dot and her father is Graham. Keepers said a pregnancy of about 16 months ended smoothly, and that both Dot and the calf are doing well.

Shonagh Bell, who leads the large-mammals team, said the calf is active, alert, and already coming to the bars in the morning. The calf will stay with Dot, apart from the park's other female rhinos, and is due to meet visitors this weekend. A public name will come later, through a competition.

Dot and Graham's family now includes this seventh calf, plus six grandchildren and five great-grandchildren. Offspring live at parks in France, Germany, the Netherlands, and Hungary. Kristine Fennessy Alexander, the park's animal collections manager, said southern white rhinos were nearly declared extinct about a century ago and called the recovery one of the stronger conservation stories she knows. The species is now listed as near threatened. The news is this birth and the plan to let visitors see the calf with Dot. It is not a count of rhinos outside the park.
