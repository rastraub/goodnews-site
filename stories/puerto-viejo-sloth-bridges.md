Conservationists are stringing rope bridges between trees on Costa Rica's Caribbean coast so sloths can cross gaps without coming down. The Associated Press reported from Puerto Viejo de Talamanca on November 24, 2026, that crews were installing the bridges in a landscape shared with hotels, tourists, and other development, including behind a beachfront hotel.

Sloths cannot jump between trees the way monkeys can. A small opening in the canopy can send them to the ground, where dogs, vehicles, and power lines are the risks the report names. The Sloth Conservation Foundation has installed about 410 bridges to keep that travel in the treetops. The news is those bridges and the reason they are there. It is not a tally of how many animals crossed on a given day.
