Ten northern bald ibises from French zoos have arrived in the Alt Empordà, in Girona, to join a reintroduction project. El Periódico reported the move on November 11, 2026. Four came from Champrépus, two from Fort-Mardyck, two from Jurques, one from Pont-Scorff, and one from the African Reserve of Sigean. They still have to pass veterinary checks before they join the group for good.

Fundació Alive said the ten will be added to 36 birds already in the programme, 21 from Andalusia and 15 from Austria, making 46. Release is planned for spring 2027, not now. The paper said the species disappeared from much of Europe and survived in the wild mainly in Morocco, with later breeding and reintroduction projects in some European regions. Adults are about 70 to 80 centimetres long, with dark iridescent feathers, a bare head, and a long curved reddish bill. They feed mostly on insects and other small animals in open ground. In March 2026 the project released 19 birds in Aiguamolls de l'Empordà natural park after a long acclimation. El Periódico called that the return of a breeding attempt to a place that had gone more than four centuries without one. Those birds carry GPS tags. Andalusia's Proyecto Eremita, started in 2004, reported at least 53 chicks fledged in 2026, its second-best year in the 2015-2026 span after 56 in 2025. Electrocution on power lines and deaths of young birds are still serious threats. The news is ten birds arriving into care. They have not been released.
