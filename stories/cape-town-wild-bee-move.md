When a colony of wild bees takes over a ceiling, a wall, or a garden ornament in Cape Town, Lourens and Dylan Kruger go and move it. Africanews, with the Associated Press, reported on October 16, 2026, that the father and son founded the Cape Honeybee Conservancy in 2018 and have since relocated more than 250 colonies. A recent call took about 40,000 bees out of a cement garden feature.

Each colony goes into a hive and then to a farm. The conservancy has six bee sanctuaries on farms. At Radford Dale, an organic wine estate in Elgin, about ninety minutes from the city, nine hives hold rescued bees, and the honey is sold. Vineyards do not depend on bees the way some fruit crops do, but the bees still pollinate cover crops and wild plants around the farm.

The Cape honeybee is native to the region and is not a threatened species. Bee specialist Mike Allsopp told the reporters there may be millions of wild colonies around Cape Town, and that most of them live unnoticed. People call when a large colony is close to children or pets. The conservancy's number is colonies moved, not a census of the city's bees.
