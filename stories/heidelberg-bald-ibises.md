Fourteen young northern bald ibises, hatched in 2026, are in a release aviary in Andalusia after a drive of more than 4,700 kilometers from Germany. Rhein-Neckar-Zeitung reported it on November 3, 2026. Ten of the birds came from Heidelberg Zoo and four from Straubing. Curator Joshua Förg and apprentice keeper Aaron Haueis took them to Zoo Jerez for Proyecto Eremita, a project of the Andalusian regional government and that zoo.

The birds spent a few days in quarantine, then moved to a larger aviary where they will learn the surroundings for several months. The pen is scheduled to open in February 2027. They are not free yet. Förg said the drive was exhausting and that, on site, he watched wild bald ibises fly in to roost in the evening. Northern bald ibises disappeared from most of Europe centuries ago. A migratory flock is being rebuilt in central Europe, while Andalusia already holds a colony that stays year-round. This move is meant to strengthen that resident group. It is not a count of birds already flying over Cádiz.
