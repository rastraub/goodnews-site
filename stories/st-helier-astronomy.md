BBC News reported on November 16, 2026, that pupils at First Tower School in St. Helier spent a lesson with an astronomer. The Jersey Astronomy Club invited Dr Robin Catchpole, of the Institute of Astronomy at the University of Cambridge, and he spoke with children aged 10 and 11. Year 6 pupil Lola, 11, said her favourite part was how the planets were made. Vihaan, 10, said he was fascinated by how black holes form.

Catchpole has written or co-written more than 100 research papers and articles. He told the BBC he makes space easier for children by starting with planets they already recognise. His own start was a book from his grandfather and a look at the Moon through a telescope on the seafront in Torquay, where he remembers craters and the shadows of mountains. Year 6 teacher Lee Bradshaw said the class was full of questions. The news is that visit. It is not a new telescope for the school.
