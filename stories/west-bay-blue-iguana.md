A blue iguana hatchling found in West Bay is back in care at the Queen Elizabeth II Botanic Park. Cayman Compass reported on November 7, 2026, that resident Parker Ebanks spotted the animal on October 30 and called the National Trust for the Cayman Islands. Joseph Jamieson, the trust's properties and facility manager, who also oversees fieldwork for the Blue Iguana Conservation Programme, said there is no blue iguana population in West Bay. Staff think the hatchling, about two to four months old, left the botanic park by accident on a car from the visitor lot.

The young iguana is in its own enclosure because it is still very small, and Jamieson said it is doing well. He thanked Ebanks for noticing an animal that is easy to miss. Blue iguanas live only on Grand Cayman. The news is this one rescue. It does not change the wild count, and the exact ride that carried the hatchling across the island is still a guess.
