Video games are how Dylan Huntbach spends long Iowa winters and stays in touch with friends, the University of Iowa College of Engineering reported. A cervical spinal cord injury more than a decade ago limited his hand movement, and many games no longer worked with a standard controller.

Students in a biomedical engineering course designed one that would. The prototype uses oversized buttons, a custom layout, and joysticks, built from acrylic, 3D-printed parts, and a Raspberry Pi. Huntbach tested it on his Nintendo Switch, liked how it felt, and kept it at his home in Waterloo. He said he could play online with friends and try games he had not been able to use. Associate professor Colleen Bringman said the course is built around making a device a real person can take home.
