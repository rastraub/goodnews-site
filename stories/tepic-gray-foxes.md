El Mañana, crediting El Universal, reported on November 14, 2026, that two young gray foxes were returned to a forest in Nayarit. They were rescued on October 16 in the Tepic region with clear signs of poisoning. Mexico's federal environmental prosecutor, Profepa, started treatment at once and checked both animals daily until their behaviour looked natural again. The agency said on social media that they were then released in a conserved wooded area of the state.

The article gives the scientific name Urocyon cinereoargenteus and says the species is not in a risk category under Mexico's NOM-059 list, while the general wildlife law still applies. It cites the IUCN status as Least Concern. The release is two juveniles back in a forest after care. It is not a claim that gray foxes as a species are recovering, and the paper does not name the poison.
