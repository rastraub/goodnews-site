NatureScot reported on November 16, 2026, that Scotland's largest beaver survey, done with Beaver Trust and the University of Exeter, found 349 confirmed territories and 56 possible ones across the Tay and Forth catchments, up to 405 in all. Earlier surveys recorded 39 territories in 2012, 114 in 2017-2018, and 251 in 2020-2021. Fieldwork in 2024-2026 covered 2,397 kilometres of rivers, burns, lochs, and ditches. Surveyors on foot and by canoe logged 20,853 signs, including burrows, dams, lodges, and feeding on trees and crops. Family size varies, so the survey is not a headcount of beavers.

Beavers are now established in Forth and Fife, along the Firth of Tay, and in the South Esk and Clyde catchments. In the longer-occupied Tay and Forth areas, gaps between territories are filling in. Half of the activity was in waterside woodland and 28 percent on farmland, a shift from 2020-2021, when farmland led. Roisin Campbell-Palmer of Beaver Trust said growth is slower than in earlier surveys as the core fills up and barriers limit some new ground. NatureScot mammal adviser Roo Campbell said more dams, relative to the population, may mean beavers are using smaller burns, and that a mitigation scheme remains open where beavers cause problems. The news is the territory map. It is not a population total, and it is not a claim that every landowner is untroubled.
