Two new species of marine tardigrade, the animals nicknamed water bears, have been named from an underwater mountain off Cabo Verde. Phys.org reported the description on November 20, 2026. The work, led by Roman Trokhymchuk and Alexander Kieneke at Senckenberg am Meer in Wilhelmshaven, is in the journal ZooKeys. The animals are Megastygarctides hekatae and Parastygarctus potercha. They came from sediment on Senghor Seamount, whose summit sits about 100 to 130 metres down while the surrounding seafloor is thousands of metres deep. The samples were collected in 2009 on the research vessel Meteor. What is new is the identification, not a fresh expedition.

The team examined 219 tardigrades. Of those, 212 were from the summit plateau and seven from a deep reference site about 100 kilometres farther north. The seamount yielded 20 taxa. The deep site yielded two. The authors read that gap as more evidence that seamounts can be rich even for animals barely visible without a microscope. Hekatae has genital spines, short hollow projections with hooks linked to the reproductive organs, a rare feature, and it is the deepest Megastygarctides yet recorded, at about 100 metres. Potercha keeps some juvenile traits as an adult, including a very small body and fewer claws. With these names, Megastygarctides has seven known species and Parastygarctus eight. The paper also updates the keys used to tell the genera apart.
