The BBC reported on November 18, 2026, that a nursery in Raja Ampat is raising Indo-Pacific leopard sharks for release, and that the number returned to protected water is approaching 100. The project, ReShark, collects fertilised eggs from public aquariums, flies them to eastern Indonesia, and hands them to local carers the report calls shark nannies. The sharks, Stegostoma tigrinum, are spotted, bottom-dwelling animals that eat small fish, crabs, and snails.

A BBC crew filmed one youngster, Ethan, moving from a tank into a fenced sea pen, and another, Christina, released after she measured just over a metre. Conservation International surveys between 2001 and 2021 logged only three leopard sharks in more than 15,000 hours on these reefs, which is why habitat protection alone is not treated as enough. The BBC described the releases as the first attempt to rebuild a wild shark population from animals bred in captivity. The goal is a group that survives and breeds. That has not happened yet. The checked news is the nursery, Christina's release, and a running total near 100.
