Thai Rath reported on November 13, 2026, that the Lower Andaman Marine and Coastal Resources Research Center surveyed rare marine animals around Libong Island, Samran Beach, Tase Bay, Sukon Island, Laoliang Island, Petra Island, and nearby waters in Trang. The area covered 729 square kilometres. The team used line transects and drones. The preliminary count was 104 animals of three species: 56 Indo-Pacific humpback dolphins, including six mother-calf pairs, scattered around Libong, Sukon, the Trang River mouth, and Laoliang; 28 dugongs, all at Chuyo Cape, including three mother-calf pairs; and 20 green turtles, 10 at Pak Khlong Wa–Chang Bridge and 10 at Chuyo Cape.

Observers said the animals that were assessed looked healthy. Dolphins and dugongs were swimming, feeding, showing maternal care, and, for some dolphins, breaching or courting. No injuries or signs of entanglement were recorded on those animals. The department said the photographs will be used for photo identification so the same animals can be followed later. Thai Rath drew the account from the department's Facebook post. The news is this survey's tally. It is not a population estimate for the whole Andaman coast.
