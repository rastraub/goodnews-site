The National reported on November 14, 2026, that 15 green sea turtles were released in the Al Qurm Protected Area, off Sharjah's east coast, after six months to a year of rehabilitation. The Sharjah Strandings Response Programme, run by the Environment and Protected Areas Authority, fitted trackers before the release. The releases happened late last month, timed for slightly cooler weather. Fadi Yaghmour, a scientific researcher with the authority, said some turtles had shell injuries consistent with boat strikes or propeller cuts, and some had lost flippers after rope or fishing line cut off circulation. Most, he said, were diseased or exhausted. Research has shown that green turtles foraging in UAE waters migrate to nest in Oman, and some of these animals needed rest after that kind of journey.

Intensive care is at Sharjah Safari Park. Turtles then move to Khor Kalba Mangrove nature park, which opened in 2021 and where the public can see them. Ten more turtles are still in rehabilitation. The programme also treats hawksbill, olive ridley, and loggerhead turtles, and one turtle now in care has fishing hooks in its beak and flipper. Yaghmour, a member of the IUCN Marine Turtle Specialist Group, said green turtles graze algae that would otherwise compete with coral, and they keep seagrass meadows from becoming patchy. A year ago the IUCN moved green turtles to least concern after four decades on the endangered list. He said some populations are still near threatened, vulnerable, or endangered. The news is these 15 releases, not a claim that the Gulf population is finished recovering.
