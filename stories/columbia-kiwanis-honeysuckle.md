Volunteers have cleared invasive bush honeysuckle from nearly all of Columbia's 20-acre Kiwanis Park, KOMU reported. Missouri Conservation Corps began the work in 2021. When they started, Stephen Bybee said, you could not see from the parking lot to the shelter. Spring wildflowers have returned as the shrubs came out, and the open sightlines help both visitors and the native habitat.

A removal day on Thursday used a warm, dry fall. Honeysuckle stays green after other plants go dormant, and its red berries make it easy to spot in October. Birds eat those berries and spread the seed. AmeriCorps VISTA volunteer Anna Do said crews keep watching treated ground so it does not grow back thick. Left alone, the shrub takes over the understory and hides trails. The group usually holds 30 to 40 volunteer days a year. Bybee said cutting can still work into mid-December, when the leaves are green and everything else is not.
