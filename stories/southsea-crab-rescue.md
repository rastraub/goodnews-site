Schoolchildren are catching crabs in a Southsea lake so the animals can be named, measured, and carried to the sea before the city removes built-up sediment. BBC reported on October 14, 2026, that pupils are using hook-free crab lines and pots. A team from the University of Portsmouth's Institute of Marine Sciences identifies and measures the crabs, and the city council organized the work.

The lake on the seafront has grown shallower in places after years of sediment, with algae in summer and problems for water quality. BBC said a trial clearance was set to start the Thursday after the report. Professor Alex Ford said the partnership gives children a way to collect data they can use in class, and the Marine Biology Research Group will keep the measurements for its work on the health of the local coast.

The article does not say how many crabs had been moved by October 14. The step it describes is the catch, the identification, and the release ahead of the dig.
