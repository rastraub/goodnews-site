A greater one-horned rhino calf has been born in Manas National Park, and the birth extends the family of Ganga, a rhino rescued in the 2004 floods. The Times of India reported it on October 31, 2026. A forest official said the calf is healthy, in the care of the forest department, in the Bansbari range, and was born about 10 to 15 days earlier.

The mother is Ganga-A, born and raised in Manas. Officials believe she is Ganga's first daughter. Ganga was rescued as a four-month-old calf in 2004 and hand-reared for nearly three years at the Centre for Wildlife Rehabilitation and Conservation, run by the International Fund for Animal Welfare and the Wildlife Trust of India under the forest department. In 2008 she was moved, with two other females, Mainao and Jamuna, into a specially built enclosure in Manas. Rhinos had become locally extinct there. After about three years of settling in, Ganga was released into the wild in 2010.

Assam's chief minister, writing on social media, described one rescued rhino becoming three generations in the wild. The report is this calf and that family line. It does not give a new park-wide population.
