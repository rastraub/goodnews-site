Tokyo Sea Life Park has seven healthy fairy penguin chicks that started as eggs in Australia. The Tokyo Metropolitan Government said on November 27, 2026, that twelve fertilized eggs left Ballarat Wildlife Park and Melbourne Zoo on June 19 and reached the park the next day in portable incubators that held the temperature and humidity. Eight hatched between June 23 and July 12. One of those chicks later died. The other seven are healthy and have been on public display since November 5. Four eggs stopped developing.

Fairy penguins, also called little penguins, are the smallest of the eighteen penguin species, about 40 centimeters long and about a kilogram. They live on warmer coasts in southern Australia and New Zealand. The city said Japan held 50 of them at four facilities at the end of December 2025, 26 of those at this park, and that many of the birds already in the country are close relatives. Talks with the Zoo and Aquarium Association Australasia began in 2017, and trial shipments of eggs inside Japan began in 2021. Moving eggs skipped a long trip for live birds and the quarantine that would come with them. The chicks are staying at the park for now. The news is seven birds on view from a completed trip. It is not a release to the wild, and it does not change how many fairy penguins live in Australia.
