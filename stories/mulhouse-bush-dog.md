L'Alsace reported on November 11, 2026, that a bush dog was born at Mulhouse Zoo, the first there since 2016. Anaïs Tritto, head of science and conservation, said August 31 is an estimate. The pup was born in a den the parents dug in the enclosure, and keepers first knew from squeaks. The pup put its nose outside about a month later, still retreats to the den, and has not been handled for a sex check. Tritto said the zoo used to have females give birth in a box indoors. This time the parents chose the burrow.

The male, 8, arrived from Prague Zoo in November 2024. The female arrived from Amiens in July 2025 at age 2. This is their first pup. The species, Speothos venaticus, is listed as near threatened. Tritto said that rank should be looked at again because a recent paper suggests the animals are hardly being seen and may be in worse shape than the list implies. European zoos in a breeding programme hold 240 individuals in 51 parks, a number that has grown. In the wild they live in groups with a dominant pair, mostly in forested parts of French Guiana and, to a lesser extent, Peru. The threats she named are deforestation and hunting for meat. The pup is described as fit and active. That is the news. A revised wild status is not finished.
