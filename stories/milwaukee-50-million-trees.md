Wisconsin has planted its 50 millionth tree under a pledge Gov. Tony Evers signed in 2021, Wisconsin Public Radio reported. The first state goal was 75 million trees by 2030, along with conserving forestland. Three years later the planting target rose to 100 million. The new count is the halfway mark on that larger number.

Program coordinator Bobbie Webster said the work depends on partners who get trees in the ground and teach people how to care for them. Havenwoods State Forest, a short drive from downtown Milwaukee and the only urban state forest in Wisconsin, is one of the places that received more trees. Urban forestry staff said the count matters less than planting trees where they can live for decades.
