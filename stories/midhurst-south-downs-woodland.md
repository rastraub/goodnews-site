The South Downs National Park wants landowners to propose ground for 500 hectares of new woodland, an area the park compared to 800 football pitches, across Sussex and Hampshire over the next six years. BBC News reported the plan on November 25, 2026, during National Tree Week, which runs from November 21 to 29.

Nick Heasman, head of natural capital at the park authority, said woodland is vital national infrastructure and that parks have a role against climate change, damaged soil, drought, flooding, and pollution. He also said studies link woods to physical and mental wellbeing. The authority said the new woodland would capture 100,000 tonnes of carbon dioxide within 25 years, and that the scheme, supported by Legal & General, would offset the equivalent of a year's emissions from 54,000 petrol cars. Those figures are the park's. The trees in this 500-hectare plan are not in the ground yet. Landowners still have to offer sites.

Alongside it, Trees for the Downs aims to plant almost 15,000 trees at 16 sites this winter. That campaign began in 2019. The park said more than 100,000 trees have already gone in at 150 places, including school grounds, public parks, and village greens. The news is a new ask of landowners, plus a planting programme that has already put trees in the ground. It is not 500 hectares of forest standing today.
