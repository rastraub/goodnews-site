Some of Sony's old robot dogs have a second job in hospitals. ABC News reported on November 27, 2026, that owners who have to part with an Aibo can donate it to Sony in Tokyo. The company repairs and upgrades the robots, then places them in hospitals and other facilities. Aibo first went on sale in 1999. The robots are sold out and no longer made. Sony does not say how many it sold, what they cost, or how many are in the adoption program.

The robot barks, cocks its head, moves its ears, walks toward a person, and settles as if going to sleep. It is built to remember its name, a face, and a room. Yoshiyuki Katsuno, the Sony official who oversees the program, said owners often feel they are sending off something that became like a child, and that he hopes the robots will give people a reason to talk to one another.

At a university on the outskirts of Tokyo, Dr. Manabu Komori, who treats ear, nose, and throat illness, said a nurse had to go looking for a wandering Aibo on a recent visit. He also said the robot helped with long waits and persuaded reluctant children to be examined. That clinic uses real dogs as well. Komori was careful not to say the robot was better than a live animal. The article did not name the hospital. The news is that donated Aibos are already in use. It is not a count of how many children they have helped.
