Four turtles that spent months in care at the Two Oceans Aquarium in Cape Town went back into the sea at the De Hoop Marine Protected Area on November 19. The aquarium published the account on November 26, 2026. The group was a loggerhead named Habanero, an olive ridley named Sage, and two hawksbills, Amber and Bellatrix. Sage is the first olive ridley the centre has released with an acoustic tag. The tags on Sage, Amber, and Bellatrix were given by the Southern African Polar Research Institute. Habanero's satellite tag was paid for by the Morukuru Goodwill Foundation.

Habanero washed up at Witsand in February 2025, covered in leeches and badly anaemic at 75 kilograms. A bone infection in the left elbow did not clear, and veterinarian Bernice van Huyssteen amputated the flipper in December 2025. A CT scan in September 2026 showed no infection. He left at about 100 kilograms. Sage was cut out of a ghost net at De Hoop in December 2025 by marine ranger Corné Lamprecht, who carried her for kilometres. A lung tear needed repeated care. She arrived at 9.6 kilograms and left at 15.9. Amber, a young hawksbill missing a right front flipper that had already healed, stranded at Betty's Bay in September 2026 and was eating and diving within three weeks. She left at 8.3 kilograms. Bellatrix, found weak in a tidal pool at Strand in April with a fresh shoulder wound, weighed 5 kilograms on arrival and 7.5 at release. A grade 5 class at St Cyprian's School had adopted her.

The centre timed the release to warmer water at De Hoop, a marine protected area since 1985. Amber went straight into the surf on three flippers. Sage, carried in by the same ranger who had rescued her, dived and disappeared. Habanero needed help through the first waves, then swam. A week later his satellite tag showed about 80 kilometres, mostly in coastal water, including a loop back toward the protected area. The aquarium said that is what it expected while he settled after nearly two years in care. The news is four releases and the start of a track. It is not proof that any of them will nest, or that the coast is safe.
