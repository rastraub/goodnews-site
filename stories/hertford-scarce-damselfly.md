A scarce blue-tailed damselfly has been recorded on wetlands beside the River Ash near Hertford. BBC News reported the sighting on November 1, 2026. The insect turned up at two monitoring sites close together, in late summer, along a stretch the Herts and Middlesex Wildlife Trust calls a restored chalk river.

About 7 kilometres, or 4.3 miles, at the Easneye Estate was returned to its old course last year. The trust said the work took seven years, and volunteers planted foliage around the site. The River Ash is one of about 260 chalk rivers in the world. New gravel beds are already being used by spawning brown trout, and checks before and after the restoration found more river insects, including mayflies and stoneflies.

A new British Dragonfly Society red-list review moved the scarce blue-tailed damselfly from near threatened to endangered. Eight dragonfly and damselfly species are now classed as endangered, twice the number in the 2008 assessment. The trust said that for species under pressure, making and restoring habitat is vital. The news is this sighting on a repaired river. It is not a new national population figure.
