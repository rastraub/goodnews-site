The Asia Business Daily reported on November 12, 2026, that South Korea's oceans ministry released an Indo-Pacific bottlenose dolphin named Anmogi that morning in waters near Sanbangsan in Seogwipo, on Jeju. A Yonhap photo places the release at Yongmeori Coast. The release had been set for November 10 and was delayed two days by weather. Anmogi had been seen since last year off Gangneung Port, formerly Anmok Port, swimming alone and following boats. The species is legally protected. After the dolphin kept approaching propellers and was injured, a rescue began on July 15.

Four months of treatment followed in a tank at the Jangsaengpo Whale Ecological Experience Center in Ulsan. At rescue Anmogi measured 230 centimetres and 140 kilograms. At release the dolphin was 236 centimetres and 142 kilograms, with injuries healed and feeding and swimming back to normal. Crews moved the dolphin from Ulsan by boat and let it go from an offshore pontoon. Jeju was chosen because it is habitat for the species, not because anyone knows where Anmogi was born. A satellite tag on the dorsal fin should transmit for one to three months and then fall off. The release is done. Where the dolphin goes next is what the tag is for.
