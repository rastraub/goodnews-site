A small conservation trust in County Durham has £65,000 for three years of work on some of England's rarest mountain plants. The BBC reported that the Teesdale Special Flora Research and Conservation Trust received the grant from the North Pennines National Landscape, through the Farming in Protected Landscapes programme.

Volunteers will collect seed and return it to the Upper Teesdale fells. The trust is also raising £15,000 of its own. Founder Dr Margaret Bradshaw has long studied the Teesdale Assemblage, about 20 rare arctic-alpine plants thought to have survived there since just after the last glaciation, roughly 10,000 to 12,000 years ago.

Sarah Tooze, natural heritage programme manager for the landscape, said the partnership looks forward to working with local farmers. The grant starts that work. It is not a finished restoration of the fells.
