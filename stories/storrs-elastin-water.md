University of Connecticut engineers report that water does most of the work when elastin stretches and snaps back, UConn Today wrote about a study in Nature Communications. The team used simulations. Those models found that hydration water, the water associated with the protein, accounts for most of that spring-like behavior. The researchers say the result can inform work on tissue and on biomaterials. It is a finding about how elastin works in simulation. It is not a treatment, and it was not a trial in patients.
