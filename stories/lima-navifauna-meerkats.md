A private zoo in Lima introduced two meerkat pups born in the city. The Associated Press reported the presentation on November 25, 2026. Eduardo Espíritu, the biologist in charge of mammals at NaviFauna, said the aim is to help people understand why wild animals are worth keeping wild. Meerkats are small African mammals that live in underground groups, dig their own burrows, and stand upright to watch for threats. They eat insects, small vertebrates, eggs, and roots.

Espíritu told the AP that meerkats cannot be pets. Keepers judge the group by whether the animals stay healthy, and births are one sign of that. NaviFauna has 15 meerkats. Eight have been born in Lima since 2025. The other seven arrived in 2022 from Botswana in a zoo-to-zoo exchange. The park is a private zoo authorized by Peru's forest service, and it also keeps capybaras, macaws, and flamingos. The news is two pups in a licensed collection. It is not a wild meerkat population in Peru.
