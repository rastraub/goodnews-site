BBC News reported on November 16, 2026, that Lincolnshire Wildlife Trust has the money to buy 215 acres, about 87 hectares, of grassland near Hawthorpe, between Grantham and Bourne. Public donations came to £400,000. A £1 million Biffa Award grant, from the Landfill Communities Fund, had already been put toward the purchase. Chief executive Paul Learoyd said the land had not been actively farmed for more than 40 years and called it a hidden gem for the county. Surveys have recorded ground-nesting birds, including skylark and linnet.

An appeal for the £1.4 million purchase opened a year ago after the land came up for sale. Learoyd said the trust has an agreement with the current owner, the Esmée Fairbairn Foundation, and that paperwork is still being completed. Once that is done, Hawthorpe is due to become the trust's newest reserve. The BBC also reported that the trust has objected to a proposed reservoir pipeline that could cross the site, and that Anglian Water has met the trust to discuss the route. The news is the purchase agreement. It is not a finished reserve, and it is not a decision on the pipeline.
