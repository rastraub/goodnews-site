The FDA approved Lembaz (adrabetadex) on November 17, 2026, the first treatment shown to help people with infantile-onset Niemann-Pick disease type C live longer. It is approved to reduce the risk of death in adults and children who weigh at least 7 kilograms, about 15 pounds, and whose neurological symptoms began before age 6. A trained clinician gives it as an injection into the spinal canal every two weeks.

The agency compared 36 patients treated with Lembaz, across three studies and an expanded-access program, with a matched external control group. Treated patients had a 55 percent lower risk of death. Median survival was 8.5 years in the treated group and 4.9 years in the control group. The FDA said that difference held whether or not patients were also taking miglustat. Lembaz carries a boxed warning for hearing impairment, including permanent hearing loss. Hearing should be checked before treatment and regularly after, and the dose may change based on those tests. Two earlier drugs for Niemann-Pick type C were already approved, and the FDA said neither had been shown to reduce the risk of death. These figures describe the 36 treated patients and their matched controls. They are not a promise for every person with the disease.
