Tuko reported on November 16, 2026, that the Mastercard Foundation Scholars Program has opened 2027 applications at 17 partner universities, and that Kenyan students apply through each university rather than through the foundation. At most of the schools the package covers tuition, housing, a monthly stipend, travel, and health insurance. Some add meals, books, or a laptop. Cambridge's offer, the paper said, can include a laptop, books, and medical exams, and it is limited to study tied to climate resilience. It excludes several business degrees. Applicants have to be African citizens living in Africa, show financial need, a strong academic record, and a record of community leadership or service. Universities add their own rules.

The foundation does not take the applications and does not charge a fee. Tuko said any fee a university does charge is reimbursed if the student is selected, and that anyone asking for money in the foundation's name should be treated as a fraud. Deadlines are not one date. The paper listed Sciences Po's early decision on November 23 and a final date of December 14, African Leadership University in Kigali on November 30, Carnegie Mellon University Africa on December 15 for early applicants, and Cambridge on December 5 or January 5, 2027, depending on the course. It also said UC Berkeley had more than 6,000 eligible applications for about 35 places. Those numbers are the outlet's account of the competition. A place is not guaranteed by applying.
