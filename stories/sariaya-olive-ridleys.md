The Philippine Daily Inquirer reported on November 13, 2026, that as many as 83 olive ridley hatchlings were released into Tayabas Bay off Sariaya, Quezon, on Wednesday. It was the first batch of the nesting season. They entered the water at 4:30 p.m. on the shore of Barangay Bignay 2, watched by fishermen and their families, barangay officials, and visitors. Edwin Frias, a deputized fish warden, told the Inquirer that another 100 hatchlings are scheduled for release on November 27. On November 12 his group recovered 224 freshly laid eggs on that coastline. Since September 24 they have recovered 1,650 eggs along the Sariaya coast.

Fishermen, volunteers, civic groups, and government agencies guard nests in Barangays Bignay 2 and Guisguis until the hatchlings are ready. Hawksbills and green turtles also nest on Tayabas Bay, mostly from October to December, and the eggs usually hatch in 45 to 60 days. Frias's count is the eggs in hand and this release. Father Warren Puno, of the Diocese of Lucena's ecology ministry, welcomed the release and also warned that coastal building, stationary fish traps, blast fishing, and the egg trade still kill turtles or take the beaches they need. The Inquirer's own photo of a hatchling was taken in January 2024, not at this release. The figure that is new is the 83 hatchlings and the 1,650 eggs under watch.
