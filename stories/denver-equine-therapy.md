A peer-reviewed study in the Journal of Autism and Developmental Disorders followed 72 children ages 6 to 11. Ten weeks of occupational therapy that included horses lowered cortisol, a hormone tied to stress, more than the same kind of therapy done in a clinic. Colorado State University reported the work from the Temple Grandin Equine Center at CSU Spur, which already hosts about 11,000 therapy appointments a year.

What the study showed is a lower stress response over those 10 weeks. It did not show a cure for autism, and it did not measure whether the cortisol change lasted after the sessions ended.
