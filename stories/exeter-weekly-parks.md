BBC News reported on November 12, 2026, that children taken to a park at least once a week showed better social-emotional development than those who went less often. Researchers at the University of Exeter analysed 4,219 children aged 4 to 11 in the Children of the 90s Study. Parents filled in questionnaires at five points in each child's life. The paper is in the Journal of Epidemiology and Community Health.

Mark Ferguson said a weekly visit could be a practical way to support emotional wellbeing, social skills, and mental health, and that it works fairly only if parks are safe, accessible, and welcoming. The team pointed to physical activity, play, time with a parent, and time with other children as likely routes. Paul Noblet of the charity YoungMinds said free, safe spaces help young people make friends and build confidence, and that many such places have disappeared. The finding is a link in this cohort. It is not a claim that one walk fixes a child's mental health.
