Britain's largest woodland bat survey logged 98 million calls, and the scientists who ran it say they could not have listened through that pile without computers. The BBC reported on October 30, 2026, that devices on poles in 310 woodlands recorded the calls over three years. Working through them by hand would have taken more than 30 years.

Kate Jones, of University College London, said the open-source tools changed the scale of wildlife monitoring and called the project a nice story of that technology used well. The red-listed barbastelle, one of Britain's rarest bats, showed up in the south-west, including Somerset, Devon, Cornwall, and Dorset. Lia Gilmour of the Bat Conservation Trust, which ran the work with Forest Research, called the survey unprecedented and said Somerset had strong activity.

The calls are a map of where bats were heard. They are not a promise that every wood is safe for them.
