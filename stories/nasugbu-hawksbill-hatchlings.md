One hundred forty-one hawksbill hatchlings went into the Verde Island Passage off Nasugbu on November 21, 2026. The Philippine Daily Inquirer reported the release on November 23, citing the Department of Environment and Natural Resources in Calabarzon. The eggs had hatched at a nesting site the department was monitoring. At sunset, the hatchlings were set down on the beach of a resort in Barangay Balaytigue. The department said the hour was chosen to cut the time they would be exposed to predators and other threats.

The work sits with the Community Environment and Natural Resources Office in Calaca and the Punta Fuego Village Foundation, which the department said have been cooperating on coastal care. The passage covers about 1.14 million hectares along Batangas, Marinduque, Romblon, and Mindoro. Hawksbills, Eretmochelys imbricata, are listed as critically endangered and are protected under Philippine wildlife law. The department described sea turtles as a sign of a living marine system. The news is this one sunset release of 141 hatchlings. It does not give a survival rate, and it does not say how many nests the beach holds in a year.
