A male Amur leopard cub born September 8 at the Potawatomi Zoo in South Bend is growing under round-the-clock care, WNDU reported. His mother, Anastasia, was raising a cub for the first time, and his father is Mango. The cub is not named yet. Anastasia showed some mothering. Staff and the veterinarian still chose to hand-rear him, with people on duty through the first weeks. He is healthy and putting on strength.

About 100 Amur leopards remain in the wild, in a small part of the Russian Far East, and about 200 live in zoos. Executive director Josh Sisk said the cub is part of the Species Survival Plan, and the zoo expects to place him at another zoo when he is ready. The birth does not change the wild count. It adds one animal the plan can count on.
