Froglife has been awarded £889,761 from the National Lottery Heritage Fund to restore ponds and other freshwater habitat on former colliery land, the BBC reported on November 20, 2026. In South Yorkshire the work includes Kiveton and Dinnington Community Woodlands, planted after the pits closed in the 1990s through the National Coalfields Programme. The same four-year Coalface to Wildspace project also covers sites in North and West Yorkshire. Froglife has already run versions of it in the Midlands and Scotland.

Across Yorkshire the aim is 48 new ponds, improvements to 93 existing freshwater habitats, and neighborhood wildlife corridors such as small ponds and bug hotels. Those links are meant to give insects, birds, and other animals food, shelter, and water between larger patches. Sheila Grundry of Froglife said freshwater habitat would last for frogs and other wildlife and would let local people learn skills. Project manager Andy Goff said he wants people across Yorkshire involved. The news is the grant and the plan. The ponds are not in the ground yet.
