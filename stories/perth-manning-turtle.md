A southwestern snake-necked turtle hatched in a lab and released at Manning Lake was caught again more than a year later, Phys.org reported on November 20, 2026, from work at Murdoch University. During annual surveys in October, doctoral researcher April Sturm and Anthony Santoro matched the juvenile to photographs of an animal they had incubated and released in July 2025. It weighed 4.77 grams then. It weighed 100 grams when it came back up in the net.

Sturm said juvenile turtles are rare in Perth's urban wetlands, so she checks every young one against her photos. Trackers cannot stay on a hatchling for long, which is why a recapture is the first confirmation that an artificially incubated animal of this species lasted more than a year in the wild. Santoro said many of those wetlands still have adults and almost no young to replace them, and that this turtle lives only in southwestern Australia.

The pair called the find encouraging and also a last resort. Incubation in a lab does not replace habitat, and they want more recaptures before anyone treats one survivor as a restored population. The project is supported by Saving Our Snake-Necked Turtle, the City of Cockburn, and the Ecological Society of Australia. The news is this one identified turtle, not a count of how many of its siblings are still out there.
