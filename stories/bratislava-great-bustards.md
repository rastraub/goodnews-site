A bird that stopped nesting in Slovakia for a decade is using the fields again in numbers not counted since 1973. BirdLife International reported on November 27, 2026, that Andrej Chudý and Adriana Cíbik Hološková finished a count at Sysľovské polia, a special protection area on the edge of Bratislava, and tallied 638 great bustards. The species is listed as endangered. Thirty years ago, BirdLife said, 129 birds remained along the border of Slovakia, western Hungary, and Austria. From 2006 through 2015, none bred in Slovakia.

In 2017 the Slovak partner SOS/BirdLife restored a first hectare of grassland. More than 10 percent of the protected area is now habitat the birds can use. Farmers in an agriculture ministry program grow rapeseed, alfalfa, and peas that feed and shelter the flocks in autumn and winter, with buffer strips and fallow between the crops. BirdLife said the site now holds more than three quarters of the great bustards from Austria, western Hungary, and Slovakia in winter, and that about ten females have nested there in recent years. The news is a finished count and the habitat already on the ground. It is not a promise that the birds will spread to every old site, and the group said the farm payments have to stay in place for that to have a chance.
