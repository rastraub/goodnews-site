CBC News reported on November 16, 2026, that the Thunder Bay Art Gallery is lending educators a copy of the Biinaagami Giant Floor Map of the Great Lakes-St. Lawrence watershed. The map is the centre of a project run by Canadian Geographic and Swim Drink Fish, guided by a circle of Indigenous elders and knowledge holders. Classroom copies measure about eight by six metres. A gym version is about eight by eleven metres. This loan includes a teacher's guide in French and English and lesson plans that cover treaties and wampum belts as well as the water.

Meredith Brown, Canadian Geographic's director of water and land relations, said the circulating kits were already booked for the school year, so an extra map was offered to local educators who would otherwise have gone without. Gallery educator Kimberly Warwick said an app attached to the map can bring up 11 augmented-reality stories from elders and knowledge holders. Loans last two weeks and are free. The last booking period ends February 1, 2027. A larger map is also on the gallery floor as part of an exhibition that runs until January 3. The news is the loan. It is not a new map of the lakes, and it does not add a fact about Thunder Bay's water beyond what the people in the report said.
