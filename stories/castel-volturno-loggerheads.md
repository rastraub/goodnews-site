Dozens of loggerhead hatchlings emerged at Castel Volturno in late August, were measured, and were released into the sea. Mongabay reported the hatching on November 6, 2026. The town sits about 35 kilometres northwest of Naples, on a coast where loggerheads are nesting farther west than the old rookeries in Greece, Turkey, and Cyprus. Spain's first confirmed nest was in 2001, with regular nesting from 2014. Adults reach about 200 kilograms. Hatchlings weigh about 20 grams.

A paper published in July 2026 by Sandra Hochscheid's team found that 87 percent of Campania nests in 2022 and 2023 were exposed to beachfront lighting, and that 82 percent needed people to step in so hatchlings were not drawn inland. Ascea, with Mayor Stefano Sansone, has installed dimmer seafront lights with the Anton Dohrn research station. From 2027, low-impact lighting is to be required for businesses seeking a beach concession. The news is the late-August emergence and the lights already changed in Ascea. It is not a tally of every western Mediterranean nest.
