Jamaica's head-start program for the Jamaican rock iguana is about to pass 1,000 animals returned to the Hellshire Hills. The Jamaica Observer reported on November 9, 2026, that 973 have already been released and that 114 more are scheduled to go out on Saturday, including the 1,000th. The species was declared extinct in 1948 and rediscovered in 1990. It lives only in the Hellshire Hills. Young iguanas are raised at Hope Zoo until they are large enough to have a better chance against predators.

Environment minister Matthew Samuda, speaking at the Iguana Specialist Group meeting in New Kingston, said more than 280 live traps are being watched and that more than 1,500 predators have been removed from the core habitat over time. The zoo is caring for about 250 to 300 juveniles. Tandora Grant, who has worked on the project since the 1990s, called the coming release a sign that wild animals are surviving and breeding, and she said the work is not finished. The news is the 973 already out and the Saturday plan. Those 114 iguanas are not in the forest yet.
