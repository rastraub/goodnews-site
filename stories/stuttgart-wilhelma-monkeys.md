Two South American monkeys were born in Wilhelma's Amazon house in Stuttgart. Südwest Presse reported the births on November 9, 2026: a pygmy marmoset and a black howler. The zoo said the howler arrived at the end of October and the marmoset shortly before that. Curator Volker Grün said howlers are leaf-eaters, so keepers have to offer enough fresh leaves all year. Adult pygmy marmosets are about 15 centimetres long in the body, with a tail of about 20 centimetres, and a newborn is barely the size of a thumb.

The two families raise their young differently. A howler infant stays with its mother, who carries it through the branches. Among the marmosets, the father and older siblings also carry the baby and hand it to the mother to nurse. Howler infants are pale; only adult males grow the black coat the species is named for. Both species live in the Albert Schöchle Amazon House, opened in 2000. The news is these two births. It is not a count of either species in the wild.
