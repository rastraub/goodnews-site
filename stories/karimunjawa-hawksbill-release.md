Eighty hawksbill turtles went back into the sea at Tanjung Gelam beach in Karimunjawa National Park on November 21, 2026. The Central Java provincial government reported the release the same day, with Governor Ahmad Luthfi and the park authority on the sand in Jepara Regency. Park head Widyastuti said the young turtles had come through a semi-natural hatchery. Fishers who find eggs can bring them in. Left in the nest, she said, the eggs are often taken by other animals. Once they hatch, the turtles are returned to the water.

The 80 were not one clutch of newborns. Their ages ran from about two months to four months, and some were about a year and a half old. The hatchery is also a study site, so a few animals stay longer before release. Widyastuti said hawksbills are one of seven sea turtle species the park protects. The turtles released that afternoon will travel. If they live, they have a chance to come back to Karimunjawa to nest.

The report is this one release and the reason the eggs were moved. It does not give a survival rate for the 80, and it does not say how many hawksbills nest in the park in a year.
