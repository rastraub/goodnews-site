Two Holstein Friesian steers who were not sent to slaughter have grown taller than most of the people who come to look at them. The Guardian reported on November 26, 2026, that Frank and Tiny Tim each stand 192 centimetres at the shoulder.

Frank lives with Krystal Camilleri, co-founder of the adoption group Companion Cows, on her property in Woodbridge, Tasmania. She took him six years ago from a dairy farm in the north of the state, where, like many male dairy calves, he had been headed for slaughter while still young. A veterinarian told her he was the biggest animal she had seen. Camilleri said he likes to be cuddled, falls asleep, and snores. She also said his size is hard on his joints, and that he may live only to about 10. Cattle can live to about 20. She did not claim any special feeding trick. She said he was simply allowed to grow.

About 2,900 kilometres away, in Waroona, south of Perth, farmer Eric Walmsley has raised Tiny Tim since the steer was a day old. Tiny Tim is now eight. He was too big for the abattoir, so Walmsley kept him. A trip over a weighbridge in a horse float put him at about 1,400 kilograms. Walmsley said Tiny Tim is quiet, curious, and will eat from a hand, and that he often shares the front paddock with a longhorn steer.

A Holstein named Knickers, who stood 194 centimetres, drew a crowd in Western Australia a few years ago and has since died. A Canadian Holstein named Beef is just under 196 centimetres. Frank and Tiny Tim are among the tallest steers reported in Australia. They are not the tallest living steers on record.
