A Pasifika film festival that has lasted almost 25 years held the last screening of its 24th edition in West Auckland. RNZ reported on November 26, 2026, that Pollywood Pasifika Film Fest 24 was showing its final film that night, still run on goodwill and thin resources. Organiser Craig Fasi said this year's screenings drew between 50 and 100 people. He expected the last night to feel different because the room is small and the directors can talk with the audience in an ordinary way.

Fasi told RNZ he does not chase major grants from agencies, because those grants come with expectations about how the funder is shown. He said that freedom is part of why the festival is still here, and that his proudest fact is the organisations and venues that give time and rooms. He hopes someday to add an awards pool of grants for the filmmakers. That fund does not exist yet.

One filmmaker in the program, Jason Taouma, was screening with Pollywood for the third time. His latest short, Tiki X 823, retells Pacific stories in a future Aotearoa. The news is a festival that reached a 24th closing night. It is not a claim that the films have a distributor, or that next year is funded.
