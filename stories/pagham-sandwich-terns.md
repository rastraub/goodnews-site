Sandwich terns are nesting again at Pagham Harbour in West Sussex after bird flu emptied the colony in 2022. The Guardian reported on October 19, 2026, that counters recorded 756 breeding pairs this year and 570 chicks that fledged. Nearby, black-headed gull fledglings rose from 51 in 2025 to 504 in 2026, and little terns went from 21 to 34. Eighteen avocet pairs on new islands at Ferry Pool produced 24 fledglings.

RSPB staff and volunteers put up predator fencing, managed the vegetation, and set out nesting rafts at Medmerry and West Hayling as part of the Solent Seascape project with the Blue Marine Foundation. Wez Smith of the RSPB described the return as a recovery that still needs that day-to-day care. The counts are for this stretch of coast, not a verdict on every seabird colony hit by avian flu.
