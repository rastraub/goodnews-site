A bald eagle that a driver first took for dead beside the Alaska Highway is flying again, CBC News reported on November 19, 2026. In September, John Smarch was driving just outside Beaver Creek with a friend when they passed the bird. He looked in the mirror, saw it move, and turned around. They think another vehicle had hit it. The eagle stayed with them for two days while they tried to offer rabbit and water. They then drove it to Whitehorse and handed it to the territorial environment department, which took it to the Yukon Wildlife Preserve.

Veterinarian Cori-Anne Huisman said the bird had a broken leg, a broken wing, and a broken beak. Weeks of bandages, medicine, and fluids followed. On Tuesday, Smarch opened the cage. The eagle did not move for about 30 seconds, which staff said is common, then stepped out, flapped, and flew over the trees near Fish Lake. Huisman said that habitat should help it keep healing, and that rehabilitation does not always end this way. The news is this one bird. It does not say the fractures are fully gone, only that it could fly out of the cage.
