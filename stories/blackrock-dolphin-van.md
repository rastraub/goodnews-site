A common dolphin stranded in the shallows at Blackrock was driven to a slipway where it could reach deep water. The Irish Independent reported the rescue on November 24, 2026. A member of the public called the Irish Whale and Dolphin Group at 8:43 a.m. on November 23 after seeing the animal close to shore at Priest's Beach. A local responder was there within 20 minutes. By then the dolphin was stranded. Its body condition looked good and its breathing was normal, but the waves were washing it around.

Responders moved it a short way up the beach so water would not cover the blowhole. Common dolphins are an open-water species, and the group said the wide shallows off Priest's Beach can leave them disoriented, with deeper water several kilometres away. The decision was to take it to Greenore, where a slipway opens onto a route out of Carlingford Lough. Conservation ranger Jennifer Lynch of the National Parks and Wildlife Service led the work, with Dundalk Sub Aqua Search and Recovery, a veterinarian from Dublin Zoo, the council biodiversity office, and IWDG volunteers. Councillor Marianne Butler, who had taken the group's stranding training, was among the first on the sand. Neighbors brought blankets and buckets.

When a vehicle was needed, Conor Hughes of the charity Crosscause offered a van. The dolphin went into it in a rescue sling, was kept wet on the drive, and was carried into the sea at Greenore. After a few minutes it swam off, diving for up to a minute at a time. It was last seen 2 kilometres from the release site, still headed toward open water. The group said several of the volunteers had trained in 2025 through a council course paid for by the Local Biodiversity Action Fund. It also repeated the rule for the public: call the hotline, and do not drag a dolphin by the tail or the fins. The news is one animal that swam away. It is not a count of every stranding on the coast.
