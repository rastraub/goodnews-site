The Guardian reported on November 15, 2026, that Hidde Burgmans, a 37-year-old digital designer from Baarn in the Netherlands, has built an app called Ancient Trees for people who will change their route for an old tree. Users upload favourites with photos and descriptions, and add the ones they visit to a profile. The map runs from Malaysian baobabs to the Fortingall yew in Perthshire, thought to be about 5,000 years old. Burgmans proposed to Nadia Roeleveld under a cedar in Japan. He told the paper his rule is simple: the tree should be worth a detour.

The idea started on work trips to Lisbon, where he found century-old jacarandas and wild figs and no easy way to record them. He built the app himself and keeps it to quirky finds rather than every trunk. He said that on Monday someone photographed five trees in Paris, on Tuesday a tree in Edinburgh, and that day a tree in Freiburg. Dendrologist Dan Crowley noted that the Woodland Trust already keeps an Ancient Tree Inventory for the UK, and that a global map adds reach. Hot, dry summers are stressing trees, Crowley said, and more people noticing them makes care more likely. The app is live. It is not a promise that any one tree will be saved.
