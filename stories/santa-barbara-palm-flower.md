A flower about the size of a grain of rice had been sitting unnoticed inside a museum rock for 130 years. Researchers at UC Santa Barbara used micro-CT scans on a specimen from the Dakota Formation in Kansas, held at the Smithsonian, and found a flower about 100 million years old. It shows the three-part pattern typical of monocots, the group that includes palms. The description, published in the American Journal of Botany, makes it the oldest palm flower yet found.
