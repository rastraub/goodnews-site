Some of the olive ridley eggs carried off Seal Beach and Huntington Beach in September have hatched, and the first of those hatchlings are in the Pacific, the Daily Pilot reported on November 16, 2026. The Aquarium of the Pacific had been incubating more than 200 eggs, the clutches biologists moved before storm surf could wash them out. Those nests were the first natural sea turtle nests recorded on the U.S. West Coast. On Saturday the aquarium released 54 hatchlings at Seal Beach, near where the eggs were found, after 58 days. On Sunday, 21 more went into the water at Huntington City Beach.

That is not the end of the nests. Dr. Lance Adams, the aquarium's director of veterinary services, said dozens of eggs from each nest are still in the incubation room. They may be viable and they may not, and any that emerge later would also be released. A third nest, found in Ventura County on November 1, was not viable because of cold sand and high tides. The September move is the [earlier story](/story/orange-county-olive-ridley). This one is the first release, with more eggs still waiting.
