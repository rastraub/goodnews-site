An anonymous Connecticut family gave $3 million to Connecticut Children's to expand emergency behavioral-health care, NBC Connecticut reported. The hospital said the gift will increase behavioral-health capacity by 145 percent in the emergency department at the Golisano Medical Center in Hartford. The money will help build a separate treatment space for children in a behavioral-health crisis and support the clinicians who care for them.

President and chief executive Shannon Sullivan said a shortage of psychiatric beds and treatment sites often leaves patients waiting days for a placement. She said the gift cannot solve that shortage. What it can do is give children privacy, safety, dignity, and specialized care while they are in the emergency department. A family representative said watching children deal with anxiety, from medical conditions, classmates, and social media, showed them that mental-health distress often starts before adulthood. The hospital also said it now serves about twice as many patients a year as it was designed to hold. Sullivan asked the state to match the emergency-room improvement with care children can reach after they leave. The gift is the space and the staffing support. It is not a new supply of inpatient beds.
