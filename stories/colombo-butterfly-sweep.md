Mongabay reported on November 16, 2026, that clouds of yellow and white butterflies moved through Colombo in the first week of November, and that watchers saw the same flight in other parts of Sri Lanka. Lakshman Kumara, secretary of the Butterfly Conservation and Research Group, said the seasonal peaks usually come in October to November and again in February to March, and that 2026's movement was especially large. He said this year's insects seemed to avoid the hill country, possibly because of the weather.

Professor Kapila Dissanayake of Sabaragamuwa University told Mongabay the flight looked dominated by the lemon emigrant, with smaller numbers of common albatross and Sri Lankan lesser albatross. He asked that it be called a mass movement, not a proven migration, until direction and extent are checked. Himesh Dilruwan Jayasinghe, of the National Institute of Fundamental Studies, linked the boom to monsoon rain and fresh growth on host plants such as cassia and senna. A separate global study of 1,758 butterfly species, led by Shawan Chowdhury, found range shifts often tied to climate, and Sri Lankan researcher Tharindu Ranasinghe said highland endemics could be squeezed upslope. That study is not a count of this week's flight. The news is the butterflies people saw. It is not a finished route map.
