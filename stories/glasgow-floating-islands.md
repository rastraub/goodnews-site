Floating islands are going into ponds in five of Glasgow's parks, planted by people who already use those parks. BBC News reported the work on November 26, 2026, and updated the story the next day. The rafts are a legacy project of the 2026 Commonwealth Games. Together they add almost 250 square metres of habitat, and they are expected to last about 20 years.

The islands are being installed at Victoria Park, Elder Park, Richmond Park, Queen's Park, and Hogganfield Loch. They are made by Biomatrix Water Solutions in Forres, a company that has floated similar rafts in Boston, Venice, and London, and on the Clyde at the Glasgow Science Centre in a separate project. Buoyancy comes from recycled plastic, including drinking-water pipes, held with marine-grade stainless steel and topped with a light clay aggregate. The plants are native, a mix of evergreens and flowers such as marsh marigolds, water forget-me-nots, and ragged robin. Roots under the rafts shelter aquatic life, and the middle of the pond gives birds a nesting spot away from the bank.

Ellen Hebdon, the company's operations manager, said the roots help limit harmful algae, and that clearer water comes over time, not overnight. Local schools and friends-of-the-park groups are doing the planting and the upkeep. Selina Donald, head of sustainability for Glasgow 2026, said the council had wanted the islands and did not have the money until the Games project stepped in. The news is habitat going into five ponds. It is not a claim that the water is already clean.
