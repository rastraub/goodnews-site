Wildlife watchers recorded Fisher's estuarine moth 111 times in 2026 at Abbotts Hall in Great Wigborough, near Colchester. BBC News reported the count on November 22. Dr Zoe Ringwood, senior conservation evidence manager at Essex Wildlife Trust, called the rise an important success and a result of action based on evidence. The moth is pale gold-brown with light and dark spots. It lives in low grassland that floods and erodes, and it depends on hog's fennel, a rare plant that rising sea levels have damaged.

The trust planted hog's fennel at Abbotts Hall in 2005 and introduced moth eggs five years later. More than 100 of the insects were recorded for the first time in 2025, and 2026 passed that mark. The charity said it is confident the reserve now holds a self-sustaining population, and it plans to keep surveying new and existing plots on the Essex coast. Hog's fennel has also gone in at reserves in Fingringhoe, Wrabness, and Tollesbury. The figure is sightings at this site. It is not a claim that 111 separate moths were handled, and it does not say the species is safe everywhere it used to live.
