Staff at Bradford Royal Infirmary spent weeks feeding a stray cat family that was living under a fenced generator outside the maternity block, then caught the father and two kittens. BBC News reported the rescue on November 25, 2026. The mother had given birth beneath the power unit. At first nobody could reach the animals, so people from several departments took turns watching them.

Melissa Barker, a senior sister in the neonatal unit, said a few people putting out food grew into a shared mission, with a busy message group and feeding rotas. Cathy Hulin, who works in the hospital's research department and volunteers one day a week at Allerton Cat Rescue, led the catching. She said the mother disappeared when the kittens were about three weeks old, and colleagues kept feeding them for another three weeks. The kittens were fast. The team scooped one, trapped the father, Burt, and then coaxed the second kitten into a carrier.

Hulin is fostering the kittens. Burt went to a foster home in Hebden Bridge. The kittens are Holly and a second kitten whose name, Ivor or Ivy, waits until the rescue can tell its sex. They already have a future home lined up. Burt and the mother, if she is caught, will be neutered before they are rehomed. The mother is still out. Chief executive Mel Pickup said the effort pulled in people from the neonatal unit, research, informatics, education, theatres, and a ward. The news is three cats in care and one still being looked for. It is not a claim that every stray around the hospital has a home.
