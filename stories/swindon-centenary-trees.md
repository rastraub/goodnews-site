Students at The Commonweal School in Swindon planted 100 trees on the playing field to mark a century since the school opened. BBC News reported the planting on November 7, 2026. The school first took students in 1927. The Centenary Woodland mixes species, including some chosen because they may cope with a warmer climate. Each tree will be numbered, and the name of the student who planted it will be recorded on a plaque. Head teacher Charles Drew said the digging was meant to teach conservation outside the classroom, and that a big hole is harder work than it looks.

The Wiltshire Wildlife Trust provided the trees, advice, and equipment. Swindon Borough Council backed the work through its Trees for Climate grant. The planting sits inside the Great Western Community Forest, which covers almost 170 square miles. Year 10 students Mason and Nysa spoke at the event as school ambassadors, and the vice-chancellor of the University of Bristol planted a tree of her own. The 100 trees are this field. They are not a new total for the wider forest.
