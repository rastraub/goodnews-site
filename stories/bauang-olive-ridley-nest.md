A walk on the beach before dawn put 110 olive ridley eggs into a hatchery in Bauang, La Union. The Philippine Star, citing Agence France-Presse, reported on October 25, 2026, that Steve Llabaya saw turtle tracks around 5 a.m. on Saturday, October 24, in Barangay Pudoc and called Barangay Captain Edgar Beninsig. The municipality and the local environment office helped move the nest so it could be watched until the eggs hatch.

The paper said olive ridley eggs take about 60 days, so hatchlings from this nest may emerge around December 24. It is the province's second nest of the season. On October 13 a patroller found the first, with 114 eggs, in Ili Sur, San Juan, and those hatchlings are expected around December 13.

Coastal Underwater Resource Management Actions, which works with local partners on La Union's nesting beaches, said the group has seen more than 54,000 olive ridley hatchlings released since 2010. Executive director Carlos Tamayo asked beachgoers to keep the shore clean, quiet, and dark at night, and to report tracks instead of digging into a nest.
