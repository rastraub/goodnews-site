Researchers at Vanderbilt, in Nashville, and at Johns Hopkins built an open-source tool called AURA into electronic health records, Johns Hopkins Engineering for Professionals reported. The study is published in NEJM AI. In a two-year randomized trial of about 1,960 patients, people whose clinicians received the alert were diagnosed in a median of 43 days, compared with 138 days without it.

That is a shorter wait for a diagnosis, more than cut in half on the median. It is not a treatment and not a cure. The alert flags patients who may be at high risk for autoimmune disease so testing can start sooner. The 43 and 138 figures are medians in this trial, not a promise for every patient.
