Ninety larvae of the anchor-marked tiger beetle were placed in the sand at Taean Coast National Park on October 29. The Asia Business Daily reported the release on November 2, 2026, quoting Korea Zinc. The company worked with the National Institute of Ecology, the Korea National Park Service, and local citizen scientists. The beetle, known in Korean as datmunui-gilapjabi, is listed as Class I endangered wildlife. It lives mainly on sandy beaches, and coastal development has shrunk that habitat.

The larvae were bred in care and released in late October because that is when they burrow and overwinter. The spot was a sandy stretch with little foot traffic, chosen so the larvae would be less disturbed while they settled. They are expected to stay underground through the winter and emerge later as adults. The count is these 90 larvae. It is not a survey of how many beetles already use the park.
