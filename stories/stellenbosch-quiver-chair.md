Wendy Foden will lead a five-year programme on how to protect wildlife as habitats get hotter and drier. Business Day reported on November 11, 2026, that she has been awarded the inaugural Oppenheimer Research Chair in Conservation in a Changing Climate. She is a climate specialist at South African National Parks and an extraordinary professor at Stellenbosch University's School for Climate Studies. She also chairs the climate change specialist group of the International Union for Conservation of Nature's Species Survival Commission.

The work will focus on the Richtersveld in the Northern Cape, including quiver trees and halfmens, desert succulents already under pressure from heat and scarce water. Foden said the award gives her team room to develop and test new approaches, and that stronger evidence is needed before conservationists can be sure which responses work. The team will look at existing methods, including assisted translocation, which means moving a species to a place that may suit it better, and will examine the risks and limits of those steps. A collaboration with the University of Cambridge's Conservation Evidence group is meant to sort which measures have already shown promise. The chair was set up by the Benjamin Raymond Oppenheimer Trust through Oppenheimer Generations Research and Conservation. The programme will also train postgraduate researchers. Nothing in the announcement is a result from the field yet. It is the start of the work.
