A municipal wildlife reserve now covers about 715,000 acres along the Iténez River, the long water border between Bolivia and Brazil. Good News Network reported on October 27, 2026, that the Agua Dulce reserve was created in local law, with the Bolivian group Faunagua leading and Rainforest Trust supporting the work. Rainforest Trust's own October announcement placed the reserve in Baures.

The report describes jaguars, Amazon river dolphins, giant otters, lowland tapirs, and black caimans, and about 260 fish species, 25 of them found nowhere else. The river runs roughly 780 miles. The new reserve sits among protected areas on both banks, including Noel Kempff Mercado National Park and Iténez Departmental Park on the Bolivian side and Corumbiara State Park and the Pedras Negras extractive reserve on the Brazilian side.

Faunagua executive director Rosmery Ayala Lozada said the outside support helped local and municipal agreement, the legal designation, and the management papers. The rules rest on three aims: wildlife, the services the forest and floodplain provide, and sustainable use for the local economy. Faunagua wrote the management plan with the communities who live with the river. The designation is a legal corridor. It is not, by itself, proof that the animals are safer this season than last.
