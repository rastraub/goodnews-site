Eleven African penguins have arrived at Uchi Zoo in Gwangju. Seoul Economic Daily reported on November 12, 2026, that the birds recently moved from Seoul Children's Grand Park and have started adjusting. The city said keepers are monitoring their health and behaviour. The zoo plans to overhaul the enclosure and the way the birds are cared for so the space matches their needs. That rebuild is a plan, not a finished habitat.

African penguins, also called jackass penguins, live along warm stretches of the southern African coast. Adults grow to about 70 centimetres and weigh about 3 kilograms. In 2024 the International Union for Conservation of Nature listed the species as critically endangered. A population once numbered in the millions of pairs has shrunk by about 97 percent over the past century. The birds are also on Appendix II of the convention that regulates wildlife trade. The paper cited a shortage of prey from overfishing and climate change as the main drivers, and said experts warn the species could disappear from the wild within about a decade without stronger protection. Seong Chang-min, head of the Uchi Park Management Office, said he hoped visitors would notice that risk, not only the birds' appearance. The news is a move between zoos and a plan for their care. It is not a release to the ocean.
