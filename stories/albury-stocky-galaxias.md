Stocky galaxias bred at Charles Sturt University are living in Snowy Mountains streams, the university reported on November 20, 2026. The small alpine fish had been known from only two wild populations. Scientists at the Gulbali Institute in Albury spawned them in captivity for the first time, in a program funded by Snowy Hydro, managed by the NSW Department of Primary Industries and Regional Development, and run at the institute.

Executive director Lee Baumgartner said nobody had bred the species in tanks before. Researcher Amina Price said the aim is several secure populations, so a fire, an invasive species, or some other shock at one creek does not take the whole fish with it. Senior fisheries manager Luke Pearce named the long-term goal as self-sustaining groups in alpine waterways beyond Upper Tantangara Creek and Sallys Flat Creek, the two known wild sites.

The newest field news is narrower than a recovered species. Crews put about 250 juvenile fish into target streams, the largest release so far, and recapture surveys found 10 healthy adults from an earlier release. Those 10 show that fish from the Albury tanks can grow up in the wild. They are not a census of every stocky galaxias now in the mountains.
