BBC News reported on November 13, 2026, that 370 acres on the edge of Cambridge are back with the National Trust for the first time since the 1960s. The land, called Northfield, runs along Quy Road toward Bottisham, about seven miles from the city and close to Anglesey Abbey. An agreement with the tenants brought it back. It includes a chalk stream on what the Trust calls a fen-edge landscape, where low fen meets a higher chalk ridge, and there are traces of people using the site for thousands of years, including in the Roman period.

Emma Ormond-Bones, general manager of the Trust's north Cambridgeshire portfolio, said the aim is a place where nature can thrive and people can reach green space. The Trust hopes harvest mice, water voles, and turtle doves will benefit over time. Northfield is the first project in a wider plan tied to the Wicken Fen Vision, linking Wicken Fen to the edge of Cambridge. Drop-in sessions run from November 21 through November 27, and proposals are to be developed during 2027. The land has changed hands. The habitats are still ahead.
