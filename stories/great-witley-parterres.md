Archaeologists, gardeners, and volunteers are tracing lost Victorian flower beds at Witley Court in Great Witley. BBC News reported the dig on November 16, 2026. William Andrews Nesfield laid out the gardens between about 1850 and 1870, with embroidery-like beds around the Perseus and Andromeda fountain, meant to be admired from the terraces above. A summer heatwave left parch marks that drones photographed, including one bed about 6 metres wide and 18 metres long. A one-week excavation is checking those outlines against old plans, looking for foundations and changes in the soil.

The team has already uncovered buried bases for tazzas, the large urns that held flowers, and a piece of patterned bath stone that may show how big the urns were. Head gardener Mark Pardey said the elaborate planting is a fading skill, and that the aim is to bring more of Nesfield's landscape back for visitors. Planting in that style is due to start next year. The house burned in 1937 and was later taken on by English Heritage, which restored parts of the parterres and the fountain about 20 years ago. The news this week is the dig and the urn bases. The beds are not replanted yet.
