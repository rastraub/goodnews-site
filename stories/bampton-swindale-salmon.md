BBC News reported on November 14, 2026, that Atlantic salmon fry have more than doubled in two years at three survey sites on a restored stretch of Swindale Beck in the Lake District. The beck was straightened in the 1800s to make more farmland, and the faster water scoured away the gravel salmon need for eggs. In 2016 the RSPB and United Utilities put the bends back into about a kilometre of channel. Further work downstream let other sections meet their floodplain again.

Environment Agency fisheries specialist Philip Ramsden said this year's surveys found significantly more fry at the three sites on that section, and a scattering of parr, the next stage of young salmon. He said the young fish are coming from redds, the nests adults can now dig in the restored gravel, not only drifting in from elsewhere. The same surveys recorded brown trout, lamprey, and eels, and the wetland holds snipe, kingfishers, and goosander. The BBC did not publish an absolute fry count. The news is the more-than-doubled comparison on this stretch. It is not a recovery figure for Atlantic salmon everywhere.
