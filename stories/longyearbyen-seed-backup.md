The Margot Forde Genebank in Palmerston North has placed another 300 seed samples in the Svalbard Global Seed Vault. RNZ reported the deposit on November 23, 2026, ten years after New Zealand's first shipment. The vault, in the permafrost of a Norwegian Arctic mountain, holds more than 1.4 million samples from around the world as copies of crops people rely on.

Director Kioumars Ghamkhar said this year's box includes grasses grown for more than 25 years in air enriched with carbon dioxide, a stand-in for a harder future atmosphere. He said the plants adapted by ordinary change over time, not by genetic modification, and that the seeds are there in case of a disaster. The gene bank chooses what to send by what matters to New Zealand's farm heritage and what might matter more widely as the climate shifts. Ryegrass and white clover are among the core species. So far it has shipped close to 3,500 samples, dried and sealed in double-layered aluminium pouches. The long-term aim is about 18,000 samples, roughly a tenth of the collection. Ghamkhar said the team has a role and rejected the idea that the country's food future sits in their hands alone. The news is this deposit. It is not that larger goal, and it does not say the grasses are ready as new farm varieties.
