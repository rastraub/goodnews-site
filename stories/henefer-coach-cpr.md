Eight-year-old Tyce Gardner was gathering baseballs at his older brother's practice in Henefer when a throw during a drill hit him in the chest. He collapsed with no pulse and was not breathing, KSL.com reported on October 7, 2026.

Head coach Gene Richins started CPR immediately while Tyce's father, Bryce, called 911 and a dispatcher helped count compressions. Crews from the new Henefer fire station, about half a mile away, arrived in about three minutes and restored his heartbeat with a defibrillator within six minutes. Tyce was flown to Primary Children's Hospital and went home after two days. Tests are still under way, but doctors believe he will fully recover.

The North Summit Fire District honored Richins at Henefer Town Hall. It was his first time doing CPR, though he had taken classes. "It will take a few hours of your day," he said. "And it will pay off sometime in your life."
