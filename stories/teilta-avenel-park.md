ABC News reported on November 18, 2026, that about 15 traditional owners returned to Avenel Mount Westwood, a reserve of four former stations in western New South Wales, to help decide how it will be run as a national park. The combined area is 121,000 hectares. The state bought the stations in 2021 after about 150 years in private hands. The land is Malyangapa and Wilyakali country. Representatives of both groups sit on the Avenel Mt Westwood Aboriginal Co-management Committee with National Parks and Wildlife Service staff. The park is not gazetted yet. ABC said that step is what comes next.

Mike Gilby, a Malyangapa, Barkandji, and Wangkumara man in his late 50s, told ABC it was his first time on the former station, and that the private lease had kept the family off it. He said the decisions at the table are meant for younger people: jobs, culture camps, and time on country, alongside the neighbours who still run stock next door. The reserve holds 38 threatened plant and animal species. Grinding plates, stones, and digging sticks have been found, and Gilby said those will be managed with the committee. Station manager Makhalia Girdler, who lives on the reserve, described the daily work as weeding, fixing infrastructure, and keeping pig numbers down. She said she would like university students back for geology and ecology. None of that is a finished park. It is the first planning visit the report describes.
