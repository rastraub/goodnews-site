Two captive-bred male great Indian bustards were released on October 4, 2026, at Sam in Desert National Park, near Jaisalmer. The Times of India reported that the birds, about seven months old and fitted with satellite tags, flew after the covers came off their eyes. The Indian Express put the wild population at about 150 birds. This was the first release of captive-bred males from India's bustard recovery program.

The Press Information Bureau said the centers at Sam and Ramdevra then held 97 birds, 40 from wild-laid eggs and 57 bred in captivity, with 11 marked for release. Captive pairs produced 24 live chicks in 2025 and 29 in 2026. A separate "jumpstart" method, placing captive-laid eggs in wild nests, has hatched six chicks in Rajasthan and two in Gujarat. About 200 square kilometers of fenced grassland inside the park are being kept as breeding habitat. The two males are a pilot. Officials said their movements will be used to adjust the rules for later releases, not as proof that the wild population has recovered.
