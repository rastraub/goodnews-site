Scientists have named five new species of tiny horned frogs from Arunachal Pradesh, in India's eastern Himalayas, more than doubling the known species in the group. BBC News reported the work from a paper published on November 11, 2026, in Royal Society Open Science. A team from the University of Delhi and Harvard University identified the frogs as Jingophrys after five years of fieldwork. One of them, Jingophrys tanogogo, eats snails. It is the first frog known in India with that diet, and the first member of its family recorded eating snails. Sonali Garg, who led the study at Harvard's Museum of Comparative Zoology, said large evolutionary changes can go unnoticed in little-studied animals. The team also included James Hanken, S. D. Biju, Tage Tajo, and Radhakrishna Upadhyaya K.

Biju said some of the new species are extremely secretive and turn up in remote forests only during a short breeding season. Researchers said more work is needed on their biology and on what conservation they need. DNA work showed the horned frogs split into multiple species in the eastern Himalayas as the mountains rose and the Asian monsoon took shape. The researchers said local tribes gave essential field support, and some names honor those communities. Jingophrys taniitatii, the Tanii horned frog, joins the name of the Tanii people with tatti, the word for frog in Apatani. The other new names are Jingophrys siangensis, Jingophrys hrdy, and Jingophrys wularsoni. The paper describes animals that were already in those forests. It does not say how many individuals of each species remain.
