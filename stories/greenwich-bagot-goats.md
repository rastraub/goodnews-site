A small herd of Bagot goats is grazing a steep bank in Greenwich Park, BBC News reported on November 24, 2026. The Royal Parks brought the animals in for five days to clear holly, brambles, and thorny stems on Castle Hill Bank, below the Royal Observatory. The slope is hard to reach with machinery or by hand. Visitors can watch them between 11:00 and 15:30 GMT through Friday.

The trial is conservation grazing: animals hold back the plants that would otherwise take over, which leaves room for other growth and for insects, birds, and small mammals. The Royal Parks is working with Mudchute Park and Farm, on the Isle of Dogs, and the Rare Breeds Survival Trust, with funding from People's Postcode Lottery players. The charity calls the Bagot Britain's oldest native goat breed. The animals are small and light-footed, which suits uneven ground.

Park manager Clare Lanes said the view up to Flamsteed House is part of what makes the park a Unesco site, and that keeping the bank from closing that view has been a real problem. Mudchute farm manager Tom Davis said the work also gives a rare breed a job. The herd has a paddock, a lean-to, and fresh water, and it is walked on and off the site each day. The news is this five-day trial. It does not mean the goats will stay in the park.
