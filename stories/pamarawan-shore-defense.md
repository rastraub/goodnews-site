Residents of Pamarawan, a low fishing village on North Manila Bay, told Mongabay the flooding they used to live with has stopped since a shore project went in. The October 30, 2026, report said the work skipped a concrete wall and used bamboo poles, discarded oyster shells, and coconut husks.

Bamboo cribs in the water are meant to catch sediment so the bed can rise enough, in time, for mangroves to be planted. Shells packed in cocomesh bags are there to help oyster reefs recover and give the shore another layer. Mongabay described the pilot as the first nature-based coastal protection project of its kind in the Philippines, and as a model other flood-prone villages might study.

The claim that flooding has eased is what residents reported after the structures were built. The mangrove planting is the hoped-for next stage, not a finished forest.
