Fiordland National Park is now the largest International Dark Sky Park in the southern hemisphere. RNZ reported the accreditation on November 12, 2026. The park covers 12,607 square kilometres, which also makes it the world's third-largest park of this kind, and close to five percent of New Zealand's land. It is the second recognised dark sky place in Southland. Stewart Island Rakiura was accredited in 2019.

DarkSky International's places manager, Amber Harrison, said the scale of the darkness is part of what makes the landscape exceptional, and that protecting the night sky extends the protection already given to the land. She also pointed to Māori knowledge of the sky, including navigation, seasonal cycles, and Matariki. Great South said the title followed years of work with the Department of Conservation: monitoring sky quality, checking and improving public lighting, and teaching visitors why natural darkness matters. Te Anau operations manager John Lucas said many native species rely on natural light cycles, and that lighting can stay where safety needs it. Biodiversity ranger Glen Greaves said the Milky Way is so bright there that meters can register it as light. The work is not finished. Monitoring, light management, and interpretation continue. The news is the accreditation. It does not switch off every light in the region.
