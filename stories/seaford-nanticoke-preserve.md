Conservation partners gathered Friday in Seaford to mark the permanent protection of 181 acres of forest and wetlands along the Nanticoke River and Wright Creek, land that crosses the Delaware-Maryland line, WBOC reported. The effort took more than 15 years. Maryland Department of Natural Resources Secretary Josh Kurtz called it the first time the two states have worked together on a land acquisition.

The tract includes Atlantic white cedar, a tree Delaware's DNREC is trying to conserve. Kurtz said the Nanticoke matters in Maryland because Atlantic sturgeon reproduce in the state only in Marshyhope Creek, which feeds the river. The fish lay eggs in gravel that extra sediment can smother. Keeping the land forested holds that soil in place, even though the spawning beds are miles downstream. Wetlands on the property also filter water on its way toward the Chesapeake Bay.
