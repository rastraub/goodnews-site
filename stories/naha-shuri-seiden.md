The restored Seiden, the main hall of Shuri Castle in Naha, opened to the public on Monday, seven years after fire destroyed it. The Japan Times reported the opening on November 23, 2026. The Hoshinmon gate to the square opened at noon. Visitors needed a reservation to go inside the hall. A homemaker in her 70s from the Shuri district said she had been looking forward to the day and that the interior colors felt like the kingdom era. Satoshi Tachioka, 57, from Osaka, said he and his wife had visited before the fire and during the repairs.

The hall is a three-story wooden building where the Ryukyu king conducted affairs and ceremonies. Each of the first two floors has an Usasuka throne, and the second floor has golden dragon pillars. Nonoka Sakita, 9, came from Urasoe with her family and said she is studying the castle at school. Before the public hours, the Okinawa prefectural government held an event in the square, and high school students performed court dances.

The fire, in the early hours of October 31, 2019, also destroyed the north and south halls. The reconstruction uses research done since a 1992 restoration so the hall follows the older appearance more closely, and fire safety has been strengthened. The news is this opening day. It does not say the rest of the castle grounds are finished.
