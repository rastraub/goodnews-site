Leticia Peh, 22, spends her final year at the National University of Singapore on a double major in psychology and philosophy, and on volunteering she can put her hands to. The Straits Times reported on November 22, 2026, that she has genetic hearing loss. She cannot hear higher-pitched sounds such as consonants, hearing aids overstimulate her, and she lip-reads. She was diagnosed in Primary 6 after she failed a listening test. Classmates had thought she was rude when she did not answer. Three younger siblings inherited the same condition. Her parents later divorced, her father had stretches without work, and she stopped taking an allowance in secondary school so she could save for university. She also helped care for her youngest sister, who has Down syndrome.

At NUS she learned Singapore Sign Language with SIGNapse and asked to be matched, through a ComLink+ befriending course, with a deaf family in a rental flat. She interpreted for them and their family coach, who had been writing notes that did not always land. When her signing felt too thin, she went to deaf church services and interned at the Singapore Association for the Deaf. One day the family walked into that office, looking for the help she had described. She told the paper that volunteering shifted her attention from what she lacked to what she could give. In October she received the Asia Pacific Breweries Foundation Scholarship for Persons with Disabilities, administered by SPD, which covers her final-year tuition. She hopes to become a clinical psychologist for deaf people. The news is this student and this family. It does not say the scholarship is open to every student who needs it.
