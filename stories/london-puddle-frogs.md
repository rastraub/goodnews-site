Fifteen froglets about five millimeters long are the first Afia Birago's puddle frogs known to be born in captivity. BBC News reported on October 15, 2026, that the births happened at London Zoo. Phrynobatrachus afiabirago was described in 2018 and lives in just two pools in Ghana's Atewa Hills, where mining threatens the forest. A full-grown adult weighs less than 1.75 grams. The zoo received 20 adults in September 2025 and does not know of another zoo that keeps the species.

The frog is named for Afia Birago, the mother of Ghanaian researcher Caleb Ofori-Boateng, who described it. Keepers including Unnar Aevarsson, and conservation lead Ben Tapley, are sharing the breeding methods with the CSIR-Forestry Research Institute of Ghana in Kumasi. The aim is a population in Ghana that could later be released. That release has not happened. The births follow a first captive breeding of the related Atewa slippery frog in November 2025. Fifteen froglets are a start in a tank, not a recovered wild pool.
