A howler monkey described as a new species in October is already walking rope bridges over roads in Alta Floresta. National Geographic reported on November 9, 2026, that Alouatta matogrossensis turned up in camera-trap photos from crossings built by Projeto Reconecta. Fernanda Abra, who leads the project, said the team was amazed to be giving a new species a safe way across. Romina Batista of the National Institute of Amazonian Research called the images important for gene flow between patches of forest.

Howler calls top 100 decibels and carry for miles. Genetic samples suggest this population has been separate for more than half a million years, cut off by rivers and by the Cerrado savanna. The same paper names a second howler, Alouatta iroi, farther north, and puts the howler count at 16. The bridges also carry the Alta Floresta titi, described in 2019, and Schneider's marmoset, described in 2021. The new howler is not yet on the IUCN list. Jean Boubli of the University of Salford said the damaged forest around it makes him think the species is endangered or critically endangered, which is his reading, not a formal listing. The news is the bridge photos. It is not a census.
