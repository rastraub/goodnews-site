RTÉ reported on November 17, 2026, that Dublin Zoo and Fota Wildlife Park have just over €480,000 for a four-year project, running until 2029, to breed Ireland's white-clawed crayfish with the National Parks and Wildlife Service. Minister of State Christopher O'Sullivan announced the funding at Dublin Zoo. The crayfish, Ireland's only native species, can reach about 12 centimetres and live up to 10 years. It feeds on other invertebrates and is treated as a sign of how a river or lake is doing. Crayfish plague, invasive signal crayfish, and pollution have destroyed populations across much of Europe.

Fota director Aileen Tennant said the park rescued the last known white-clawed crayfish in the River Blackwater catchment from the Awbeg River, and that its aquarists have since bred the species in a purpose-built facility. This year alone they reared more than 150 young. She said Fota is the first conservation organisation in Ireland to do so, and that the hope is those animals will one day start populations in rivers free of plague. Dublin Zoo's Andrew Mooney and NPWS nature conservation director Suzanne Nally described the project as secure breeding plus later recovery work. The news is the funding and the 150-plus young already reared. It is not a release into a river.
