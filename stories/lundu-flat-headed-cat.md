Mongabay reported on November 16, 2026, that a camera survey in southwestern Sarawak recorded the endangered flat-headed cat in a mangrove proposed as the Lundu Mangrove Protected Forest. Researchers watched the site from August 2021 to May 2024. The article says the cameras caught the cat 59 times. Lead author Ahmad Ampeng, of the Forest Department Sarawak, told Mongabay that 48 of 58 habitat records came from nibong palm wetland, nine from buta-buta, and one from nipah. Those palms stand about 10 to 20 metres above sea level, with freshwater puddles. The cats mostly skipped the tidal swamp at sea level.

The cameras also showed the cats moving in daylight as well as at night, especially in the nibong, which is more flexible than the old idea that they are strictly nocturnal. One sequence showed an adult female with a kitten. Ahmad called that a sign of possible breeding in the study area. The cat made up 0.8 percent of 7,426 wildlife records, so the team is not estimating how many live across Borneo. Ahmad said the findings are a reason to gazette the proposed reserve. The forest is not protected yet. The news is these photographs, including a kitten. It is not a population count.
