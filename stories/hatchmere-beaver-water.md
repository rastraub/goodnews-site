BBC News reported on November 5, 2026, that a University of Liverpool study found beavers at Hatchmere in Cheshire kept a wetland wet through drought. Author Melanie Baker based the work on the Cheshire Wildlife Trust enclosure. She said that in the drought of July 2022 the enclosure stayed wet, water kept leaving it into a site of special scientific interest, and the peat and the plants that depend on it held on. Foxes and wading birds use that water too.

A male and a female arrived in 2020 and have produced seven kits. They have felled trees and turned dry ground into ponds. Gareth Longden of the trust said the pond they built also soaks into the peat underneath, which stores carbon and keeps water in the landscape. He said the site stayed wet through a dry year, and that beavers can also flood places people would rather keep dry or fell a tree that matters for other wildlife. He said the benefits outweigh those problems when people work with the animals.

Baker noted that England's early projects, including this one, were fenced trials, and that licensed wild releases are a separate government decision. The Cheshire project was the tenth reintroduction scheme in the country. The news is what the Hatchmere enclosure did in the 2022 drought, and the seven kits born there since 2020. It is not a new release of beavers outside the fence.
