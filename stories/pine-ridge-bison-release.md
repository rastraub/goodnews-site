Close to 300 people gathered in the badlands to watch Thunder Valley Community Development Corporation release 50 bison, Prairie Public reported. The herd is now on nearly 40,000 acres leased from the Oglala Sioux Tribe in the Stronghold, one of the largest range units on the Pine Ridge Reservation.

The ground was once a U.S. military bombing range. Thunder Valley installed about 27 miles of buffalo fencing plus a corral, and it worked with ordnance teams so the pasture was clear of unexploded bombs. The range then rested for two years while crews measured grasses, flowers, and soil. Many of the people at the release were students. Board chair Beau LeBeaux said the group wants young people to treat the animals as relatives. The nonprofit also plans hands-on herd work for its workforce program, so students can learn ranching skills South Dakota still uses.
