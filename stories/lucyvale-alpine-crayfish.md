Scientists are carrying endangered alpine spiny crayfish back to a stream near Lucyvale after keeping them through the months when the water was not fit to live in. ABC News reported on November 7, 2026, that the team had gone in after January bushfires, including the Walwa–River Road fire that burned about 121,000 hectares, and pulled 11 crayfish from a stream where debris and sediment could smother them.

Will Sowersby, a senior scientist at the Arthur Rylah Institute, told ABC that crayfish stuck in small pockets of habitat can disappear from those places if they are not rescued. The animals went to the Snobs Creek conservation hatchery, run by the Victorian Fisheries Authority near Eildon. Dylan White, a senior conservation hatchery officer, said it was the first time the species had been kept in captivity. They fight, so each one had to be housed alone.

ABC described the scientists traveling with a group that is heading back to the home waterway, and said some of the rescued crayfish are now returning as the country greens again. The story does not give a number for the animals in the containers on this trip, so the checked count is the 11 that were saved, not a claim that every one of them went back on the same day. Staff hope the species might later be bred at the hatchery. That breeding program is a hope. The news is the rescue and the return now underway.
