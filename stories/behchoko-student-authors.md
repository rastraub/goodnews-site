Two high school students in Behchokǫ̀ are published authors of children's books. CBC News reported the launch on November 12, 2026. Chief Jimmy Bruneau School hosted it on Tuesday for Chace Mantla, in Grade 11, and Leora Rabesca, in Grade 12. They were among six students chosen for a workshop run with Northern Quill Publishing. From the start of the manuscripts to publication took three years. At the launch they signed copies in a school hallway, with friends, family, and faculty around them.

Mantla said he started after watching his younger sister read, and reading to her. His book, A Dog's Adventure, is about a dog named Jack and is based on his own dog. Rabesca's book, Different, follows a girl named Kiki who struggles to make friends because others see her as different. She said it came from her own childhood, when she did not have many friends. Both authors said the hardest part was working with illustrators so the pictures matched what they had imagined. Literacy coordinator Keri Peach, of the Tłı̨chǫ Community Services Agency, said she watched their confidence grow, and that Rabesca wanted other children to feel they did not have to be the same as everyone else. Mantla told other students that with motivation, tools, and the right people, writing a book is worth it. The news is two finished books and a school launch. It is not a count of how many copies have sold.
