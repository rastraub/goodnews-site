ABC News reported on November 18, 2026, that Christmas Island's red crabs have started the annual walk from the forest to the sea. About 100 million crabs make the trip to mate and spawn. The first rain of the wet season is the signal. Islanders spend weeks steering around them. Pharmacist Grace Orr-Young told ABC she slows down and tries not to squash crabs, and that a passenger with a rake can clear the ones in front of the wheels. She said she often stays in town during the migration.

Parks Australia is livestreaming the walk this year, a first for the event. National park program manager Mark Bennett said the crabs time the journey so they release eggs before dawn on a falling high tide in the moon's last quarter. A crab bridge and shared raking already help, and some roads close, which makes parts of the island hard to reach. Lund University researchers Marie Dacke and Eric Warrant, visiting to study navigation, described the movement as a red river through the forest. Warrant said the compass could be the sun, the Earth's magnetic field, or polarised light, and that younger crabs might learn the route from older ones. Those are still questions. The news is that the march is underway, and that the island is organised around it.
