On November 10, an 88-year-old hiker became separated from her companions at Mount Burdell Preserve, near Novato, and headed downhill off the trail, Outside reported. Her group could not find her and called for help. About 50 members of Marin County Search and Rescue turned out. Molly Williams, the team's operations director, said the call came in around 2 p.m. and the woman was found around 4 p.m.

Cody Davis, a 17-year-old volunteer and high school senior, was among the first volunteers at the command post. Dispatchers had already tried to place her by phone and could not. The hills and trees did not give him a landmark he could use, and the mapping tools did not show her either. He stayed on a video call with her for about an hour and a half, through dropped calls, and asked her to show him the ground around her. When that still was not enough, he used a video-call feature that, with her permission, let him open the map and compass on her phone and read the coordinates to teams in the field.

Williams said the searchers would have found her eventually, and that Davis's persistence made it faster. By the time he got the call back, a team was walking up to her. She was not hurt. Rescuers helped her back to the trail, and Williams said she walked out with them in good spirits. Davis's advice, as Outside reported it, was to stay on the trail instead of heading downhill in search of a road. Williams said the woman had a phone that still worked, had service, and answered when rescuers called.
