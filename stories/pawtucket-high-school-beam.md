Pawtucket marked the last beam on a new high school rising where McCoy Stadium once stood, NBC 10 WJAR reported. On Tuesday, community leaders gathered at the site. The final beams were signed before they were set. Mayor Donald Grabien said the milestone was bigger than a piece of steel. He called it an investment in the city's future, and said a modern school tells students they matter and gives them tools to succeed. He described the old ballpark as a place the community used to gather, and the school as a way to honor that by investing in the next families. Governor Dan McKee, who was also there, said the high school is a big deal for the city and the state.

The plan is to combine Shea and Tolman, so students from the two high schools become classmates. Superintendent Randy Buck said the building is a point of pride and that the history of the separate schools will be carried forward rather than dropped. Last year, city leaders said they expect the school to welcome students in 2029. The ceremony is a topping-off. It is not an opening day.
