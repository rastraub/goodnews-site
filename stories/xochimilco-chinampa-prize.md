Mongabay reported on November 18, 2026, that Lucio Usobiaga won the Basque Culinary World Prize for Arca Tierra, a network that links chinampa farmers with restaurants in Mexico City. The prize, started in 2016 by the Basque government and the Basque Culinary Center, awards €100,000, about $110,000, to a project the winner chooses. Chinampas are artificial garden islands in the wetlands of Xochimilco, older than the Aztec city. Farmers still grow flowers, maize, legumes, and vegetables on them.

Arca Tierra cultivates about 6 hectares, roughly 15 acres, and works with 50 families across Xochimilco, Puebla, Hidalgo, and the State of Mexico. Those farms supply about 60 Mexico City restaurants. Usobiaga told Mongabay the farmers have lived with the chinampas for generations and that the islands filter water back to the aquifer, hold carbon, and keep a culture going, even as urbanization and abandonment eat the system away. The canals are the last remaining habitat for wild axolotls. He said the group has helped develop biofilters that keep predators out and clean the water so axolotls can live there, and that the farming they promote does not use toxic chemicals. The news is the prize and that farming network. It is not a measurement of how many chinampas were saved this month.
