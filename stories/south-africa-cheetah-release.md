Twelve cheetahs born in captivity were released into seven fenced game reserves in South Africa, fitted with GPS collars, and followed for two years. The Wildlife Society reported the results on October 21, 2026, from a study in Conservation Science and Practice. Three of the twelve died. The others survived. Every female reproduced within four months of release. Together they produced 36 cubs and, from that next generation, 19 grandcubs.

The reserves already had wild cheetahs. The fences mean this is not a release into open, unfenced range. What the study shows is narrower, and still useful: captive-born adults can live alongside wild cheetahs on managed reserves and can raise young there. The three deaths are part of the result, not a footnote to skip. The cubs born in the United States and shown at a Virginia conservation center are a different litter, and they are not these animals.
