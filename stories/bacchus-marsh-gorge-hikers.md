Eleven hikers who were caught by rising water in Lerderderg Gorge are out, and none of them was hurt. ABC News reported on November 28, 2026, that Victoria Police said the group was in the gorge when the water stopped them from hiking out, at about 10:30 a.m. They had set off while significant rain was already falling across the state. The gorge sits in the 44,000-hectare Wombat-Lerderderg National Park, a popular walk near Bacchus Marsh.

A Victoria Police helicopter was called in to help find them. Crews then guided the hikers to safety along a high ridge line. The State Emergency Service unit at Bacchus Marsh asked people to stay out of the Werribee and Lerderderg gorges until the floodwater drops. Police said no one was injured. The news is a finished rescue on the day of the call. It does not say how long the ridge walk took, or when the trails will reopen.
