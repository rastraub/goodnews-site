A volunteer who would not let a barn clear-out throw away the Peak District's ranger papers has a team sorting them at last. BBC News reported on November 7, 2026, that Sheila McHale, who has worked with the national park for about 30 years, grabbed photographs, maps, and reports from bin bags in a field at Edale about eight years ago. The bags were being disposed of on a wet Saturday. She said the papers included reports by full-time area rangers back to the 1960s and notes from weekend patrols.

The pile lived in her shed, then a loft, then an airing cupboard after she moved. In June 2024 she and other volunteers began working through it, and the collection found a corner at Aldern House, the park's headquarters in Bakewell. McHale said a core team of four, including a history graduate, is cataloguing it, with a bit of funding only for storage boxes. People have added training logbooks, uniform, and written memories. Two small displays have already gone up.

The Peak District was designated on April 17, 1951, the first national park in the United Kingdom, and the first ranger service was based at Edale, also a mountain-rescue post and the start of the Pennine Way. Ranger team manager Hayden Bridgeman told the BBC the archive helps later generations see how the landscape was looked after. The next step the volunteers want is a digitised archive people can open online. That site is not built yet. The news is the rescue of the papers and the cataloguing underway.
