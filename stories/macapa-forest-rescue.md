Janarino Ferreira dos Santos, 74, was found alive on November 20 after 11 days in forest north of Macapá. g1 reported on November 22 how trackers read the signs he left. He has Alzheimer's disease and high blood pressure, and he is blind in one eye. He disappeared on November 10 near Ramal da Mércia, at kilometre 12 of the BR-210, after he walked into the vegetation with his daughter and two dogs nearby. She followed and could not find him. Family and neighbors searched that evening and called the fire department, which joined on November 11. The aerial tactical group flew with a thermal camera.

Park ranger Nerivan da Silva and woodsman Aniel Cardoso, with the Xibante de Selva team and the park guard, found him near Ilha Redonda, about 10 kilometres from the last sighting. He was sitting, conscious, and very weak. Cardoso said the first step was water, and that dos Santos drank nearly a litre at once. The way out had no ordinary trail. Crews built a stretcher and carried him through flooded ground, buffalo pasture, fences, and closed forest to a point where the rescue could finish. He went to the Hospital de Emergências de Macapá.

The tracks explained the 11 days. Searchers found buriti palm fruit with human bite marks, and footprints beside them. Da Silva found two beds of açaí leaves, one several days old and one newer, and footprints that circled the same ground. A strong smell of urine near the last bed, matching a health detail the family had given, put the team within metres of him. The news is this rescue. It does not say when he will leave the hospital, or how fully he will recover.
