BBC News reported on November 6, 2026, that a keeper at Wildwood Trust, near Canterbury, found crown-tipped coral fungus growing on a fallen log by the rabbit enclosure. Heritage officer Steve Kirk said the fungus, also called candelabra coral, went 116 years without a confirmed British sighting before it turned up again in Suffolk in 2012. It is still rarely recorded, though there have been further finds in Hampshire, Leicestershire, and Dorset. Kirk said more of them seem to have appeared since about 2022, and that warmer summers plus a wet autumn may suit them. That is his reading of recent records, not a finished study of the cause.

The visible part is the fruiting body. Kirk said the rest of the organism runs through the dead wood, and that the little crowns are what push out, a bit like apples on a tree. Visitors can see the log near the rabbits. Staff have put up signs and a barrier. The fruiting body will decay. The park hopes the fungus fruits there again next year. The news is this log at Wildwood. It is not a map of how common the fungus is across Kent.
