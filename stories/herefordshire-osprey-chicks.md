A pair of ospreys raised three chicks to fledging in Herefordshire, in what local conservationists describe as the county's first breeding in at least about 200 years. The BBC reported on October 10, 2026, that the pair settled in the county in 2025 and returned this year. The nest was in an oak near the River Wye, close to the Powys border. Toby Fountain, nature recovery officer at Herefordshire Wildlife Trust, said the birds are thought to have come from the Welsh population. Britain has about 300 breeding pairs, and Wales about 20. Ospreys are amber-listed, and the nest site is not being published.

The adults are thought to have left for western Africa, a journey of about 3,000 miles. Photographers Victor Talmaciu and Jip Darke saw fishing ospreys at Bodenham Lake, including in early September, where crows harassed them. That lake is a stopover. It is not the nest.
