Every autumn, Corvo, the smallest and most northerly island in Portugal's Azores, switches off its public lights for five nights. The Guardian reported on October 25, 2026, that the island's roughly 450 residents have kept up that blackout since 2019 so fledgling Cory's shearwaters can leave the nest and fly toward the ocean by the stars.

The young birds spend about three months in the nest before that first flight. Streetlights can draw them into the narrow streets of Vila do Corvo instead. Neighbors began walking the village at night to pick up grounded birds in 1991. In 1995 the Azorean government widened the rescue into the SOS Cagarro campaign on the other islands, and the Portuguese Society for the Study of Birds later joined the work. Corvo is still the only island that turns public lighting off entirely. People do not have to darken their houses, and many choose to anyway.

Birds that are found spend the night in cardboard boxes, then are released the next day from a cliff close to the ocean. The campaign says more than 30,000 fledglings have been rescued across the Azores since 2015, about 6,000 of them on Corvo.
