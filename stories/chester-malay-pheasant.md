Two Malay peacock-pheasant chicks are thriving at Chester Zoo, and the Chester Standard reported on November 12, 2026, that they are the only chicks of that endangered species known to have hatched in a European zoo this year. The European Association of Zoos and Aquaria records just 15 of the birds in European zoos. As few as 2,500 may remain in the wild, where forest is being cleared for farms.

Lead bird keeper Leanne Lowton said not every parenting skill is instinctive, and the mother was not sitting on her first egg. Keepers incubated that egg, then gave it to a broody bronze-tailed peacock-pheasant, a closely related species and an experienced mother, so the chick would learn to be a pheasant rather than grow up only around people. That chick is now a healthy young male and is due to move into his own habitat this week. The foster arrangement also let the Malay peacock-pheasant lay a second egg, which she incubated herself. She left the nest too early, and the hatchling was dangerously cold. Lowton made a tiny hot-water bottle from a rubber glove and gave the chick glucose syrup every hour for a day and a night. The next morning the mother accepted the chick at once. Lowton said she had never been so relieved. The chicks are an insurance population in human care. They are not birds returned to the forest.
