A koala joey is recovering on North Stradbroke Island instead of being sent to the mainland for good, ABC News reported on November 23, 2026. Males competing to mate had separated her from her mother. Wildlife volunteers took her to the island's only vet clinic. Thalita Calvi said the joey was exhausted, slept as soon as she had a teddy to hold, and two weeks later was putting on weight. Calvi hopes to return her to the wild near the place she came from.

That was not possible a year ago. Injured koalas left for mainland care and were not allowed back, because of the chance they would carry disease onto the island. Jade Patterson, who owns Straddie Vet and founded the Minjerribah Wildlife Veterinary and Conservation Foundation, said koalas released outside their home range often do poorly. The reason is not settled. It may be the eucalypts they eat, or the microbes in their gut, and young males are territorial if they are dropped into someone else's patch.

Quandamooka elder Auntie Mary Burgess said it had been painful not to know what happened to koalas that left country. She said Patterson has already released two babies that mothers had abandoned. The clinic still runs on volunteer time, and Patterson has held a carer workshop so more people can look after animals under supervision and release them when they are ready. The joey in the story is still in care. The two releases are the animals already back in the bush.
