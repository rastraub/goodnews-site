MESHA reported on November 16, 2026, that 14 African universities in nine countries have launched a free, open curriculum on neglected and underutilised crops, the foods the project calls forgotten. Partners include Makerere University, the University of Nairobi, the University of Ghana, Jomo Kenyatta University of Agriculture and Technology, Egerton University, the University of Pretoria, and the University of Zimbabwe, among others. RUFORUM led the work with the Global Forum on Agricultural Innovation and Research, with European Commission funding. Joanna Kane-Potaka of GFAiR said the materials were co-designed rather than written by one institution.

A pretest from October 5 to October 31, 2026, drew 392 enrolments. Of the 96 people who started, 45 earned a certificate, about 47 percent of starters. The course has seven blocks, from foundations and agronomy to post-harvest work, business, nutrition, climate, and policy. It can sit inside a degree or stand alone, and it is aimed at undergraduates, master's and PhD students, teaching assistants, and extension workers. The news is the published curriculum and that pretest. It is not a count of farms that have switched crops.
