The Food and Drug Administration on November 18, 2026, approved Infilbray (venglustat) tablets, the first treatment for the neurological symptoms of Type 3 Gaucher disease in adults and in children who weigh at least 15 kilograms, about 33 pounds. The agency said the pill crosses the blood-brain barrier and works as a glucosylceramide synthase inhibitor, slowing the buildup of fatty substances the body cannot break down. Type 3 disease, unlike type 1, affects the brain and can worsen balance, speech, memory, and thinking. The approval went to Genzyme Corporation, a Sanofi company, after orphan-drug designation and priority review.

The evidence is a randomized, double-blind study of 43 patients age 12 and older who were already stable on enzyme replacement therapy. For 52 weeks they took Infilbray once a day or continued intravenous enzyme therapy every two weeks. At week 52, the Infilbray group did better on a score that combined a coordination measure and a thinking measure. The FDA did not report a survival difference. The most common side effects were gastrointestinal symptoms and effects on mood. Serious psychiatric risks, including suicidal thoughts and behavior, have been reported. Animal studies flagged possible clouding of vision in younger patients and harm to a fetus if the drug is taken during pregnancy. The label has the full risk list.
