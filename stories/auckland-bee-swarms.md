Auckland beekeepers are fielding a record run of calls to collect honeybee swarms after a milder than usual winter. The Guardian reported on November 10, 2026, that the Auckland Beekeepers Association is taking five to ten requests a day, compared with fewer than five in past years. Swarm coordinator Bryan Lang said the team has handled at least 100 call-outs. In October a swarm covered the back of a parked car in Birkenhead for about two hours. A month earlier, one took over a sign at a busy Queen Street intersection and pedestrians walked around it.

Swarming is ordinary in spring and early summer: part of a colony that has outgrown its hive leaves to find a new home, and the mass rests while scouts search. Lang said a beekeeper would rather keep those bees, because a swarm can take about two-thirds of a hive just when it should be making honey. Association president Ken Brown said a swarm is usually at its most placid, even if thousands of bees look intimidating in town. Earth Sciences NZ said 2026 was the country's third-warmest winter. Brown said milder weather can mean a larger brood and earlier swarms if the colony survives varroa mite. Entomologist Phil Lester of Victoria University of Wellington said beekeepers are also catching swarms in Wellington, and that unmanaged swarms can carry mites to other honeybees. He also called the numbers a likely sign of a good bee year. The news is the call-outs and that reading. It is not a promise that every hive will make a big honey crop.
