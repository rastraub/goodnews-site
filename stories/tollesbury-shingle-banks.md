Dredged sediment from Harwich Harbour is being placed on shingle banks in the Blackwater Estuary, at Cobmarsh Island off West Mersea and at Old Hall Point near Tollesbury. The BBC reported on October 14, 2026, that the banks are nesting habitat for little terns, ringed plovers, and oystercatchers. RSPB senior project manager James Dunn and Jim Warner, harbour engineer at Harwich Haven Authority, described the work, which began in October and costs about £2.5 million. It is funded by the Environment Agency. In winter the estuary also holds wildfowl, including wigeon, teal, pintail, and mallard.

The RSPB said less than 1 percent of dredged sediment is reused nationally. Similar placement was done on the Stour in February and March and on the Blackwater early in 2025. The news is that the material is going onto these banks. It is not a report that birds have already nested on the new shingle.
