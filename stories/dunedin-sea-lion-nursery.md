RNZ reported on November 17, 2026, that New Zealand sea lions are treating a beachside plant nursery in Dunedin as a place to give birth. Nicole Bezemer, manager of the Tomahawk Smaills BeachCare Trust nursery, told Checkpoint that females move off the sand and into the dunes looking for spots hidden from males. The nursery, right at Smaills Beach, has become one of those spots during breeding season.

Bezemer said a sea lion named Tektite, about to give birth that morning, was tucked behind a volunteer shelter for at least an hour before anyone noticed. The animals are not bursting through fences. They climb. A stock fence about 1.2 metres high, even with barbed wire, is not enough, she said, and the nursery is looking at a treated-timber fence about 1.6 metres high. A damaged pest fence is also letting rabbits in to eat native plants. The news is the pups at this nursery, including Tektite's. It is not a census of Otago's sea lions.
