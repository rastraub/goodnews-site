Garden City University in Kumasi has launched a program to help senior high school students with Ghana's revised curriculum. The Daily Graphic reported the GCU Pathways Programme on November 11, 2026. The university is adding its lecturers, practical sessions, and laboratory tours alongside what teachers already do. Vice-Chancellor Eric K. Ofori tied the work to inclusive tertiary education. Director of Admissions Shannan Akosua Magee said the goal is not to replace classroom teachers. Schools tell the university where help is needed, and lecturers bring practical teaching so students can see how a subject connects to university programs and later work.

A pilot at Prempeh College reached about 138 business students in accounting and business management, with the university's business faculty leading the sessions. The program is set to extend to five more schools in the Ashanti Region. Opoku Ware Senior High School will focus on health sciences and business. Bosomtwe STEM Girls' Senior High School will cover ICT, information science, and Python programming. Ejisu Secondary Technical School is lined up for business, ICT, and information science. Kumasi Wesley Girls' Senior High School is expected to get ICT, information science, Python, and digital skills. Serwaa Nyarko Senior High School is the fifth school. The news is a pilot already taught at one school and a list of five schools still to come. It is not a result on exam scores.
