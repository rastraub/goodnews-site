Volunteer medics returned a stranded male common dolphin to the sea from a remote stretch of the Gower Peninsula, BBC News reported on November 23, 2026. Callers reached British Divers Marine Life Rescue, and the team, with the National Trust and HM Coastguard Rhossili, found the dolphin on his right side on November 21.

They set him upright. BDMLR said a stranded whale or dolphin can crush its own breathing and circulation when the water is no longer holding its weight. Medics kept his eyes and skin wet with a watering can and sheltered him from sun and wind. After a veterinary check, and once he showed he could support himself, they eased off and let him try. When he was ready to swim, they moved him on a tarpaulin, formed a line, and splashed to send him toward deeper water. He disappeared from view.

The charity thanked the people who reported him quickly and asked the public not to push a stranded dolphin back in on their own. Some animals need treatment first, and some are too ill to survive a rushed return. The news is this rescue and the swim away from shore. It is not a later sighting of the same dolphin.
