China's mangrove forests more than doubled between 1998 and 2020, and more bird species were recorded in them, Mongabay reported on November 23, 2026, from a paper in The Innovation by researchers at Ningbo University. Mangrove cover grew from 10,617 hectares to 28,194 hectares. China had lost nearly 70 percent of its mangroves between 1950 and 1990. Later policy set reserves aside and restored degraded sites, including former aquaculture ponds. The authors also say warmer temperatures and more rain favored the trees.

Recorded bird species in those habitats rose from 148 to 397. Globally threatened species on that list rose from 11 to 21. The paper ties the bird records to mangrove area and to better conditions for fish and other food. Guangdong, including Hong Kong and Macao, and Guangxi accounted for most of the new area, together more than 88 percent of the national gain. Hainan added 1,678 hectares. Fujian and Zhejiang added much less.

The counts run through 2020. They are species recorded in the mangrove zone, not a census that every population increased by the same share. Mongabay noted a limit: sandpipers and plovers feed on open mud, and both may become scarcer as the forest grows denser. A separate global assessment has also found mangroves expanding since about 2010, including forest that grew without anyone planting a tree. The China result is the mapped area and the recorded bird list. It is not a claim that every shorebird benefited.
