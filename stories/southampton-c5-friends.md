Two friends in Southampton have each spent more than £1,000 putting an original Sinclair C5 back on the road, BBC News reported on November 24, 2026. Clive Sinclair launched the electric tricycle in 1985 as a new way to get around. About 14,000 were built and about 5,000 sold. Buyers found the range short, the speed low, and the hills difficult.

Will Murphy, 30, a cybersecurity engineer, told the BBC he does not use his C5 as daily transport. He likes the conversations it starts when he rides around the city. People remember the machine and come over to talk, which he said does not happen with a luxury car. He and Toby Penter call it both objectively terrible and really good fun. Murphy said modern batteries have fixed some of what was wrong, and that the vehicle still feels ahead of its time to a lot of people. It remains legal on the road in the same broad way as an electric bicycle, if the rider stays in cycle lanes and is sensible.

Penter has written a technical paper on lighting the old vehicle so it can be seen. Murphy's own C5 had sat in a seller's garage since it was new. The news is these two riders and the chats they get. It is not a revival of the 1985 sales plan.
