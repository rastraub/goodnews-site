A project that has been putting native oysters back into the Firth of Forth has a national research prize for work already in the water. Heriot-Watt University said on November 27, 2026, that Restoration Forth won Research Project of the Year in science at the Times Higher Education Awards. Since 2022 it has returned more than 60,000 European flat oysters and recorded some of the highest survival rates the partners have seen. At their peak, the university said, the firth's beds yielded up to 30 million oysters a year. By the start of the twentieth century, overfishing had wiped them out locally. Oysters filter water and build habitat for other animals.

Professor Bill Sanderson, who leads the science, accepted the award on Thursday evening with Professor Teresa Fernandes. He credited volunteers and partners including WWF and the Marine Conservation Society. The university said more than 700 volunteers joined 37 events, gathering habitat data that went into models of where oysters can live. Deputy principal Chris Turney said the work has brought native oysters back to the firth for the first time in more than 100 years. The next phase is a hatchery at North Berwick, run by Balanced Horizons with the university's help, aimed at a much larger number. That larger number is an ambition. The news is the oysters already returned, the volunteer counts, and the prize.
