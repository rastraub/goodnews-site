A North Atlantic right whale calf that missed the winter count was photographed with its mother near Anticosti Island, Quebec, on September 25, 2026. NOAA Fisheries said researchers from the Mingan Island Cetacean Study made the pictures, and scientists who keep the photo catalog recognized the mother as Silt, catalog number 1817. She is at least 38 years old. This is her sixth known calf.

Silt had been seen briefly on the southeastern U.S. calving grounds in February 2026. NOAA said she may have given birth there and left before survey teams documented her with a calf, and that the pair also was not recorded along the East Coast through spring and summer. The birthplace and the exact date are not known. What is known is the September sighting and the identification that followed.

The calf brings the 2025–2026 season to 24 calves. NOAA said researchers had identified 24 calves this season. Other reports of the same identification, including the New England Aquarium's November 2 announcement, described the new calf as the one that moved the tally from 23 to 24, the highest total since 2009 and the fourth-highest on record. The species is still critically endangered, with a population of about 380 animals, and fishing gear and ships remain dangers. The news is this calf, found months after the official season ended, not a claim that those dangers are gone.
