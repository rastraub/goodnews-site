The first year of New Mexico's LeakTracer program may have saved an estimated 327 million gallons of drinking water, the Santa Fe New Mexican reported. The Environment Department launched it this year for rural systems too small to fund the repairs on their own. A satellite from ASTERRA looks for pockets of moisture as far as 10 feet down. Local crews then hunt for the pipe.

As of September the program had identified 216 leaks across 28 utilities. Left alone, those leaks were estimated at 898,000 gallons a day. Truth or Consequences confirmed 23, the most of any participating system. Jonas Armstrong, who directs the department's Water Protection Division, said the total left him speechless. This is the first year of a four-year, $4 million contract paid through the state's Rural Infrastructure Program. About 562 water systems could qualify before the contract ends in 2029, and officials want the search to go statewide.
