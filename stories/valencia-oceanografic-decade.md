El País reported on November 18, 2026, that Fundación Oceanogràfic marked ten years with a gathering in the aquarium's Mediterranean area. Since it was set up in 2016, the foundation has run 182 research and conservation projects, published more than 300 scientific papers, and carried out 560 education and outreach actions. It has studied 168 species, cared for more than 1,000 sea turtles, and reintroduced more than 1,500 animals. More than 200 companies and institutions have worked with it.

Scientific director Daniel García-Párraga told the paper the result he considers most important is the finding that sea turtles trapped in fishing nets can suffer decompression sickness, the injury divers know, and that this may explain deaths that had been underestimated. The foundation has also studied how beluga calves sound like their mothers, and how ship noise on newer Arctic routes might get in the way of that call, and it has worked on ways to breed animals that are hard to breed in human care. García-Párraga said the aquarium's job is not only a visit. The transfer of what keepers and vets learn to the coast is the point he named. The turtle total and the reintroductions are counts from these ten years. They are not a stocktake of the Mediterranean.
