Salthaven Wildlife Rehabilitation and Education Centre in London, Ontario, has four new feeding puppets for young raptors, shaped like a great horned owl, a merlin, a red-tailed hawk, and an American kestrel. Phys.org reported the project on November 12, 2026, from the University of Western Ontario. Caregivers can offer food through the printed beak while their hands stay hidden, and they can play recordings of parent calls. Founder Brian Salt said birds learn by watching their own kind, and that a puppet takes the human out of feeding. He said he believes that raises the chance a released bird will cope. That is his judgment from years of care. The puppets are for next baby season, so this story is not a measured change in survival.

Joel Sierra, a visiting undergraduate from Lima, Peru, designed and printed the heads on feedback from Salt. A local artist painted the heads and canvas sleeves. Salthaven kept the digital files so it can print more. The partnership started with student-designed leg splints for injured Canada geese and mallards, which the centre can now reprint on a donated printer. Before the new puppets, staff wore reflective masks and gowns, and an older owl puppet made them reach around the face to offer food. Professor Ryan Willing supervised the engineering teams.
