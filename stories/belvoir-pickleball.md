A-level students in south Belfast are spending Friday with the pensioners next door, and the game in the middle of it is pickleball. BBC News reported on November 25, 2026, that pupils from Breda Academy, beside the Belvoir estate, go to the estate's pensioners' club each week. They make lunch, play, and stay to talk.

Anna, 18, told the BBC she looks forward to it. Older members talk about jobs they have had and about university. Deborah, 17, said the students help cook and clean, and that some of the pensioners are fierce once a game starts. They have also been decorating the hall for Christmas, and the help does not stop on Friday. Linda Woods, a regular pickleball player, called the club a bridge: older people get to hear what younger ones are living through, and the students get the longer view.

Alison Breadon, family support worker at the Belvoir Community Association, said the teenagers often volunteer for other events, including the Friday bingo. The news is a weekly visit that both sides asked to keep. It is not a new sports league.
