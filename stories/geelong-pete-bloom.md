ABC News reported on November 13, 2026, that a corpse flower named Pete at Geelong Botanic Gardens is about to open. He stands 1.5 metres tall in a climate-controlled greenhouse, and staff expect the flower within a few days. A titan arum, the plant's other name, usually blooms for one or two days every 7 to 10 years. Lucy Griffiths said the smell has been compared to a dead possum, rotting cheese, or fermenting cabbage. The point of the stink, and of the deep red colour, is to draw flies and beetles that lay eggs on rotting flesh.

Pete stores energy in an underground corm. Griffiths weighed it at just under 10 kilograms in 2024 and at 17.7 kilograms in May 2026, which told her a flower was likely. He came out of dormancy last month and has been growing about 5 centimetres a day. The gardens hold four of the plants. Two were donated in 2021 from the Botanic Gardens of South Australia. The city's last flowering, in 2024, drew more than 20,000 visitors and more than 350,000 livestream views. This one is not open yet. The news is that Pete is close.
