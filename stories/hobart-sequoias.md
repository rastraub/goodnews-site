ABC News reported on November 14, 2026, that St David's Park in Hobart marked 100 years as a public park by planting two young giant sequoias. The trees are 5 to 7 years old. Dozens of people came to a garden party on Friday, with a band in the Victorian rotunda, and watched one of the sequoias go into the ground.

The park's previous pair were 88 years old. They had been planted in 1937 and were removed in April after fire, vandalism, and a decline the council could not reverse. Sean Black, the city's open-spaces manager, said the new trees sit in a different spot so more sun reaches the lawn, and that they will take 15 to 20 years to start maturing. The council has had more than 60 requests to reuse timber from the old trunks, including for seats, instruments, and research. In the wild, giant sequoias can live for thousands of years. These two are a new start in a park that opened in 1926.
