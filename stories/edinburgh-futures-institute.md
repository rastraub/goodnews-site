A Victorian hospital that sat empty after the health service left it is now a place where students work, and it has just been named the best building in Scotland. BBC News reported on November 27, 2026, that the University of Edinburgh's remaking of the Old Royal Infirmary won the Andrew Doolan Best Building in Scotland Award, given by the Royal Incorporation of Architects in Scotland. Architects Bennetts Associates are credited with the design.

The NHS left the Category A-listed building in 2003. The university bought it in 2015 and opened it as the Edinburgh Futures Institute. The old wards and the long corridors, about 150 metres, are still there. New rooms and pavilions are woven in. Teaching, shared work, and project space spread over five floors, with digital maker rooms and places for exhibitions. Judges described a building that shows its past plainly. An outdoor pool pavilion in Macduff and Paisley's central library were also on this year's shortlist.

The prize is for the restoration and for a building back in daily use. It is not a measure of how many students have finished a course there.
