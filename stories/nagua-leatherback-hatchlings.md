Patrol teams on the Dominican Republic's northeast coast counted 843 leatherback hatchlings into the sea in the 2026 season, and nest raiding on the beaches they watch fell to 7.7 percent. Hoy reported on November 3, 2026, that AERODOM, which operates Presidente Juan Bosch International Airport at El Catey, and the Ecological Foundation Bahía Príncipe signed an agreement to continue the work through 2030. Cyril Girot and Isabel Piñero signed for the two groups. The beaches in the program include Nagua, La Majagua in Sánchez, and Cosón in Las Terrenas, plus the shore near the airport. The nest corral is in Los Yayales.

Hoy said the 2026 figures, reported by the organizations, were 26 leatherback nests protected, a poaching rate of 7.7 percent, and 843 hatchlings released. In 2025 the team switched from patrols every two or three days to a daily watch and doubled the coast under protection from 15 kilometers to 32. That year poaching fell from 95 percent in 2024 to 29.6 percent, with 19 nests protected, 1,535 eggs recovered, and 461 hatchlings released. The paper also said 180 students from five schools and 50 people from coastal communities took part in education in 2025.

Leatherbacks are listed as critically endangered. The news is this season's nest and hatchling counts, and a patrol agreement with a date on it. It is the organizations' tally for these beaches, not a population estimate for the species.
