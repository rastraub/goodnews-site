Mongabay reported on November 16, 2026, that researchers have named an extinct bird from a single foot bone excavated at Liang Bua cave on Flores. Hanneke Meijer, a paleontologist at the University Museum of Bergen, told Mongabay it is the first fossil megapode from Indonesia. The species, Megapodius rokusi, is estimated at about 3.9 kilograms, which the paper treats as the heaviest member of that genus described so far. The only megapode still on Flores, the orange-footed scrubfowl, weighs about 0.5 to 1.2 kilograms. The bone came from cave layers dated between about 190,000 and 120,000 years ago.

The name honors the late Indonesian zooarchaeologist Rokus Due Awe, who analyzed animal fossils at Liang Bua and was the first researcher to identify the remains later called Homo floresiensis. Meijer said it is the first megapode species named for an Indonesian scientist, and the first fossil megapode described from Wallacea, the islands between Southeast Asia and Australia. She also said one foot bone leaves open whether the bird could fly, when it vanished, and whether it overlapped with the scrubfowl alive today. The news is that bone and that name. It is not a full skeleton.
