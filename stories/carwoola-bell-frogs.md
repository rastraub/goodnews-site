University of Canberra researchers released green and golden bell frogs at Carwoola, New South Wales, on the morning of November 17, 2026. The university said this was the species' last upland stronghold, and that the last known animals there were seen in 2021. Chytrid fungus has driven declines in hundreds of frog species. The Carwoola work, led by Simon Clulow and Richard Duncan of the university with Ben Scheele of the Australian National University, will use lab immunization, frog saunas, and salted satellite ponds. A $200,000 grant from the NSW Environmental Trust's research program is paying for the project, with Symbio Zoo and the Mulloon Institute as partners. The release note does not say how many frogs went into the water.

Postdoctoral researcher Milad Esmaeilbeigi said his work supports the idea that slightly elevated metals from the old Captain's Flat mine upstream inhibit the fungus without harming the frogs. That is a hypothesis about why frogs lasted here, not a result measured on today's release. Clulow also said frogs released in the Australian Capital Territory in February, where the species had been gone since the 1980s, survived their first winter and are entering breeding season. Survival through a winter is a milestone for those ACT frogs. It is not yet news from Carwoola.
