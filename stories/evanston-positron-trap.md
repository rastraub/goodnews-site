Northwestern physicists have held a single positron, the antimatter twin of an electron, for months, the university reported. Gerald Gabrielse's team captured the fast particle from the radioactive decay of sodium-22, slowed it, and cooled it to nearly absolute zero. Electric and magnetic fields then suspend it in a near-perfect vacuum so it never touches the container. A touch of ordinary matter would make it vanish. Gabrielse said the setup is not dangerous, because the lab does not have enough antimatter to make an explosion.

The group calls the controlled state a one-positron quantum cyclotron. The study, published in Physical Review Letters, does not finish the comparison with ordinary electrons. It puts one positron under the kind of control that could make that symmetry test more precise than earlier ones.
