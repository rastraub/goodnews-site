More than 400 Coptic Christians read manuscripts that are usually kept at the University of Manchester's John Rylands Library. BBC News reported the gathering on November 16, 2026, at St Mary and St Mina Coptic Orthodox Church in Stockport. People came from across the United Kingdom. The books are in Coptic, a later form of the Egyptian language, and some date to the 10th century. On the tables were biblical texts, liturgies, a Holy Week lectionary, two parchment manuscripts from Egypt's White Monastery with sermons by Shenoute of Atripe, fragments of a Coptic Gospel of Matthew on papyrus, and an 18th-century Ethiopian manuscript on the life of St George.

Jeremy Penner, a senior curator at the Rylands, said there was something remarkable in a book sitting again with people who know its prayers. The day was organized with Ibrahim Saweros and is part of Coptic in Manchester: Connecting Manuscripts and Communities, a two-year project led by Sarah Parkhouse. She said the point is to ask the communities whose histories the books carry, because they know things the library does not. The project hopes this will be the first of more such visits. The news is this one opening of the collection. It is not a change in who owns the manuscripts.
