The Guardian in Dar es Salaam reported on November 18, 2026, that a scholarship shared by the Karimjee Jivanjee Foundation and the University of Glasgow has trained 25 Tanzanians, and that all of them are now working in the country's conservation sector. The program marked its 10th anniversary in the city on Saturday. Since 2016 it has funded a master's in Conservation Management of African Ecosystems: one year of classes in Glasgow and a second year of field research in Tanzania.

Beyond those 25, the paper said 11 have gone on to PhD study. Together they have 16 scientific papers and more than 20 conference presentations. The Guardian gave three examples without claiming they are the whole record: research by a scholar named Majaliwa on wildebeest is informing how tourism is managed in protected areas; Merikinoi Kimirei's work on the Mara River is guiding decisions about where traffic should cross; and graduate Vicky Mkessa leads the Grumeti Fund's Women in the Field program. Foundation chairman Yusuf Karimjee said Tanzania had no steady pipeline of conservation scientists trained to this level ten years ago. The news is those graduates and where they are working. It is not a measure of wildlife numbers.
