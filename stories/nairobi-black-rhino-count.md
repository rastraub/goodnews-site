Kenya Wildlife Service counted 1,207 black rhinos as of September 2026, past the target of 1,200 in its 2022–2026 recovery plan. People Daily reported the figure on November 13, 2026, from a validation workshop in Nairobi for the next plan. The service said the population has risen from 370 in 1989. It also said it recorded no poaching in 2025 or in the first nine months of 2026, and it credited rangers, intelligence teams, partners, and communities. WWF-Kenya supported the Friday workshop, where partners looked at a draft of the eighth Black Rhino Recovery and Action Plan, for 2027–2031.

The draft sets a goal of at least 1,500 black rhinos by 2031, and it sits under a longer national aim of at least 2,000 by 2037. That next target is not finished. Recommendations from the workshop still have to go through technical review and ministry endorsement, and a launch is planned for World Wildlife Day. The service also said growth has created a space problem. More than 80 percent of the rhinos are in fenced populations at or above what the habitat can hold. The draft proposes two more sites that could hold viable populations, plus research and landscape links. It aims for yearly growth of at least 5 percent and for poaching and other preventable deaths to stay under 0.5 percent a year. The achieved number is 1,207. The 1,500 figure is a proposal, not a count.
