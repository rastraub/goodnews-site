Tiergarten Schönbrunn has raised two young of a gecko that does not yet have a scientific name, the zoo said on November 19, 2026. The hatchlings, from the middle of June and the end of July, made it through the fragile first weeks and are developing well. Zoology director Anton Weissenbacher said the parents arrived in 2023 after a confiscation at Vienna Airport and came from Namibia. Their coloring did not match the sand geckos, Chondrodactylus angulifer, that the zoo already keeps from a separate confiscation.

Until more study is done, the zoo is calling the animals Chondrodactylus sp. The "sp." marks a species inside that genus that has not been formally described. Little is published about how they live. The breeding adds a practical piece: keepers now know they can reproduce in care, and what the young need at the start. Weissenbacher said that knowledge is a base for keeping the kind going. It is zoo husbandry, not a restored wild population.

Visitors can look for them in the conservation house. The reptiles usually spend the day in a burrow they dig themselves. Short, stubby toes are the trait that lets them work the sand, and the same toes set them and the sand geckos apart from other geckos. A daytime visit may show only the enclosure until an animal comes up.
