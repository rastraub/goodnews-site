Mongabay reported on November 17, 2026, that West Nusa Tenggara has designated 73,165 hectares, about 180,795 acres, of Saleh Bay on Sumbawa for whale sharks. The designation, made by the governor in May, extends an existing marine protected area to more than half of the bay's 145,900 hectares. It covers feeding places, areas used by young sharks, and the corridors between them. Indonesia's Ministry of Marine Affairs and Fisheries is reviewing the proposal. Konservasi Indonesia hopes for formal national recognition in 2027. That recognition has not happened yet.

A population assessment published in July identified 174 individuals from 884 sightings, using each shark's spot pattern. Since August 2024, fishers have reported five sharks between 1.2 and 1.5 metres long, and a later video of a similar animal in a net confirmed they were whale sharks. Mochamad Iqbal Herwata Putra of Konservasi Indonesia told Mongabay the nursery evidence is still preliminary: births in the bay have not been confirmed, and repeated use by newborns has not been shown. Fishers who once saw the sharks as rivals for anchovies and sardines are being trained as guides. Hamzah, a fisher and guide from Labuan Jambu, and his son Adam told the group, through a translated interview, that tourism has added work in the village. The news is the enlarged provincial designation and those early records of small sharks. It is not a confirmed pupping ground, and it is not yet a nationally gazetted park.
