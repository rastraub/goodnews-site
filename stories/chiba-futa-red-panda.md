The Japan Times reported on November 17, 2026, that Chiba Zoological Park marked a Guinness World Records certificate for Futa, a red panda recognised as the oldest of his species living in captivity. He turned 23 years and 135 days old that day. The paper said more than 100 people came, and that the ceremony itself drew about 180, including the media. A Guinness representative presented the certificate. Futa was not there. Keepers kept him off the stage because of the cold, and current keeper Yasushi Ito accepted the award.

Ito said he was carrying on the work of every handler Futa has had, and that he hoped the panda would stay healthy. Former keeper Masahira Hamada, who cared for him from 2007 until 2019, said there had been no health worries in those years and that Futa's popularity still amazed him. Fans in matching ears told the paper the panda had drawn visitors to a zoo that once struggled for a crowd. Kaoru Ishikawa, vice president of Guinness World Records Japan, said he was struck that the staff put Futa's condition ahead of the photograph. The record is this animal's age on this day. It does not say how red pandas are faring in the forests where the species lives.
