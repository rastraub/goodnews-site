The Lheidli T'enneh have a salmon feast behind them and more hatchery money in front of them, and the two are not the same kind of news. CBC News reported that the nation, working with the federal government on a hatchery near the meeting of the Fraser and Nechako rivers in Prince George, has another $10.7 million to raise Fraser sockeye on top of a project announced in August. That earlier sum, $38 million, was for raising 400,000 juvenile chinook a year. Chief Dolleen Logan, in a video released with the new funding, called it work for the youth.

The nation said the timing follows fifteen years of returns so low that people could not fish. In 2025 the runs were strong enough to fish again, and in 2026 the community held its first salmon feast. The hatchery is federally owned. Its opening is expected in 2028. The first sockeye eggs are expected in late summer or early fall of that year. If fish go out in the fall of 2028, a four-year life cycle would bring the first adults back around 2032. Fisheries Minister Joanne Thompson said the sockeye capacity is meant to help rebuild vulnerable Fraser populations with the nation. The feast is something that has happened. The sockeye eggs have not.
