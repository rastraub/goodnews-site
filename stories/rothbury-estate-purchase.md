The Wildlife Trusts say they have raised £30 million to buy the Rothbury estate in Northumberland. The Guardian reported on October 30, 2026, that about 25,000 people contributed £17 million of that sum. The land covers more than 3,800 hectares, an area the paper compared with the size of Athens, and campaigners called it the largest piece of land in single ownership to come up for sale in England in more than 30 years.

The appeal ran for about two years after Max Percy, the Duke of Northumberland's youngest son, put the estate on the market. Sir David Attenborough, who had backed the campaign, thanked donors on Friday and said the vision of a landscape where nature and people thrive side by side can become a reality. Mike Pratt, chief executive of Northumberland Wildlife Trust, said he felt elated after a long haul and humbled by the public support.

The Guardian described hills, moor, valley woods, the River Coquet, and farm fields on the estate. The money is for the purchase and the recovery work the trusts want to do there. It is not a count of animals already returned.
