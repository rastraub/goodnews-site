BepiColombo is in orbit around Mercury after an eight-year flight of nearly 10 billion kilometres, BBC News reported on November 22, 2026. The mission is a joint project of the European Space Agency and the Japan Aerospace Exploration Agency. It launched from French Guiana in 2018. To keep from falling into the Sun, the craft spent the whole trip braking, looping once around Earth, twice around Venus, and six times past Mercury. A propulsion fault stretched a seven-year plan by about a year.

The last step was a 37-minute maneuver meant to let Mercury's gravity take hold. ESA said telemetry showed the probe was flying around the planet, though a software issue made the thrusters fire for less time than planned. Only two spacecraft had visited before: Mariner 10, which flew past three times in the 1970s, and NASA's Messenger, which orbited from 2011 for four years.

Mercury's surface runs from about 430 degrees Celsius to about minus 180. A year there lasts 88 Earth days, and a day lasts 59 Earth days. Emma Bunce of the University of Leicester, a principal investigator, said the planet is under-loved and that the mission is meant to learn how it formed, why ice sits in polar craters, what the young hollows on the surface are, and why so much of so small a world is a metal core. Those are the questions. The news is that the spacecraft is there to start asking them.
