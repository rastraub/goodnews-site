The Food and Drug Administration on Nov. 13 approved durvalumab, sold as Imfinzi, given with enfortumab vedotin before the bladder is removed, followed by durvalumab afterward. The approval is for muscle-invasive bladder cancer in adults who cannot take cisplatin or who decline it. In the 695-patient VOLGA trial, the combination cut the risk of an event by about half, an event-free survival hazard ratio of 0.51, and improved overall survival, a hazard ratio of 0.58, compared with surgery alone. The agency approved the use six weeks early. Those ratios describe this trial, not every person with bladder cancer. The trial results were reported earlier from UCLA. This step is the approval that makes the regimen an FDA-cleared option. It is not a cure.
