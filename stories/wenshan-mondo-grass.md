Botanists have named a mondo grass from southeastern Yunnan that hides in plain sight among plants people already know, Phys.org reported on November 19, 2026. Ophiopogon longiscapus grows in the Yunnan Wenshan National Nature Reserve. Researchers from the Xishuangbanna Tropical Botanical Garden and Southwest Forestry University collected it on floristic surveys in 2025 and 2026, then checked it against herbarium sheets and the literature. The description is in Phytotaxa.

The name refers to a flowering stalk, the scape, that is longer than the grasslike leaves. The plant looks similar to Ophiopogon longifolius, but its roots are slender and wiry, without the swollen tubers of that relative, and the rhizome is thick, branched, and densely noded. The flower cluster takes up only about a quarter to a third of the scape. The flowers are large for the group: cream-white on the inside, pinkish-purple outside, opening flat, with yellowish-green anthers.

It is known only from evergreen broadleaf forest in that reserve, between about 1,500 and 2,700 metres. Tan Yunhong of the botanical garden said every known population is inside protected land, and that the population size and trend are still unclear. The authors recommend a Data Deficient rank under IUCN rules, which means the plant is new to science and not yet scored as safe or threatened. The news is the name and the place. It is not a survey of how many clumps are in the forest.
