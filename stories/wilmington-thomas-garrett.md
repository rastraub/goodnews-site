Delaware Tech's George Campus in Wilmington unveiled three markers for abolitionist Thomas Garrett, the college reported on November 18, 2026. The November 6 ceremony was held with the Quaker Hill Historic Preservation Foundation. A bronze marker identifies 227 Shipley Street, where Garrett's house stood and where the campus Southeast Building stands now. A wayside panel went up at Fourth and Shipley, and a stretch of Shipley Street received the honorary name Garrett Way. Mayor John Carney read a proclamation for the street name. Bob Seeley, a Garrett descendant, and Bayard Marin, the foundation's president, were there for the unveiling.

Garrett and his wife, Rachel Mendenhall Garrett, were stationmasters on the Underground Railroad. From that Quaker Hill house they gave shelter, food, clothing, and other help to people escaping slavery, including people assisted by Harriet Tubman. The college said they helped more than 2,700 people reach freedom. The new signs mark that site. They do not add a new count of the people he aided.
