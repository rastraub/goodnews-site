SALZBURG24 reported on November 12, 2026, that an African penguin chick hatched at Salzburg Zoo on August 31. The female, still unnamed and still half in down, is the first chick of Ona and Lesedi. Ona was the first African penguin hatched at the zoo, in May 2021, and Lesedi was the second, in July 2021, so the chick is a next generation born on site. Visitors can sometimes see her. The zoo is looking for sponsors.

The zoo said the species has been listed as critically endangered since autumn 2024, the only penguin native to Africa. It cited about 140,000 breeding pairs on southern coasts in the 1950s, about 70,000 in the 1970s, and fewer than 10,000 now, a drop of 97 percent over 100 years. SANCCOB, which the zoo supports with money, says the wild population is falling about 8 percent a year and could be gone from the wild by 2035 if that continues. The zoo attributes the slide to warmer water moving sardines and anchovies out of reach, fishing pressure, and heat waves and storms. Director Sabine Grebner said the zoo's breeding birds are a reserve population. Salzburg has kept the species since July 2020, and in early summer 2025 it sent nine penguins to a zoo in France. The 2035 date is SANCCOB's warning, not a count. The chick is one bird in human care.
