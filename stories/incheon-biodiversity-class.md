The Herald Business reported on November 16, 2026, that the National Institute of Biological Resources is running its 15th overseas biodiversity course from Monday through Friday. Nine civil servants and researchers are taking part, from Cambodia, Laos, Myanmar, and the Philippines. The institute, under the Ministry of Climate, Environment and Energy, has offered the training since 2009. So far 181 people from 13 countries have completed it.

This session is meant to turn that habit into joint research with ASEAN partners. The group will build species lists at the institute in Incheon, at Seoul Botanic Park, and at the Hantangang UNESCO Global Geopark in Pocheon. They will see specimen stores, the wildlife resource bank, and genomic work at the Macrogen Global Genome Center, then talk through what each country actually needs to study next. Director Yoo Ho said sharing that skill is part of protecting biodiversity across borders. The news is this week's class of nine. It is not a new protected area.
