Almost £6 million from the National Lottery Heritage Fund will back a five-year effort to restore Scotland's Atlantic rainforest, with hubs in Torridon, Morvern, and mid-Argyll. The BBC reported the award on November 11, 2026. Our Rainforest Futures is an Alliance for Scotland's Rainforest project led by Woodland Trust Scotland, with Argyll Countryside Trust, the Community Woodlands Association, Plantlife, RSPB Scotland, and SRUC. The plan is to train local people in habitat restoration, deer management, and removal of invasive plants.

About 75,000 acres of rain-soaked west-coast woodland carry the rainforest label because of the oceanic plants that grow there, including rare lichens, and the woods also hold glow-worms and slime moulds. Overgrazing, invasive plants, and disease are the threats the report names. The Torridon hub would work with Kinlochewe, Shieldaig, Lochcarron, Applecross, and Gairloch. Morvern would include Lochaline, Strontian, and Tobermory on Mull. Mid-Argyll would include Crinan, Tayvallich, Lochgilphead, Ardrishaig, and Tarbert. Caroline Clark of the heritage fund said a 2025 survey found fewer than one in three people knew the rainforest existed. Julie Stoneman of Plantlife said the hope is more than 20 jobs and 15 trainee posts. The news is the grant and the three hubs. The jobs are not filled yet, and the woods are not restored.
