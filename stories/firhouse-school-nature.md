The Dundalk Democrat reported on November 15, 2026, that education groups signed Our Dúlra Agreement at Firhouse Educate Together Secondary School. The agreement asks school leaders to help schools turn grounds into places for wildlife, instead of leaving that work to one or two teachers. Pupils from St Thomas Senior National School in Jobstown and from Firhouse Educate Together were at the launch. Newer signatories include the Edmund Rice Schools Trust, Educate Together, several education and training boards, Le Chéile Schools Trust, and the Irish Waldorf Education Association. They join earlier names, among them 18 education support centres, the Irish Forest Schools Association, and the National Association of Principals and Deputy Principals.

The agreement grew out of the Irish Schools Sustainability Network, whose teachers volunteer. A biodiversity working group, including Green-Schools and the National Biodiversity Data Centre, is carrying it. The paper said the text was shaped in June 2026 at Wildacres, a 17.5-acre reserve on the Redcross River in County Wicklow. Patrick Kirwan, of the teacher network, said schools follow the people who lead them. Boards can sign by downloading the agreement from the network's site. The news is those signatures. It is not a count of meadows already planted.
