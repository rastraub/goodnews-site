RNZ reported on November 19, 2026, that the Onetahua Restoration Project at Farewell Spit has its first takahē chick. The Department of Conservation moved eight of the threatened birds there in May, into a 2,000-hectare habitat ringed by a 4-kilometre predator-resistant fence. Golden Bay operations manager Ross Trotter told Checkpoint the parents were not an established pair when they arrived. They were introduced at Triangle Flat in Pūponga, and the chick hatched in October. It is about six weeks old.

Trotter said that age matters because the birds are vulnerable early on. Other pairs tried to nest. One nest was washed away, and another pair had an infertile egg. The parents are still keeping this chick hidden. From about eight to twelve weeks it will spend more time in the open, where hawks and falcons can reach it. DOC asked visitors to stay on the tracks. The chick does not have a name yet. Trotter said the team will work with Manawhenua Ki Mohua on a name once survival looks likely. Chair Margie Little said the hatching, five months after the move from Gouland Downs, brings the species back to Onetahua. Trapping inside the fence is still going on. The news is this one chick, not a national census of takahē.
