Volunteers at Shelby County High School turned lumber into 50 bed frames on Saturday for children who did not have a bed of their own, Spectrum News 1 reported. The day was part of Metro United Way's Build-A-Bed program, with Shelby County Public Schools and A Place to Sleep, a ministry of First Presbyterian Church in Shelbyville.

Each child receives a full set: a wooden frame, a twin mattress, sheets, a blanket, a pillow, and a laundry bag. The schools helped identify families. Adria Johnson, president and chief executive of Metro United Way, said buying a bed can be the barrier, and that the change for a child is hard to put a number on. The program began in 2017 and has provided more than 1,000 complete sets in the greater Louisville region. A Place to Sleep, founded by Jessica Adkins in 2009, has helped more than 4,000 children across Kentucky. She said the first family they found lived about a two-minute walk from her house.
