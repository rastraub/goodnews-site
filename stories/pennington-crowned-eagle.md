The South Coast Herald reported on November 12, 2026, that a juvenile African crowned eagle found badly hurt in Pennington was released last week on the KwaZulu-Natal south coast. In the last week of October someone reported him to the Centre for Rehabilitation of Wildlife. CROW suspects he was injured while trying to catch one of a troop of mongooses. The paper says the exact circumstances are unknown.

Before release he was fitted with identification rings, including a larger ring meant to be seen, and his wings and tail were measured. Fluffy down under the tail is CROW's sign that he is still young. The ring feeds research by Dr Shane McPherson with the University of Vienna. That work has found crowned eagles nesting about every 2.5 kilometres along river systems in eThekwini, relying on remaining indigenous forest, with domestic pets making up less than 1 percent of the diet. The species is listed under South Africa's Threatened or Protected Species regulations. One rehabilitated juvenile is flying. The nest spacing is from the wider study, not from this release.
