Student volunteers opened a library in a room that had stayed locked at a government school in Mullur Colony, Citizen Matters reported on November 16, 2026. The school, in Bengaluru's Mahadevapura area, teaches in Kannada and English and had two teachers. When Shape the Future started working there in 2024 it had 24 students. It now has 47. The room has more than 2,000 books, a puzzle corner, art supplies, and a reading rug. Toddlers from the anganwadi next door use the picture books. Teachers borrow books. So do Akshaya Patra drivers who deliver midday meals, and women from the neighborhood join storytelling sessions.

Asavari Agrawal, a grade 12 student, wrote that she started the group in 2022, at 13, with weekend visits to a home for orphaned children. More than 100 volunteers, mostly 15 to 17, now take part on weekends and holidays. They collected the books from their own schools and apartment complexes and spent nothing on purchases. A rule was that a book not good enough for them was not good enough for anyone else. Two months went into cleaning, sorting by age, and repacking. The Kodathi gram panchayat introduced them to the school. FirstSteps Babywear and Mini Klub paid for repairs. The Dash Square gave a table and a rug. PayPal and DonateKart later sent about 100 volunteers to paint the walls. Because the student volunteers are in class on weekdays, adults take turns reading aloud. Books are in English, Kannada, Hindi, and Tamil, and a missing book is replaced by the next donation. Extra books have gone to Thayi Mane and the Government Higher Primary School at Mulluru. A lending arrangement with nearby schools is still a pilot, waiting on permission. Agrawal wrote that academic results are too early to measure. The news is this library and this school. It is not a new public-library system for the city.
