Two male kākāpō have joined the three already living at Sanctuary Mountain Maungatautari, bringing the mainland trial to five birds. RNZ reported the arrivals on October 22, 2026.

The Waikato ecosanctuary sits inside a predator-proof fence about 47 kilometres long, on more than 3,363 hectares that hold hundreds of native species. The kākāpō have been there since 2023 as a test of how the birds use fenced forest on the mainland, rather than only on offshore islands. Annie Wallace, acting operations manager for the Department of Conservation, said the two extra males help show how kākāpō use that habitat. The national population is 325 after a strong breeding season. The trial is a partnership among the Department of Conservation's Kākāpō Recovery programme, Te Rūnanga o Ngāi Tahu, and mana whenua.

On the same sanctuary, 122 Mahoenui giant wētā were released on Thursday. They were bred at Ōtorohanga Kiwi House with Ngāti Maniapoto. The release comes a little over a year after 192 of the insects were moved there last August.
