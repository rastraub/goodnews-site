CBC News reported on November 14, 2026, that an 8-metre bridge now spans an amphibian trail on the northwest shore of Shane Lake in Forests for the World, a Prince George park. The City of Prince George, Nahanni Construction, and other partners finished the work on November 12. Walkers can cross above long-toed salamanders, western toads, wood frogs, and Columbia spotted frogs that leave the lake for the forest in July and August.

Forester Ashley Sutherland first reported the trampling after a dog walk in early August 2022, then got warning signs installed. She said the animals move in the thousands, and that her 2025 data put mortality on the main loop as high as 12.8 percent, not all of it from feet. She also said only about 1 in 100 reach maturity, so the thousands on the trail are not proof the populations are secure. Dean Price of Nahanni Construction said the city paid about 30 percent and donations covered the rest. Parks staff Michael Le Morvan said a second bridge is planned about 100 metres away. This span is already in use.
