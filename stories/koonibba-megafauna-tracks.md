Students from Koonibba Aboriginal School have been reading giant animals out of the ground, not only out of books. ABC News reported on October 31, 2026, that 21 students traveled from their remote classroom to rock art in the Flinders Ranges and then to fossil tracks near Fowlers Bay, on South Australia's west coast.

Reece, 10, found a round print in the rocky beach crust that had not been documented. Palaeontologist Aaron Camens of Flinders University said it was most likely a Procoptodon, a short-faced kangaroo. The largest of those animals stood about 2.7 metres tall and weighed about 240 kilograms. They died out at least 15,000 years ago. Camens said the coastal prints were probably left in the last warm period between ice ages, about 120,000 to 130,000 years ago, when the sea sat near where it is now. Storms bury some tracks and uncover others.

Adnyamathanha guide Kristian Coulthard took the students to a petroglyph site, Yura Mulka, and explained carvings of ancestors with megafauna. Principal Mark Prince said the trips sit beside classroom work and a student talk at the 2026 Aboriginal STEM gathering in Adelaide in August. The class used quadrants to map tracks, including measurements that can hint at how fast an animal was moving. The story ABC published is about that learning. It is not a claim that the animals still live on the coast.
