Port of Dover employees have been using a paid volunteer day to take on jobs for schools, a church, and a farm. BBC News reported the latest round on November 16, 2026. The scheme, started in 2022, gives each worker one working day a year. So far this year the hours come to nearly 600. Since 2022 the total is more than 3,000 hours, spread across homeless charities, scout groups, and wildlife projects as well as schools. The port's community fund has raised £128,000 for local causes. That money is separate from the hours.

The jobs in this round include an eco-toilet and a rebuilt sensory path at Shepherdswell Primary School, a pergola at Frampton Farm, which works with young people including those with special educational needs, and more than 1,000 spring bulbs in the grounds of St Mary's Church in Dover. At Lydden Primary School the crew built an outdoor shelter. At Woodpecker Court in Eythorne, a college for ages 16 to 24, they put up a polytunnel in the horticulture area. A college spokesperson said students can now grow fruit and vegetables on a much larger scale. The news is this set of finished jobs and the hours logged. It is not a promise about next year's roster.
