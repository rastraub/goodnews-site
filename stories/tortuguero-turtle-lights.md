Hatchlings on Costa Rica's Caribbean coast find the sea by crawling toward the brightest horizon. The Tico Times reported on October 25, 2026, that porch lights, hotel signs, and street lamps can outshine the water and pull them inland, onto roads or private land.

At Tortuguero, a Duke University analysis of light levels found fewer green turtles coming ashore on the brightest stretches near the village. In March 2023 the Costa Rican Electricity Institute installed 71 red lamps there. A later study of green turtle hatchlings, the paper said, found that 15 percent crawled toward artificial light under white lamps, compared with 5 percent under red light.

The newspaper's account is a comparison of two kinds of light, plus a record of lamps already in the ground. It does not say the village has gone dark, or that every hatchling now reaches the surf. Red light reduced the wrong-way crawls in that study. White light, and a bright waterfront, are still the problem the researchers describe.
