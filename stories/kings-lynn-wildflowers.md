Hundreds of native wildflower plugs are in the ground at Fairstead Recreation Ground in King's Lynn. BBC reported on October 26, 2026, that members of the Nature Volunteer Network and staff from the Borough Council of King's Lynn and West Norfolk did the planting as the next step in turning the recreation ground into a meadow.

The council had already spread green hay on the site earlier in 2026. Michael de Whalley, the cabinet member for climate change and biodiversity, said each plug moves the ground closer to a meadow that can support bees, butterflies, birds, and other species. Biodiversity officer Chris Walker said the borough wants to take what it learns here and start meadows elsewhere.

The flowers are not a finished habitat. BBC said the council will keep watching the site, with the aim of a meadow that can sustain itself and, later, supply seed and plants for other projects. The authority described the work as one of its first practical steps to restore insect habitat across the borough.
