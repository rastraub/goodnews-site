Year 4 students at St Peter's Anglican College in Broulee spent a week putting about 1,000 native plants into a bare piece of playground. ABC News reported on October 25, 2026, that the planting is the start of a microforest meant to add shade and bring back plants from the region's endangered coastal littoral rainforest.

It is the first school in New South Wales to research, design, and plant a microforest with the nonprofit Microforest Collective as part of the curriculum. Teacher Danielle Phillips said the class visited local rainforests, named species, and worked out what each plant needs before anyone picked up a spade. They mapped the site with science, maths, geography, and literacy, including a volume problem for how much mulch the ground would take. Phillips said the outdoor work is also practice in how a group talks to one another when a task gets difficult.

Student Zale Neal told ABC the class was trying to bring back plants and animals lost in the 2020 bushfires, and that planting felt calming because the forest would still be there when they are older. The school expects the microforest to be a model other schools can follow.
