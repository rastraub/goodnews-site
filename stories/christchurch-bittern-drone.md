Discover Wildlife reported on November 16, 2026, that conservation teams in New Zealand are using thermal drones to find Australasian bitterns, birds that match the reeds so well they are rarely seen. Jo Malcolm, of the Department of Conservation, wrote that watching a drone mark one matuku-hūrepo felt like spotting a needle in a haystack. She put the Canterbury population at about 50 birds and the national total at about 1,000. The department's own pages say the bird matters in Māori stories and place names, and that it was common when Europeans arrived and is now usually seen one at a time.

In the footage described by the magazine, the wetland looks like a tangle of plants until the heat view turns the bird into a bright speck. Malcolm tied that skill to Environment Canterbury's Whakaora Te Waihora project, whose aim is to keep the birds from declining. The article does not name the exact pond in the clip. The news is that a heat sensor can find a bird people cannot see. It is not a new census, and it is not a claim that the population has recovered.
