Neighbours around Waitomo met to prepare for kiwi that are not on the farm yet. King Country News reported on November 12, 2026, that Rick and Moira Haddrell spent five years retiring their 470-hectare sheep and beef farm west of the Waitomo Caves, then six years preparing it as kiwi habitat. They received a Department of Conservation translocation permit in July. About 40 landowners and staff gathered last week at the Waitomo Caves Museum and Discovery Centre, at an event hosted by the Queen Elizabeth II National Trust and the Waitomo Catchment Trust.

The first 20 western brown kiwi are expected in April, in two batches of 10, from the Taranaki Kohanga Kiwi Trust at Rotokare, near New Plymouth. A further 40 are planned for 2028 and 2029. Moira Haddrell said the exciting call will be someone in town asking the farm to come collect a kiwi that walked off the property. Kiwi can travel more than 10 kilometres while foraging. Wildlife biologist Cam Speedy and Darren Peters of NZ AutoTraps showed predator-control methods. Speedy said ship rats take birds in the canopy and that traps alone sometimes cannot keep up. Community dogs had kiwi aversion training earlier this year, with another round planned. The news is the permit, the meeting, and the timetable. No kiwi have been released on the farm.
