Drivers with colour vision deficiency can work trains on the Northern line, the BBC reported on November 20, 2026. London Underground long relied on red and green lights, so most of the network was closed to people who cannot reliably tell those colours apart. The Jubilee line was the exception from 2018, after its signals became a digital message in the cab. Osman Hassan, who has worked for Transport for London for eight years, including in customer service, is one of two drivers with the condition now on the Northern line. He had already driven on the Jubilee line. He told the BBC he loves getting people across London and hopes other employers look at what they can change.

Most Northern line signals were replaced in 2014, but two red-and-green cues remained. Drivers leaving the Morden depot still depended on coloured flags, and the evacuation ladders in the cab were colour-coded. Strategy and change manager Joe Brown said those flags are now a plain white light, and the ladders carry numbers as well as colours. He traced the idea to 2011, when a Jubilee line driver asked whether a younger brother with colour vision deficiency could be assessed for the cab once the coloured lights were gone. The Hammersmith and City line and the Circle line are also fully upgraded for this. Transport for London hopes to do the same on every line. About 3 million people in the UK have colour vision deficiency, roughly 1 in 12 men and 1 in 200 women. The news is these lines and these two Northern line drivers. It is not a change on the whole Underground yet.
