A critically endangered northern white-cheeked gibbon was born at Adelaide Zoo on Tuesday afternoon, the first of the species born there in a decade. InDaily reported the birth on November 12, 2026. Primates team supervisor James Bibby said it is the only northern white-cheeked gibbon born in Australasia this year, and that the association managing the species had recommended the pair be allowed to breed again.

The baby returned that morning to the island habitat with mother Viet, 27, and father Remus, 30. Bibby said this is Viet's fifth baby at the zoo and that she is an experienced, attentive mother. Keepers do not yet know the infant's sex. The family can go back into night quarters, and they will spend nights inside while the baby is still so young. These gibbons are born golden and later turn black. A female turns gold again around five years of age, while a male stays black, with white cheeks and a black crest. Wild northern white-cheeked gibbons live in the rainforests of Laos, Vietnam, and southern China. The news is this one birth in a managed pair. It is not a count of gibbons in the forest.
