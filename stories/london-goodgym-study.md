People who run or ride to help a neighbor, and keep doing it, reported better mental health than a comparison group. Positive News reported on October 23, 2026, that researchers at King's College London studied 2,800 volunteers with GoodGym, a charity whose members combine exercise with practical help for isolated older people and community jobs.

Regular participation was linked to a significant drop in poor mental health against the control group. Volunteers also described more trust, connection, and belonging. Maria Cotofan, a senior lecturer in economics and policy at King's, told Positive News that sport and volunteering can ease pressure on health services because they cost less than many clinical routes. Some doctors in Britain already prescribe time outdoors.

The roundup does not name the journal, the size of the change, or how long people were followed. It does not say the runs replace treatment. What it reports is a human comparison, already completed, between people doing this volunteering and people who were not, and a difference in how the volunteers said they felt.
