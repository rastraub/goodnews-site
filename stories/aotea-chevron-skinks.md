Eleven niho taniwha, the chevron skinks that are New Zealand's longest lizards, were released on November 23, 2026, at Glenfern Sanctuary on Aotea, also called Great Barrier Island. The Department of Conservation said Ngāti Rehua–Ngātiwai ki Aotea Trust, Auckland Zoo, and the sanctuary carried out a return that iwi had wanted for years. The species is listed as threatened and nationally vulnerable. It grows up to 30 centimetres from nose to tail and is now found only on Aotea and Te Hauturu-o-Toi, Little Barrier Island.

Three injured skinks left Aotea for care at Auckland Zoo between 1997 and 1999. They bred there. The group grew to two of the original adults and 19 zoo-bred animals. The eleven released on Monday join ten put into the predator-controlled sanctuary in July. At the zoo they spent weeks getting used to bamboo tubes. Small transmitters were taped to their tails, and they were set down inside those tubes so they would have a place to come back to.

Auckland Zoo's Richard Gibson said a Unitec master's student, Sophie Whittall, is using the radios to learn whether the tubes help and how the July group, the November group, and wild skinks behave after a move. Kaumātua Rodney Ngāwaka called the skink a jewel of Aotea and said the return reconnects the animals with their whakapapa. DOC's Fletcher Beazley said a pōwhiri at Matariki welcomed the species, and that some people helped place the bamboo shelters. The report is this release and the tracking that follows. It does not say the sanctuary population is secure on its own yet.
