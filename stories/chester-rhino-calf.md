A greater one-horned rhino calf was born at Chester Zoo this week after a 15-month pregnancy, the BBC reported on November 19, 2026. Video shows 19-year-old Asha with the newborn. Tom Garland, of the zoo's rhino team, said the calf is Asha's fourth, and that the father, Beni, died earlier this year after a short and sudden illness. Garland said Asha has been a good mother to her youngsters and that every birth matters for the species.

Keepers said mother and calf are in good health. Conservation scientist John O'Hanlan said relatively few greater one-horned rhinos in Europe are breeding, so a successful birth helps keep that population varied. About 4,000 of the animals live in wetlands, forests, and grasslands in India and Nepal. Mark Brayshaw, head of mammals, said they are better protected in the wild than they once were, but many are concentrated in one national park in India, which leaves them exposed to illness and environmental change. The news is this one calf in a European breeding programme. It is not a new census of the wild population.
