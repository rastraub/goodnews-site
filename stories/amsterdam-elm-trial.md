NOS reported on November 13, 2026, that Amsterdam has lifted 180 identical elm trees to see which soil grows a wide crown and real shade. Eight years ago the trees went into 15 soil mixes in the harbour district, with researchers from Wageningen University. Trunks and crowns were measured through those years. Root scans are being added this week. City tree expert Hans Kaljee called the dig about the most beautiful day of his life. He said the city once wanted small street trees that would not grow fast, and now wants broad crowns because summer heat is worse.

One mix used for about 25 years produced a smaller root ball with fewer fine roots, the ones that take up water and food. Kaljee said the city will stop using granulate that contains clay, and that a finer mix looks better for roots. Some roots escaped the screened squares. Adviser Gerrit-Jan van Prooijen compared them to Houdini. The full report is due in the spring, so the best mix is not named yet. Most of the 180 elms will be replanted, many of them in parks.
