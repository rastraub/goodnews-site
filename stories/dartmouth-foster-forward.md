CBC News reported on November 15, 2026, that the Nova Scotia SPCA is launching Foster Forward, a program to move healthy animals into temporary homes so kennels stay open for pets that need one-on-one care. Spokesperson Sarah Lyon said the animals in mind are healthy pets, new mothers, kittens, puppies, rabbits, and guinea pigs. The SPCA is one of 10 organisations in Canada to receive a 2026 Pedigree Foundation grant. The money will pay for tracking software, automated updates, and virtual veterinary care. Lyon said the society receives no government funding for its programs.

Once the program is running, volunteers will be trained in skills such as bottle-feeding and will have medical support at any hour, plus an online forum. Lyon said the SPCA placed more than 5,000 pets into homes across the province last year, and that people can register online to foster. She also asked owners who are struggling to call before a crisis, including for food and litter from the pantry. A dog named Daisy was pictured at the Dartmouth SPCA as available for adoption, which is a separate step from fostering. The news is the grant and the program being built. It is not a count of animals already moved into new foster homes.
